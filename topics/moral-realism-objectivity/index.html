Eric Sampson vs Lance Bush: Is Moral Realism True? · 2022

Sampson narrowly prevails by defining a limited realism and defending its intelligibility, while Bush supplies powerful underdetermination and genealogy challenges that remain evidentially incomplete.

Eric Sampson: 82 · Lance Bush: 81. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

Is Moral Realism Better Supported? Michael Huemer vs. Don Loeb · 2022

Huemer’s intuitive and convergence-based realism met Loeb’s semantic, explanatory, and practical alternatives; mixed evidence narrowly left anti-realism at least equally plausible.

Michael Huemer: 80 · Don Loeb: 82. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

David Enoch & Eric Sampson vs Don Loeb & Matthew Lutz: Is Moral Realism True? · 2024

Enoch and Sampson defend robust moral realism through Moorean cases and rational reflection; Loeb and Lutz argue that desire-relative reasons and a naturalistic explanation of moral experience make such facts unnecessary.

David Enoch & Eric Sampson: 92 · Don Loeb & Matthew Lutz: 91. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

David Enoch vs Justin Clarke-Doane: Moral Realism and Objectivity · 2026

Enoch grounds moral objectivity in stance-independent facts and ordinary deliberation, while confronting rival-package, noncognitivist, and reflective-anomaly challenges to action guidance.

David Enoch: 84 · Justin Clarke-Doane: 88. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

Lance Bush vs Derek McAllister: Moral Anti-Realism vs Realism · 2025

Bush systematically challenges realist presumptions from common sense, history, and semantics, while McAllister offers competent but insufficiently documented convergence and perspectival-collapse arguments.

Lance Bush: 90 · Derek McAllister: 76. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

Mike Huemer vs Matt Lutz: Is Morality Real? · 2026

Huemer defends objective morality through evaluative judgment and defeasible appearances; Lutz counters with desire-based reasons, projectivism, and explanationist standards for knowledge.

Michael Huemer: 88 · Matt Lutz: 81. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

Peter Singer, Jessica Frazier, Richard Swinburne, and Alex O'Connor: The Mystery of Morality · 2025

Singer, Frazier, and Swinburne offer divergent realist accounts of morality; O'Connor defends ethical emotivism and treats moral language as expressive rather than truth-apt.

Peter Singer, Jessica Frazier, Richard Swinburne: 82 · Alex O'Connor: 80. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

Michael Huemer vs Lance Bush: Do Objective Moral Facts Exist? · 2024

Huemer defended attitude-independent morality through semantic tests and intellectual appearances; Bush countered with contextual quietism, folk indeterminacy, and challenges to intuition’s public authority.

Michael Huemer: 85 · Lance Bush: 83. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

David Enoch vs Lance Bush: Are There Objective Moral Truths? · 2026

Enoch defends mind-independent normativity through justified appearances and comparative explanation; Bush contests realist priority through neutral language, causal genealogy, pragmatism, and parsimony.

David Enoch: 85 · Lance Bush: 84. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

Alex O'Connor vs Craig Biddle: Is Morality Objective? · 2024

Biddle grounds objective morality in life and flourishing; O'Connor argues that conditional valuation cannot establish objective duties, especially toward other people and animals.

Craig Biddle: 82 · Alex O'Connor: 89. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.

Sam Harris vs Alex O'Connor: Objective Morality and Emotivism · 2024

Harris grounds evaluative direction in conscious experience, while O'Connor distinguishes subjective destinations from objective means and denies that flourishing requires moral truth.

Sam Harris: 77 · Alex O'Connor: 88. Scores assess the reasoning in this debate.
