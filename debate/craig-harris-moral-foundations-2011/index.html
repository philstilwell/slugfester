Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Supernatural foundation: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Clearly separated the proposed foundations of value and duty, locating the former in divine nature and the latter in nature-constrained commands.
  • Delivered the exchange's sharpest diagnostic reply by using possible divergence between flourishing and evil character to challenge strict welfare-goodness identity.
  • Consistently distinguished moral ontology from semantics, epistemic access, applied disagreement, and the mere evolutionary genealogy of moral belief.
  • Directly exposed the unresolved naturalistic transition from descriptive welfare facts to binding duties, even though the authority-based solution needed further defense.

Published weaknesses

  • The evolutionary critique moved from biological origins and cosmic contingency to absent objective worth without eliminating stronger naturalistic grounding theories.
  • The duty argument established that description alone is insufficient but did not establish that commanding authority is necessary or uniquely capable of generating obligation.

Sam Harris — Natural foundation: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Established consciousness as a carefully limited necessary condition for value through a clear and memorable consciousness-free-universe thought experiment.
  • Used universal maximal misery to provide an intuitively powerful minimum standard for objective evaluation without initially claiming a complete moral ordering.
  • Directly challenged definitional asymmetry by comparing intrinsic divine goodness with an explicit experiential axiom governing maximal suffering.
  • Made the limits of bare authority concrete through the distinction between teacher-permitted conventions and authorized interpersonal harm.

Published weaknesses

  • The natural-science route showed that welfare facts are empirically investigable but did not fully derive moral authority or binding obligation from those facts.
  • The revelation replies often addressed public access and sectarian reliability while leaving the opponent's separate ontological foundation claims unanswered.
  • The authority analogy omitted the decisive qualification that the proposed divine commands were constrained by an essentially good nature.

The Ontological Ground of Objective Moral Value

7:54–38:28

William Lane Craig — section score 79/100

Objective moral values and duties require a foundation independent of human opinion; theism supplies that foundation, whereas atheism does not, although these are conditional claims rather than an argument that God exists.

Strongest feature: The explicitly conditional formulation directly organizes the supernatural-foundation route, preserves argumentative scope, and identifies objective independence from human opinion as the indispensable explanatory target. Principal limitation: The opening announces theological sufficiency and atheistic insufficiency before providing independent justification for either exclusivity claim, leaving its strongest modal language provisionally unsupported. Live burden: Subsequent reasoning must establish that divine nature nonarbitrarily constitutes value, that divine authority generates obligation, and that plausible naturalistic alternatives cannot perform equivalent grounding work. Locked score: The supplied very-strong band reflects exceptional motion relevance, disciplined qualification, and coherent architecture, while appropriately reserving credit because this programmatic thesis alone cannot substantiate its foundational conditionals.

Sam Harris — section score 87/100

Conscious experience is a necessary condition for value because changes in a universe can matter only when they can at least potentially affect some conscious system.

Strongest feature: The consciousness-free thought experiment lucidly supports a carefully limited necessary-condition claim, distinguishing the possibility of mattering from any stronger assertion that consciousness alone suffices for morality. Principal limitation: Its intuitive force does not fully address theories assigning nonexperiential intrinsic value to life, nature, rational form, or other properties independently of felt consequences. Live burden: The natural route must build from this domain condition toward objective comparisons, moral authority, and duties without silently converting consciousness's necessity into the sufficiency of aggregate well-being. Locked score: The supplied very-strong band reflects exceptional clarity, calibration, coherence, and central route contact, while appropriately preserving a modest evidential reservation because alternative value theories receive little direct comparison.

Well-Being, Moral Objectivity, and Scientific Inquiry

18:59–55:19

William Lane Craig — section score 83/100

Defining moral goodness through conscious-creature well-being risks making the goodness of maximizing well-being tautological, while leaving unexplained why well-being possesses objective moral significance rather than merely prudential importance.

Strongest feature: The objection isolates an important metaethical distinction between verbally defining goodness through well-being and substantively explaining why facts about flourishing possess specifically moral authority. Principal limitation: Characterizing the proposal as mere redefinition insufficiently considers a charitable naturalistic identity or constitution thesis, under which conceptual reduction could represent metaphysical analysis rather than tautology. Live burden: The criticism must test stronger interpretations while requiring an independently defended bridge from experiential welfare to moral goodness, especially where flourishing, justice, intention, and character potentially diverge. Locked score: The supplied strong-and-competent band rewards conceptual focus, central relevance, and a durable explanatory demand, while acknowledging slightly overstated rhetoric and incomplete engagement with reductive naturalism.

Sam Harris — section score 79/100

Moral questions depend on conscious experience; minds are natural phenomena governed by natural regularities, so facts influencing conscious well-being are open to disciplined scientific investigation.

Strongest feature: The argument offers a concise naturalistic research program connecting experience-dependent moral questions, naturally instantiated minds, and empirically discoverable conditions that influence suffering and flourishing. Principal limitation: Natural dependence establishes scientific relevance but not that empirical inquiry alone can determine every normative premise, comparison, aggregation rule, or reason for action. Live burden: The route must clarify whether science means empirical method or rational inquiry broadly, then defend the normative bridge connecting measured welfare facts with moral truth and obligation. Locked score: The supplied strong-and-competent band recognizes coherent architecture, substantial central relevance, and a plausible investigative role, while reflecting partly unstable methodological scope and an inference that exceeds the premises' demonstrated reach.

From Facts to Objective Duties and Responsibility

21:37–123:13

William Lane Craig — section score 73/100

Natural science can describe circumstances and consequences without generating objective obligations; binding duties therefore require moral imperatives from competent authority rather than evolutionary advantage, social taboo, or conditioning alone.

Strongest feature: The fact-norm distinction directly confronts the natural route's most difficult transition, correctly observing that empirical descriptions alone do not automatically entail objective obligations or action-guiding requirements. Principal limitation: The further claim that obligation requires commanding authority remains substantially unsupported, and the presentation overlooks naturalist accounts based on reasons, agency, relationships, or objective welfare. Live burden: The argument must establish why authoritative imperatives are necessary and uniquely divine, while explaining how commands create normative reasons rather than dependence on power, sanctions, or prior value. Locked score: The supplied mixed band reflects central relevance and a legitimate bridge problem, but also broad claims about science and naturalism, incomplete comparative analysis, and an underdefended authority conditional.

Sam Harris — section score 77/100

Preferences, conduct, and cultural practices can be objectively mistaken when they reliably frustrate creaturely well-being, regardless of whether individuals or communities presently endorse those values.

Strongest feature: The move directly rejects simple preference relativism by supplying an intelligible natural criterion under which entrenched personal or cultural endorsements can be objectively mistaken. Principal limitation: Demonstrating reliable welfare frustration establishes evaluative error only if well-being has already been shown to possess moral authority, and it does not independently generate binding obligation. Live burden: The reply must confront the stronger ontological and authority-based challenge, explaining how objective welfare reasons become duties rather than merely comparative recommendations or instrumentally effective guidance. Locked score: The supplied strong-and-competent band reflects clear objective-error contact, practical evidential force, and central relevance, while acknowledging partial responsiveness, an implicit foundational assumption, and incomplete engagement with the opponent's strongest formulation.

Divine Goodness, Command, and Nonarbitrariness

10:58–123:27

William Lane Craig — section score 83/100

God's necessarily holy and loving nature constitutes objective moral value, while divine commands expressing and constrained by that nature constitute humanity's objective duties without arbitrariness.

Strongest feature: The account clearly separates the ontological functions of divine nature and divine command, while expressly constraining commandments through holiness and love rather than unexplained sovereign preference. Principal limitation: It asserts more readily than demonstrates why one necessarily perfect personal nature constitutes objective goodness, or why commands from that nature create obligations for every relevant agent. Live burden: The proposal must explain noncircularly how maximal divine attributes entail recognizable moral goodness and how authority supplies binding reasons without merely redescribing obedience as obligation. Locked score: The supplied strong-and-competent band recognizes unusually clear structure, central route contact, and an explicit nonarbitrariness condition, while retaining the consequential explanatory gaps concerning constitution and authority.

Sam Harris — section score 79/100

Calling God intrinsically good does not independently explain goodness, while beginning moral inquiry from universal maximal misery is an explicit axiom comparable to foundational assumptions elsewhere in science.

Strongest feature: The symmetry challenge directly contacts both divine-nature grounding and the circularity objection, asking why conceptual divine goodness receives treatment denied to an experiential misery axiom. Principal limitation: Identifying both foundational moves does not demonstrate that the welfare axiom enjoys superior justification, explanatory power, universality, or freedom from comparable circularity concerns. Live burden: The reply must compare their epistemic accessibility and metaphysical roles while addressing the possibility that maximal greatness entails goodness rather than attaching it through arbitrary stipulation. Locked score: The supplied strong-and-competent band rewards full contact, careful probabilistic qualification, central relevance, and a productive reversal, while recognizing limited comparative defense and the remaining need for noncircular asymmetry.

Moral Ontology, Knowledge, Disagreement, and Revelation

46:37–126:04

William Lane Craig — section score 74/100

Moral language's meaning and ordinary use differ from moral ontology: divine character grounds values and divine commands ground duties, independently of how speakers define or learn moral terms.

Strongest feature: The light analogy cleanly distinguishes a term's ordinary meaning and historical use from the underlying reality that might explain what the term ultimately concerns. Principal limitation: Although conceptually sound, the reply largely restates divine grounding and does not answer Harris's natural-mind inference or his proposed welfare-based criterion for objective error. Live burden: The distinction must engage the charitable possibility that well-being is offered as naturalistic ontology, then compare that proposal directly with divine nature rather than dismissing it as semantics. Locked score: The supplied mixed band reflects high internal coherence, stable terminology, and useful subsidiary clarification, while appropriately accounting for limited responsiveness, asserted theological grounding, and incomplete representation of the strongest naturalist interpretation.

Sam Harris — section score 67/100

Incompatible religions can employ materially similar apologetic reasoning, while birthplace, parents, culture, and inherited theology strongly influence belief, making sectarian revelation an unreliable route to moral knowledge.

Strongest feature: The competing-religion comparison powerfully raises underdetermination and inherited-access concerns, showing why public moral confidence cannot rest merely upon geographically contingent sectarian commitment. Principal limitation: Similar argumentative form and birthplace dependence do not establish equal evidential quality, exact doctrinal symmetry, falsity, or the absence of discriminating historical considerations. Live burden: The challenge must compare rival evidence more carefully and distinguish unreliable access to revelation from the separate ontological possibility that objective values possess a supernatural ground. Locked score: The supplied mixed band recognizes a meaningful subsidiary epistemic objection and substantial intuitive relevance, while reflecting materially overstated equivalence, incomplete evidence, and noncontact with the decisive conceptual and ontological claims.
