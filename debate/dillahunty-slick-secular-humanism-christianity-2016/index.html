Overall assessment

Matt Dillahunty — Secular humanism: 82/100

Published strengths

  • Established flourishing and empirically informed interests as a clear common domain for comparison.
  • Presented contextual revision and disaster exceptions as concrete advantages of a consequence-sensitive moral method.
  • Answered foundational attacks with a strong distinction between using logic reliably and explaining its ultimate origin.
  • Combined fallibility, logical testing, experienced reality, and independent corroboration into a practical epistemic method.
  • Used central Christian doctrinal disagreement to challenge scriptural conflict-resolution reliability.

Published weaknesses

  • The claim that God is merely irrelevant compressed the stronger Christian view that relationship with God constitutes flourishing.
  • The categorical exclusion of every secular instruction to kill or enslave exceeded the support provided by equality and autonomy.
  • The case clarified value-relative objectivity more successfully than it justified why flourishing should possess governing moral authority.

Matt Slick — Christian foundations: 74/100

Published strengths

  • Made non-self-refutation a clear threshold condition for comparative intellectual adequacy.
  • Pressed serious questions about reliable cognition under physical causation and about science’s unproved presuppositions.
  • Identified the distinction between evaluating consequences and independently justifying the value used to evaluate them.
  • Offered New Testament revelation, divine character, and neighbor-love as substantive components of the Christian alternative.

Published weaknesses

  • The cognition objection did not establish that physically caused processes cannot track logical truth, while divine image was asserted as the alternative mechanism.
  • The universal-mind inference omitted acknowledged steps between abstract universality, mentality, and specifically Christian theism.
  • The closing claimed uniquely Christian justification without comparing serious secular alternatives or answering practical doctrinal conflict.

Comparative Standard and the Meaning of Flourishing

9:40–161:05

Matt Dillahunty — section score 79/100

Human flourishing, informed by reality and methods likely to identify human interests accurately, supplies a practical domain for comparing secular humanism with Christianity.

Strongest feature: The move establishes an intelligible comparative domain by connecting flourishing, ascertainable interests, factual investigation, and accuracy-oriented methods directly to the secular route’s central evaluative burden. Principal limitation: Its practical plausibility does not demonstrate that both worldviews genuinely share this standard, nor that empirically accessible interests exhaust every consideration relevant to a comprehensive comparison. Live burden: The case still must justify privileging flourishing, specify its components and distribution, and show that the proposed method discriminates reliably between competing interpretations of human welfare. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent relevance, stable formulation, and coherent methodological direction, moderated by incomplete foundational justification and limited evidence that the proposed standard is sufficient rather than merely useful.

Matt Slick — section score 75/100

A genuinely self-refuting worldview is necessarily inferior, at least intellectually, to a competing worldview that avoids the same internal defeat.

Strongest feature: The conditional threshold is logically powerful and appropriately bounded: if one worldview genuinely defeats the intellectual foundations required to assert it, comparative inferiority follows in that respect. Principal limitation: Establishing the threshold does not establish that secular humanism actually self-refutes, nor that Christianity avoids equivalent internal difficulties or performs better across the motion’s practical domains. Live burden: The Christian case must derive a specific contradiction from secular commitments, defend every bridge in that derivation, and connect foundational consistency to flourishing and decision-making outcomes. Locked score: The strong band recognizes exceptional clarity, sound conditional force, and direct support for a subsidiary burden, while reserving completion for the unproven application and broader comparative bridge.

Cognition, Logical Inference, and Epistemic Reliability

49:47–84:13

Matt Dillahunty — section score 81/100

Fallible brains do not create logical truths; people can recognize mind-independent logical relations even though particular reasoning episodes may fail.

Strongest feature: The reply cleanly separates production from recognition and candidly concedes individual fallibility, thereby avoiding the unnecessary claim that rational cognition requires an infallibility guarantee. Principal limitation: Recognition remains more asserted than explained, leaving unresolved how physically embodied thinkers reliably access mind-independent logical truths and distinguish genuine insight from systematic error. Live burden: The account must connect cognitive mechanisms, logical constraints, corrective practices, and successful prediction into a non-question-begging explanation of practical truth-tracking. Locked score: The strong band reflects stable terminology, intellectual calibration, substantial direct responsiveness, and a coherent conceptual distinction, moderated by the missing reliability mechanism, limited evidentiary support, and incomplete engagement with the foundational version of the objection.

Matt Slick — section score 71/100

If cognition is wholly physically caused, secular naturalism must explain reliable logical inference, whereas Christianity grounds rational capacity in minds bearing God’s image.

Strongest feature: The objection identifies a genuine epistemological burden by asking how physically realized cognition can reliably track mind-independent logical relations rather than merely produce causally determined responses. Principal limitation: It assumes without sufficient argument that physical causation undermines justification, while the appeal to divine image likewise does not independently explain reliable access, error, or cognitive calibration. Live burden: The case must show incompatibility between causal embodiment and truth-tracking, then demonstrate that Christian anthropology provides a less circular, more predictive account of successful and unsuccessful inference. Locked score: The mixed band reflects high relevance and a clear contrast between naturalist and Christian pictures, but also an unstable causation-to-defeat transition, sparse warrant, and an asserted theological alternative.

Foundations of Logic, Science, and Intelligibility

53:28–146:44

Matt Dillahunty — section score 86/100

Presupposing logic without an ultimate divine account does not, by itself, demonstrate that secular reasoning is false, unreliable, or internally defective.

Strongest feature: The reply precisely blocks an invalid inference from lacking a further metaphysical account of logic to having a false or defective practical reasoning framework. Principal limitation: This successful diagnostic denial does not provide the positive reliability account requested elsewhere, so it answers one entailment while leaving a broader epistemological challenge active. Live burden: The secular case must explain how logical principles are recognized, how reasoning errors are detected, and why its checking practices warrant confidence without promising certainty. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional directness, explicit concession, excellent calibration, and decisive contact with the alleged defect, while retaining a modest deduction because rebutting insufficiency is not equivalent to completing the affirmative account of reliability.

Matt Slick — section score 73/100

Science presupposes logic and nature’s uniformity and cannot establish every precondition through its own methods, leaving secular humanism with a foundational obligation.

Strongest feature: The move accurately distinguishes scientific inquiry from justification of every presupposition it employs, especially logical validity and expectations concerning nature’s regularity. Principal limitation: Showing that science cannot experimentally prove all its preconditions neither reduces secular justification to unsupported faith nor establishes that Christianity uniquely grounds those preconditions. Live burden: The argument must specify the required kind of justification, compare secular pragmatic, abductive, and realist accounts, and derive logic and uniformity from Christian commitments without circularity. Locked score: The strong threshold band reflects a clear, important foundational challenge with stable terms and direct central relevance, moderated by an underdefined justificatory demand, overstated secular conclusion, and missing comparative demonstration.

Moral Standards, Consequences, and Context

24:18–138:26

Matt Dillahunty — section score 84/100

Secular humanism can revise judgments with new information and evaluate exceptional circumstances contextually instead of relying only on unexplained fixed prohibitions.

Strongest feature: The disaster example concretely demonstrates why morally relevant circumstances, reasons, and possible exceptions matter, giving the secular method an intelligible advantage over an isolated bare prohibition. Principal limitation: One compelling illustration cannot establish general methodological superiority, because sophisticated rule-based traditions may treat prohibitions as defeasible principles embedded within broader interpretive and justificatory structures. Live burden: The method must articulate the welfare criterion, decision procedure, evidentiary thresholds, and safeguards that prevent contextual flexibility from becoming selective rationalization or inconsistent application. Locked score: The upper strong band reflects explicit qualification, stable terminology, excellent central relevance, and a well-chosen counterexample, tempered by an implicit decision framework and insufficient comparison with stronger Christian moral reasoning.

Matt Slick — section score 79/100

Consequentialism needs a non-circular basis for classifying outcomes as good or harmful rather than using harm reduction to justify itself.

Strongest feature: The move presses an indispensable normative question: consequence-sensitive reasoning requires some defensible criterion for identifying good, bad, harmful, and beneficial outcomes. Principal limitation: Its circularity diagnosis treats reliance on harm too schematically, because welfare criteria may receive constitutive, experiential, contractual, or reflective justification beyond verbal repetition. Live burden: The objection must distinguish vicious circularity from unavoidable coherence among basic values, engage the strongest justification of conscious interests, and show that Christian grounding avoids parallel dependence on foundational commitments. Locked score: The upper strong band reflects excellent central relevance, coherent conditional pressure, and substantial diagnostic value, tempered by insufficient proof that every secular welfare criterion is circular and limited engagement with available normative bridges.

Practical Outcomes, Cooperation, and Conflict Resolution

28:17–163:02

Matt Dillahunty — section score 79/100

Peace and cooperation benefit human life, while secular commitments to equality, autonomy, and flourishing strongly oppose slavery and murder.

Strongest feature: The move connects abstract secular commitments with concrete practical consequences, showing how equality, autonomy, cooperation, and flourishing can jointly support opposition to slavery and murder. Principal limitation: The categorical assertion that nothing within secular humanism could instruct killing or enslavement exceeds what naming these values establishes, especially where values conflict or applications become contested. Live burden: The case must explain priority rules among autonomy, equality, aggregate welfare, and defensive necessity, then compare their historical and institutional reliability with Christian alternatives. Locked score: The strong band recognizes clear practical relevance, coherent value-to-consequence reasoning, and meaningful motion contact, while incorporating the unsupported universal exclusion, missing qualifications, and limited evidence for comparative reliability across difficult cases.

Matt Slick — section score 68/100

Christian flourishing consists in proper worship, humility, and biblical obedience expressed through love of God and one’s neighbor.

Strongest feature: The reply supplies substantive Christian content by defining flourishing through worship, humility, and neighbor-love rather than accepting a purely temporal or preference-based welfare conception. Principal limitation: It presupposes that the Bible is God’s word and that proper worship constitutes flourishing, while neither comparing practical outcomes nor answering the highlighted scriptural disagreement. Live burden: The account must defend its theological premises, explain how love resolves conflicting commands and interpretations, and show superior consequences or decision procedures across the motion’s domains. Locked score: The mixed band recognizes a clear, stable, and relevant alternative conception, but reflects its assumed foundations, incomplete responsiveness, absent comparative evidence, and failure to resolve the practical reliability challenge.
