Overall assessment

Andrew Loke — First-cause theism: 70/100

Published strengths

  • Loke accepted the incompleteness of present physics and supplied an independent philosophical route, keeping his positive case from depending entirely on unsettled cosmological models.
  • His best replies drew useful distinctions between efficient and material causes, between deliberation and choice, and between causing matter and causing an ordered state.
  • He repeatedly returned to real events, causal dependence, and the need for differentiation, giving the affirmative case a recognizable inferential spine across the full exchange.

Published weaknesses

  • The regress argument relied chiefly on a borrower analogy and references to outside publications, leaving the decisive exclusion of infinite or circular dependence underdeveloped in the debate itself.
  • The agency route repeatedly treated instability, withholding, and libertarian freedom as mutually supporting without independently showing why an impersonal nonmetric ground could not remain eventless.

Dan Linford — Cosmological skepticism: 88/100

Published strengths

  • Linford correctly preserved a modest epistemic burden: he argued for nonacceptance of a cause or beginning without pretending to prove that physical reality is beginningless or uncaused.
  • His strongest sequence distinguished local observation from global spacetime structure, metric length from event count, and emergent temporal relations from properties of their timeless physical ground.
  • The material-parity case developed throughout questioning, moving from ordinary comparison to field constraints, explanatory reasons, and a direct material-only transformation parallel.

Published weaknesses

  • The opening material-principle analogy initially moved too quickly from familiar rearrangements to equal metaphysical warrant, requiring the later field-property and transformation arguments to supply its strongest support.
  • The emergent-causation argument compressed the transition from time-symmetric or reciprocal fundamental relations to the stronger conclusion that physical reality as a whole could not be caused.

The causal principle and its proposed symmetry breaker

10:28–49:15

Strongest feature: Loke presents a recognizable modus-tollens route to the central causal principle, using the absence of arbitrary lethal field increases to give the universal claim observable significance. Principal limitation: The crucial conditional assumes that without a cause there could be no antecedent differentiator, yet laws, essences, circumstances, or reasons might constrain what begins without functioning as the efficient cause he emphasizes. Live burden: He must show why every relevant differentiating constraint counts as a cause or is unavailable before existence, rather than repeatedly treating the disputed conditional as already established. Locked score: The 72-point rating credits substantial relevance, a recoverable inferential structure, and useful responsiveness to the parity challenge, but records mixed coherence and warrant because the universal generalization rests on precisely the constraint alternative later developed by Linford.
