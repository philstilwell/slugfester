Overall assessment

James White — The Bible is true: 81/100

Published strengths

  • White’s manuscript-stream case converts interpolation into a testable prediction and repeatedly presses the complete absence of any surviving contrary First Corinthians lineage.
  • His mission-sequence reply resolves the alleged Matthean contradiction through narrative chronology while exposing the weakness of inference from Acts’ selective silence.
  • White distinguishes admitting supernatural claims from believing them and insists that Christian and rival traditions receive comparably critical historical examination.

Published weaknesses

  • Defining truth through internal consistency overstates coherence’s power because elaborate false systems can remain consistent while failing against external evidence.
  • The Apollonius comparison asserts superior biblical coherence without enough paired evidence for listeners to verify the claimed difference independently.

Robert Price — Biblical reliability is unestablished: 78/100

Published strengths

  • Price sharply separates theological assurance from historical warrant when providential preservation guarantees the very reliability supposedly under examination.
  • His meteorology analogy and provisional-history account permit miracles metaphysically without pretending historians can trace unpredictable divine causes through ordinary methods.
  • Price preserves literary and moral value despite textual criticism, preventing skepticism about origin from becoming indiscriminate hostility toward biblical material.

Published weaknesses

  • Saying the Damascus narrative screams literary origin outruns the compressed Heliodorus and Pentheus comparison and neglects ordinary motif explanations.
  • The speculative Paul–Simon reconstruction answers White’s standing question only by adding a contested chronology and identity theory with weak support.

Historical method and supernatural claims

7:30–2:11:48

James White — section score 77/100

White argues Price’s analogy principle excludes divine action before evidence is heard, so rival historical methods should instead be compared for consistent evidential treatment.

Strongest feature: White directly contests Price’s governing historical method by arguing that a principle of analogy can exclude divine action before evidence is assessed, and his appeal to consistency makes that methodological dispute clear and responsive. Principal limitation: Price expressly permits miracles while withholding automatic credibility from miracle reports, so White does not establish that this disciplinary restraint is covert metaphysical naturalism or that Price has barred every positive conclusion in advance. Live burden: White must supply publicly assessable criteria that can admit divine explanations when warranted while distinguishing them from competing supernatural claims under equally demanding historical standards. Locked score: The fixed 76 reflects a coherent, central, and notably responsive challenge whose force is reduced by modest evidential support and by its insufficiently charitable treatment of Price’s stated distinction.

Robert Price — section score 80/100

Price separates miracle possibility from Gospel accuracy, arguing that providential preservation assumes the historical reliability it is supposed to establish through independent evidence.

Strongest feature: Price uses Ladd’s providential-preservation claim to draw a responsive distinction between confessional assurance that the Gospels remained accurate and publicly assessable historical evidence for that accuracy. Principal limitation: The need for divine supervision does not itself show that distortion was ordinarily probable, and one cited preservation claim does not establish that evangelical apologetics generally depends on this controlling assumption. Live burden: Price must provide independent evidence of actual distortion and demonstrate that White’s reliability case rests on providence rather than manuscript evidence, contextual comparison, or other historical considerations. Locked score: The fixed 75 recognizes the move’s clear formulation, concrete target, and strong relevance to public warrant, while its weaker causal inference and broader characterization keep its logical coherence and evidential support below its responsiveness and precision.

Eyewitnesses, oral tradition, and distortion

9:11–1:38:20

James White — section score 83/100

White rejects Price’s Hadith analogy because major collections arose centuries after Muhammad, unlike the comparatively early documentary foundation he attributes to New Testament writings.

Strongest feature: White isolates a temporal disanalogy in Price’s comparison, arguing that hadith collections arose roughly 250 to 270 years after Muhammad while New Testament documents, on White’s dating, stand nearer their events and retain earlier material. Principal limitation: That contrast depends on the disputed chronology White asserts and does not establish that shorter transmission prevented pious attribution, especially without comparing oral layers, community controls, attribution incentives, and intermediary evidence across the traditions. Live burden: White must defend his Gospel dates and show, through historical evidence, that proximity produced effective correction rather than merely reducing the time available for development. Locked score: The fixed 80 reflects a coherent and responsive challenge to an important analogy, tempered by moderate evidential warrant, incomplete comparison, and overstatement in declaring no parallel.

Robert Price — section score 81/100

Price argues that imagining vigilant disciples suppressing invented reports is not evidence they successfully policed tradition, and reasoning backward from desired reliability is circular.

Strongest feature: Price distinguishes a conceivable eyewitness safeguard from evidence that living disciples consistently detected and suppressed invented reports, exposing the inferential gap where mere possibility was used to guarantee uncontaminated tradition. Principal limitation: His critique remains negative and offers little affirmative evidence about how reports circulated, when witnesses intervened, which communities could coordinate correction, or whether partial eyewitness constraint operated without comprehensive policing. Live burden: Price must map circulation routes, named witnesses, competing communities, and documented correction episodes to estimate the safeguard’s reach while allowing that limited controls could constrain some developments. Locked score: The fixed 81 recognizes high responsiveness, clarity, coherence, and charitable calibration in denying a guarantee rather than asserting certain distortion, while reduced evidential support prevents the forceful conceptual objection from ranking higher.

Authorship, sources, and literary composition

31:22–1:40:28

James White — section score 77/100

White announces that Price’s authorship and transmission theory is undemonstrated and conflicts with consistent interpretation, but initially supplies only a roadmap for later support.

Strongest feature: White identifies a standard by insisting that Price’s authorship and transmission reconstruction must cohere with authorial purpose and the extant manuscript record before displacing a historical reading. Principal limitation: This passage announces that Price’s theory is incapable of demonstration or defense and contradicted by the data, yet supplies no literary seam, manuscript finding, dated comparison, or account of Price’s internal evidence to establish those categorical judgments. Live burden: Present passage-level conflicts and transmission evidence that distinguish White’s reconstruction from Price’s, then show why those materials favor one explanation without assuming the disputed chronology. Locked score: The 63 recognizes a centrally relevant roadmap and a demand for explanatory fit, but its evidential warrant, precision, responsiveness, and calibration remain limited because the promised supporting argument has not appeared.

Robert Price — section score 73/100

Price argues diaspora Judaism’s cosmopolitan setting made scriptural and Greek literary materials jointly available, so mixed source use by Gospel authors remains culturally coherent.

Strongest feature: Price answers White’s incompatibility objection with synagogue art, funerary imagery, a treatment of Ezekiel, awareness of foreign gods, and Maccabean conflict, showing that Jewish communities could encounter scriptural and Hellenistic materials without sealed cultural worlds. Principal limitation: These examples establish availability and interaction, not evangelists’ dependence on Homer, Euripides, or pagan gods, while differing regions and periods weaken their representativeness for the authors and audiences at issue. Live burden: Price must date each comparator, establish plausible authorial access, identify distinctive textual correspondences, and show that borrowing explains them better than shared Jewish traditions or cultural knowledge. Locked score: The fixed 80 reflects a coherent, concrete, highly responsive rebuttal that keeps mixed-source composition plausible, but compressed chronology and the unproven path from contact to dependence prevent a stronger rating.

Manuscripts, interpolation, and textual transmission

18:16–1:45:04

James White — section score 87/100

White argues independently branching manuscript streams would preserve First Corinthians versions without verses 3–11 if that passage entered only through later interpolation.

Strongest feature: White makes the manuscript tradition a historical test, arguing that branching transmission and tenacious variant readings should preserve traces of a major interpolation, while surviving witnesses contain no manuscript stream without First Corinthians 15:3–11. Principal limitation: The inference assumes branching preceded the alteration, because an insertion made before recoverable streams diverged could pass unanimously into later witnesses and leave exactly the uniform record White cites, despite not belonging to the earliest text. Live burden: Date the relevant branch points and compare expected survival of passage-free witnesses under early-insertion, late-insertion, and original-text models, applying equivalent preservation assumptions throughout. Locked score: The 86 recognizes a clear, highly responsive argument grounded in observed manuscript behavior, but stops higher because absent variants constrain later interpolation without independently excluding alteration before recoverable branching.

Robert Price — section score 78/100

Price notes Paul was misrepresented during his lifetime and disputed writings gained ancient followers, undermining confidence that contemporaries reliably suppressed distorted Christian traditions.

Strongest feature: Price grounds his challenge to apostolic policing in examples, pairing Paul's complaints about distortion during his lifetime with divided reception of disputed writings to show that correction and proximity did not ensure uniform transmission. Principal limitation: These counterexamples establish misrepresentation and contested attribution, but not that central claims were durably replaced or lost, or that the cited communities fed the canonical transmission streams at issue. Live burden: He must trace durable corruption into those streams and show why that explanation fits the surviving historical pattern better than preservation amid ordinary disagreement. Locked score: A 75 reflects a coherent, responsive, source-focused challenge with useful historical analogies, tempered by weaker evidential linkage and calibration because the examples support transmission risk more clearly than the claimed scale or persistence of corruption.

Contradictions, context, and anachronism

19:50–1:13:44

James White — section score 85/100

White argues Galileans understood Roman crucifixion as a death march, so Jesus could demand taking up one’s cross before his own later execution.

Strongest feature: White gives the cross metaphor a concrete pre-crucifixion setting by explaining that Galileans could recognize Roman crucifixion as terror and understand the call as self-denial joined to a death march. Principal limitation: Showing that the words were intelligible before Jesus died establishes a possible reading, but not that Jesus uttered them, while their fit with later Christian reflection leaves retrospective formulation or reshaping a live alternative. Live burden: The argument must compare wording and narrative placement across traditions and show that pre-event origin explains them better than later composition under demanding historical standards. Locked score: The score of 83 reflects a clear, responsive contextual rebuttal with strong coherence and precision, moderated by limited direct warrant for utterance and the unresolved possibility that later resonance influenced the received form.

Robert Price — section score 77/100

Price compares John, Mark, Matthew, Luke, and Acts to show the temple-destruction saying receives divergent attribution, narrative setting, and interpretation across traditions.

Strongest feature: Price anchors his developmental account in a concrete comparison among John's bodily interpretation of the temple saying, its false-witness placement in Mark and Matthew, and Luke's related treatment through Stephen. Principal limitation: These differences make interpretive development plausible but do not demonstrate incompatible judgments about one identical sentence, because distinct speakers, purposes, and narrative settings could produce complementary selections without textual distortion. Live burden: The argument must establish sufficiently identical variants and show that expectation-driven transmission explains their divergent handling better than contextual attribution or complementary narrative choice, with alternatives tested by the same historical standards. Locked score: The score of 79 rewards specific cross-canonical evidence, direct relevance, and an auditable comparative inference, while remaining constrained because logical compatibility persists and alternative narrative explanations receive too little consideration.

Inspiration, meaning, and the Bible's value

58:14–2:15:52

James White — section score 76/100

White argues biblical claims differ fundamentally from Apollonius traditions in worldview and coherence, so admitting divine speech need not collapse every miracle account together.

Strongest feature: White rejects the choice between excluding divine action in advance and accepting every miracle story alike, arguing that Apollonius and Scripture differ in worldview and narrative consistency while reconnecting the dispute to whether God has spoken. Principal limitation: He asserts those fundamental differences and biblical consistency without presenting paired examples, defining a coherence test, or showing why coherence establishes historical truth or divine authorship rather than literary unity. Live burden: Compare specific claims from Philostratus and Scripture under equal historical standards, identify measurable differences, and explain why the better supported account warrants the theological conclusion. Locked score: The 76 reflects a coherent, responsive comparative reply with clear relevance to the motion, tempered by thin displayed evidence, undefined criteria, and an inferential gap between consistency and divine speech.

Robert Price — section score 83/100

Price argues ethical and literary value survives disputed authorship or textual secondary status, citing admired passages that he regards as meaningful later additions.

Strongest feature: Price separates a passage’s origin, authorship, canonical status, inspiration, and present value, using secondary examples to show that critical conclusions need not erase literary, moral, or theological worth. Principal limitation: That distinction preserves value but does not establish biblical unreliability, identify whether any disputed saying is original, or defeat White’s historical claim, leaving its contact with the motion supportive rather than decisive. Live burden: Connect the independence of value from provenance to a reason for rejecting the motion while recognizing why provenance matters when a text is offered as historical testimony. Locked score: The 83 rewards clear distinctions, concrete examples, charitable calibration, and responsiveness to the claim that criticism leaves no treasures, while withholding stronger credit because the argument clarifies Price’s position more than it proves unreliability.
