Overall assessment

Cameron Bertuzzi — Theistic no-evidence defense: 78/100

Published strengths

  • Precisely identified that a known naturalistic likelihood does not by itself establish comparative confirmation when the theistic likelihood remains genuinely unknown.
  • Corrected the portrayal of skeptical theism as reliance on favorable hidden reasons by presenting it as a limited and potentially unrepresentative sampling problem.
  • Directly challenged the background information and analogical basis used to generate naturalistic expectations rather than conceding those likelihood assignments.
  • Offered soul-building, freedom, complete-life integration, and eventual defeat as substantive theistic mechanisms instead of resting on logical compatibility alone.

Published weaknesses

  • The closing synthesis declared the soul-building routes successful without answering the strongest objections about nonhuman scope, destructive effects, proportionality, necessity, and less harmful alternatives.
  • Several theodicy claims moved from possible recovery or development to comparative probability without supplying the required distributional and counterfactual support.

Justin Schieber — Draperian evil evidence: 87/100

Published strengths

  • Clearly defended the correct comparative-likelihood standard and distinguished relative prediction from entailment or high absolute probability.
  • Developed three mutually reinforcing data classes involving biological orientation, widespread languishing, and horrendous evil.
  • Pressed the soul-building account on its weakest dimensions: nonhuman scope, destructive outcomes, observed proportions, preventability, and less harmful alternatives.
  • Separated afterlife compensation from prediction, justification, necessity, and complete explanation of the observed suffering pattern.

Published weaknesses

  • The final skeptical-theism reply treated an unknown theistic likelihood too much like a determinate inferior likelihood, leaving the claimed comparative ordering unsupported.
  • The unknown-reasons symmetry argument answered an asymmetric appeal to hidden reasons more effectively than the actual limited-sample case for suspended judgment.

Comparative Likelihood and Naturalistic Prediction

15:40–64:14

Cameron Bertuzzi — section score 86/100

Schieber has not established naturalism's prediction because evolutionary background information either assists both hypotheses or improperly supplies explanatory resources unavailable to naturalism alone.

Strongest feature: The reply targets the shared likelihood premise beneath all three opening arguments and usefully asks whether evolutionary background information independently supports naturalism rather than merely redescribing observations already placed into the evidential background. Principal limitation: Prediction, explanation, and background enrichment partly change functions within the objection, and the claim that additional information makes every hypothesis predict the data lacks a demonstrated bridge for each suffering class. Live burden: Bertuzzi must show that the permitted background collapses or equalizes the likelihood comparison, while Schieber must specify independently disciplined background commitments and distinguish relative prediction from a demand for complete explanation. Locked score: The strong band reflects broad decisive contact and an intelligible methodological challenge, moderated by conceptual instability, limited case-specific demonstration, and incomplete charity toward comparative confirmation.

Justin Schieber — section score 90/100

Naturalism need only make the suffering facts more expected than theism does; it need not entail them or make them probable in isolation.

Strongest feature: The reply precisely distinguishes absolute probability from comparative likelihood and correctly identifies the evidential standard needed for the cited facts to favor naturalism without requiring entailment or probability above one-half. Principal limitation: The correction does not entirely dispose of Bertuzzi's stronger concern, because a relative comparison still requires independently warranted likelihood assignments and disciplined background information rather than a complete naturalistic explanation. Live burden: Schieber must demonstrate the relative ordering for each principal data class, while Bertuzzi must avoid substituting an unnecessarily demanding explanatory standard for the actual comparative requirement. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional diagnostic contact, conceptual clarity, calibration, and central relevance, with only a bounded representational limitation arising from its incomplete treatment of the background-dependence objection.

Biological Orientation of Pain and Pleasure

2:04–41:17

Cameron Bertuzzi — section score 78/100

Alternative naturalistic assignments of biological and moral roles remain possible, so the analogy cannot confidently establish naturalism's expected organization of pain and pleasure.

Strongest feature: The reply directly challenges the asserted naturalistic expectation by constructing an alternative assignment in which other systems serve biological functions while pain and pleasure primarily serve moral roles, thereby exposing underdetermination in the analogy. Principal limitation: Merely possible role assignments do not carry equal likelihood, and the move does not compare evolutionary mechanisms that might specifically favor affective systems closely tracking survival, injury, reproduction, and resource acquisition. Live burden: Bertuzzi must show mechanism-level parity or defective background selection, while Schieber must demonstrate that selection-supported expectations privilege the observed biological orientation over the proposed alternative. Locked score: The strong band reflects clear recoverability, cautious calibration, broad direct contact, and a relevant subsidiary challenge, limited by sparse probabilistic support and insufficient engagement with mechanism-based expectations.

Justin Schieber — section score 88/100

Pain and pleasure primarily serve biological goals and appear morally random, a pattern Schieber argues is more expected on naturalism than on morally perfect theism.

Strongest feature: The argument identifies a concrete contrast between biologically directed sensations and the morally directed guidance plausibly anticipated from an omnipotent, perfectly good creator, thereby supplying a relevant and readily understandable comparative datum. Principal limitation: Its characterization of the system as morally random receives little independent demonstration, and the excerpt does not establish how frequently biological usefulness and moral usefulness diverge across representative cases. Live burden: The naturalistic side must substantiate the claimed distribution, while the theistic side must explain why predominantly biological orientation remains expectable given divine moral purposes and possible indirect contributions to agency. Sentient Flourishing and Languishing

5:25–23:37

Cameron Bertuzzi — section score 76/100

Significant suffering can enable loving virtues, belong within an overall good life, and ultimately be defeated, making suffering no less probable on theism.

Strongest feature: The move offers a substantive theistic mechanism rather than bare compatibility, linking significant suffering with costly virtues, an integrated good life, and eventual defeat while expressly presenting its likelihood conclusion as modest and nonexhaustive. Justin Schieber — section score 86/100

Principal limitation: The excerpt does not document the relevant proportions or demonstrate that the selected mechanisms characterize a representative range of sentient lives, so its language of entire expectation outruns the displayed empirical support. Live burden: Schieber must establish prevalence and comparative distribution more fully, while Bertuzzi must show why a perfectly good creator would permit those mechanisms to generate extensive nonhuman as well as human languishing. Horrendous Evil and Divine Prevention

Cameron Bertuzzi — section score 71/100

Freedom, possible afterlife development, and eventual defeat mean horrendous evil need not permanently destroy a soul within a broader developmental account.

Strongest feature: The reply usefully distinguishes severe present injury from permanent destruction and places freedom, continued development, eventual defeat, and saint-producing possibilities within a broader account of a person's complete life. Principal limitation: Those possibilities neither establish necessity nor support the compressed fine-tuning inference, and eventual recovery does not explain the observed incidence, original preventability, nonhuman scope, or absence of less harmful transformative arrangements. Live burden: Bertuzzi must connect afterlife development and freedom to comparative prediction rather than mere compatibility, while Schieber must show why even integrated recovery leaves the permission substantially surprising. Locked score: The mixed band reflects meaningful but partial contact with permanence and destructive effect, accompanied by clear modal relevance, while preserving serious deficits in warrant, precision, alternative-means responsiveness, and proportional explanation.

Justin Schieber — section score 87/100

Profound suffering, disfigurement, and devastating loss are less surprising under morally indifferent naturalism than under perfectly informed, resourceful, and compassionate theism.

Principal limitation: The excerpt supplies a broad category and an explanatory sketch but comparatively little detail about incidence, preventability, background conditions, or the precise expectations generated by perfect divine compassion. Live burden: Schieber must defend the likelihood contrast across representative horrendous evils, whereas Bertuzzi must explain why morally significant freedom, later recovery, or other goods make their actual severity and distribution reasonably expected. Locked score: The strong band reflects clear relevance, coherent calibration, and bounded scope, while recognizing that its empirical warrant and treatment of developed theistic alternatives remain less complete than its central importance.

Scope and Necessity of Soul-Building

33:32–64:37

Cameron Bertuzzi — section score 70/100

Bertuzzi closes by combining soul-building with continued skeptical-theist agnosticism as a joint defense against Schieber's probabilistic argument from evil.

Strongest feature: The closing synthesis connects the two principal defensive routes and correctly emphasizes that a successful global likelihood neutralizer could matter even if particular soul-building details remain contestable. Principal limitation: It categorically declares success without answering the later objections concerning nonhuman scope, destructive outcomes, proportional distribution, compensation, necessity, and less harmful alternatives, while leaving the combination's inferential basis largely unstated. Live burden: Bertuzzi must explain how the routes jointly overcome those outstanding objections without double counting, whereas Schieber must show that skeptical uncertainty does not independently neutralize the otherwise stronger theodicy critique. Locked score: The mixed band reflects direct motion-level relevance and some continuing skeptical-theist contact, but also material overstatement, missing qualification, weak new warrant, and a significant nonanswer to decisive soul-building challenges.

Justin Schieber — section score 90/100

Human soul-building leaves most sentient languishing unexplained, while an unlimited God could use less harmful transformative experiences such as vivid dreams or visions.

Strongest feature: The reply identifies two independent and central deficiencies in the theodicy: its restricted application to human moral agents and the apparent availability of less harmful transformative means to an unlimited and empathic creator. Principal limitation: Calling dreams or visions categorically better compresses possible differences in embodied agency, responsibility, interpersonal stakes, epistemic distance, and the developmental consequences of confronting actual rather than represented hardship. Live burden: Schieber must show that plausible divine aims remain achievable through materially less destructive arrangements, while Bertuzzi must explain both vast nonhuman suffering and why the observed severity is necessary for goods unavailable through alternatives. Locked score: The very strong band reflects direct, charitable, and highly relevant engagement with scope and necessity, tempered by comparatively limited defense of the proposed substitutes.

Skeptical Theism and Unknown Reasons

23:34–56:38

Cameron Bertuzzi — section score 85/100

Human cognitive distance from divinity leaves the probability of suffering on theism unknown, so naturalistic prediction alone cannot establish comparative confirmation.

Strongest feature: The reply isolates the exact comparative requirement shared by the opening arguments and explains that knowing one hypothesis's likelihood does not establish an ordering when the competing theistic likelihood remains epistemically unavailable. Principal limitation: Its categorical no-evidence consequence depends on defending total unavailability, because known divine attributes might still supply directional constraints or defensible bounds even when complete divine reasons remain inaccessible. Live burden: Bertuzzi must justify why human limitations defeat every useful likelihood estimate rather than merely counseling caution, while Schieber must derive an ordering without treating uncertainty as a determinate middling value. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, central relevance, and explicit probabilistic focus, moderated by analogy-heavy warrant and an insufficient answer to the possibility of informative lower-order bounds.

Justin Schieber — section score 77/100

Unknown reasons may oppose divine permission as readily as support it, so they confer no favorable presumption and leave known comparative reasons operative.

Strongest feature: The reply blocks an asymmetrical appeal to mystery by observing that unknown moral reasons could favor prevention as readily as permission, thereby refusing to let unspecified possibilities automatically outweigh articulated comparative evidence. Principal limitation: Symmetry among unknown reasons does not itself establish that known reasons are representative, probabilistically dominant, or sufficient for estimating the total theistic likelihood, and skeptical theism need not posit a favorable hidden pool. Live burden: Schieber must justify why known considerations retain directional force despite acknowledged sampling limits, while Bertuzzi must explain why those limits defeat useful bounds rather than merely weakening confidence. Locked score: The strong band reflects direct contact, explicit structure, and central relevance, moderated by a material representational shortfall and an incomplete bridge from symmetry to evidential priority.
