Overall assessment

Zac Sechler — Perfect-mind theism: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Established a coherent comparative framework in which inaccessible origins can still be assessed through theoretical virtues rather than direct observation alone.
  • Correctly distinguished differential evidential support from logical incompatibility and from conclusive proof.
  • Conceded suffering, disagreement, hiddenness, and moral change while proportionately narrowing the moral-agent claim to a possible local increment.
  • Connected moral perfection to moral-agent expectedness and supplied candidate relationship goods addressing divine hiddenness.

Published weaknesses

  • The simplicity case never supplied a stable complexity measure showing why perfection is cheaper than imperfect or non-theistic foundations.
  • The cumulative observational case repeatedly asserted conditional-likelihood advantages without specifying divine intentions, non-theistic baselines, or dependence among observations.
  • The hiddenness response established possible compatibility but not why actual ambiguity is necessary or proportionate to the proposed goods.

Matt Dillahunty — Evidential skepticism: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Decomposed the creator inference into possibility, identification, capacity, and causal responsibility, making the central evidential burden explicit.
  • Consistently pressed for specified comparison classes and expectation-generating assumptions behind the claimed likelihood inequalities.
  • Expanded the moral evidential profile beyond agent existence to disagreement, change, suffering, hiddenness, and absent corrective guidance.
  • Offered concrete agent-interaction and cognitive-fallibility accounts that reduced the observations' claimed distinctiveness under theism.

Published weaknesses

  • The claim that theism has no explanatory power was substantially stronger than the abbreviated agent-based support established.
  • The magical-box analogy blurred maximal power, informational complexity, testability, and unspecified intention.
  • The single-universe probability objection conflated observed actuality with antecedent probability and overstated the need for comparative universes.

Principal limitation: It largely asserts that imperfection incurs complexity without defining the relevant metric, explaining why perfection is informationally cheaper, or applying common criteria across every rival. Live burden: The comparative strategy therefore needs transparent theoretical virtues, independently justified weighting, and concrete demonstrations that perfect mentality predicts observations more economically than imperfect, evil, mindless, or naturalistic alternatives.
