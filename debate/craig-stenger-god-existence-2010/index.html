Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Christian theism: 76/100

Published strengths

  • Craig’s bounded-models reply distinguishes quantum vacuum structure from nothing and notes deterministic interpretations, directly answering several natural-origin objections with unusually strong responsiveness.
  • His intersubjective-versus-objective distinction identifies the precise gap between shared convention and mind-independent obligation, earning the debate’s strongest moral-exchange score through exceptionally direct target contact.
  • The cumulative closing reconnects cosmic origin, morality, and resurrection to the motion while directly contesting Stenger’s expected-evidence premises and burden allocation across the full case.
  • Craig’s historical case uses multiple source streams and majority scholarly judgments to support three core facts, keeping the resurrection inference publicly assessable despite dispute.

Published weaknesses

  • The evolutionary defeater compresses the difference between survival-linked cognition and truth-tracking reliability, leaving its self-defeat conclusion underwarranted despite contacting materialism directly on epistemic grounds.
  • The contingency replies clarify why literal nothing needs no explanation but do not establish why existing contingent reality must terminate in a personal necessary cause.
  • Craig’s morally sufficient reasons reply defeats a strict logical incompatibility more readily than Stenger’s evidential claim about suffering’s probability under a good God.

Victor Stenger — Scientific naturalism: 69/100

Published strengths

  • Stenger’s brain-dependence case connects changing consciousness to physical conditions and neuroscience, providing his clearest empirical support for material explanation over an immaterial soul.
  • The prayer argument proposes a genuinely testable consequence of divine intervention and cites controlled null results while conceding that positive findings could have supported God.
  • His evolutionary complexity argument combines a natural mechanism with biological imperfections, giving concrete comparative evidence against straightforward intelligent-design expectations concerning unrestricted engineering competence.
  • Stenger’s late-empty-tomb alternative sharply contacts Craig’s historical sources, emphasizing contradictions and more parsimonious possibilities rather than merely denying resurrection without an evidential comparison.

Published weaknesses

  • The pizza parody does not match maximal greatness’s alleged modal properties, so it challenges definition-driven existence without adequately testing Craig’s actual possibility premise.
  • The physical-time objection calls time a modeling concept but barely engages Craig’s simultaneous-causation account, producing the weakest combination of coherence, warrant, and responsiveness.
  • Stenger’s natural morality moves explain shared harmfulness judgments but repeatedly slide between social objectivity and mind-independent obligation, leaving Craig’s central grounding distinction intact.
