Overall assessment

Andrew Loke — Reformulated Kalam: 74/100

Published strengths

  • Presented a clear conditional route from excluding regress and loops to at least one first cause.
  • Connected the causal principle directly to beginninglessness and initial changelessness.
  • Responded to loop objections with a useful dynamic-versus-static distinction.
  • Clarified that the intended necessity concerned initial persistence rather than existence in every logically possible world.

Published weaknesses

  • The move from changing familiar physical systems to categorical immateriality left unconventional material foundations insufficiently excluded.
  • The inference from dual causal capacities to libertarian personhood did not eliminate nonpersonal indeterministic powers.
  • The proliferation and selection replies did not adequately distinguish logical consistency from metaphysical possibility.

Graham Oppy — Naturalist theory comparison: 86/100

Published strengths

  • Precisely exposed the unsupported transitivity requirement behind the initial loop exclusion.
  • Distinguished existential first-cause conclusions from the stronger and unproved claim of uniqueness.
  • Developed a coherent necessary-initial-state theory combining modal and subsequent causal explanation.
  • Separated logical consistency from metaphysical possibility with unusually clear calibration.
  • Closed at motion level by contrasting an external creator conditional with a universe-internal first-cause conditional.

Published weaknesses

  • The necessity of the proposed initial natural state was motivated by theoretical economy but not independently demonstrated.
  • The changeless-duration dilemma assumed that atemporal changelessness must occupy a duration, which was the disputed point.
  • The explanatory-economy comparison did not fully inventory the facts each rival theory purported to explain.

First Causes, Infinite Regress, and Causal Loops

4:53–36:02

Andrew Loke — section score 81/100

If infinite causal regress and closed causal loops are excluded, the causal series must terminate in at least one first cause.

Strongest feature: The conditional architecture is exceptionally recoverable, distinguishing infinite causal regression, circular causation, and termination, while expressly limiting its conclusion to the existence of at least one first cause. Principal limitation: The presentation explicitly postpones the substantive demonstrations excluding both rival structures, leaving its evidential support promissory rather than independently established within this constructive passage. Live burden: The argument therefore still requires nonstipulative defenses of causal transitivity, the impossibility of a closed causal loop, and the exclusion of an infinite regress before its announced conditional can establish an actual terminus. Locked score: The strong/competent band appropriately reflects excellent structural relevance, disciplined qualification, and coherent inferential organization, alongside materially incomplete warrant for the indispensable antecedent exclusions.

Graham Oppy — section score 92/100

Loop exclusion needs a defended transitivity principle, because irreflexivity alone does not conclusively eliminate coherent theories permitting circular causation.

Strongest feature: The objection precisely isolates the controversial transitivity requirement behind loop exclusion and proportionately concludes that loop-permitting theories remain undefeated by mere insistence on that principle. Principal limitation: It demonstrates underjustification in the presented argument rather than independently establishing that causal loops are metaphysically possible or that every stronger transitivity defense fails. Live burden: The naturalist challenge must preserve a coherent loop model, or at minimum show why available arguments for causal transitivity and viciousness do not restore the first-cause derivation. Locked score: The very strong band reflects diagnostic precision, excellent responsiveness, disciplined calibration, and charitable recognition that transitivity could receive a substantive defense beyond the source span currently presented.

The Causal Principle and a Necessary Initial Natural State

41:12–91:33

Andrew Loke — section score 71/100

If the universe began uncaused, no prior selector would distinguish it from other possible universes or events that might likewise begin uncaused.

Strongest feature: The proliferation argument directly pressures a causeless beginning by asking what could restrict uncaused origination to one universe rather than other universes or events. Principal limitation: Absence of a prior causal selector does not imply equal eligibility, probability, or actuality for every logically describable alternative, especially under a necessity-based rival theory. Live burden: The reply must engage metaphysical necessity as a noncausal restriction and establish that the compared alternatives are genuinely metaphysically possible rather than merely consistently describable. Locked score: The mixed band reflects clear relevance, recognizable modus-tollens structure, and a productive explanatory challenge, offset by unrestricted modal scope and failure to contact the naturalist account's decisive theory-level constraint on later entrants.

Graham Oppy — section score 84/100

A necessary initial natural state begins without a cause, while its causal evolution explains everything existing or occurring subsequently.

Strongest feature: The proposal clearly partitions ultimate explanation between metaphysical necessity for the initial natural state and ordinary causal evolution for every subsequent existent or occurrence. Principal limitation: The move states that the initial state must exist but does not independently demonstrate its metaphysical necessity, leaving its foundational modal commitment substantially asserted. Live burden: The theory must justify why this particular initial state is necessary, explain the evidential standard for that necessity, and distinguish principled termination from a convenient stipulation. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects a coherent, economical, centrally relevant alternative with explicit explanatory organization, tempered by the absence of an independent modal demonstration for the foundational state upon which the complete naturalist account depends.

Necessity, Possibility, and Ultimate Explanation

56:45–119:29

Andrew Loke — section score 71/100

Without a selecting cause, logically possible universes appear equally eligible for actuality because nothing distinguishes this universe from those alternatives.

Strongest feature: The move raises a consequential selection challenge by contrasting the actual universe with describable alternatives and asking what privileges this outcome without a determining cause. Principal limitation: It slides from logical possibility to equal eligibility for actuality without defending the bridge to metaphysical possibility, thereby overstating what consistency alone establishes. Live burden: The argument must show that the alternatives inhabit the relevant metaphysical domain and answer the proposal that every genuine world shares a necessary initial history. Locked score: The mixed band reflects direct modal relevance and an intelligible explanatory question, counterbalanced by unstable distinctions among consistency, possibility, and actuality plus incomplete engagement with the rival account's central necessity claim substantively overall.

Graham Oppy — section score 88/100

Every possible world may share an initial history and diverge only when later chances play out differently, making the initial state necessarily first.

Strongest feature: The theory clearly separates a universally shared initial history from later chance-driven divergence, providing a modal architecture that directly restricts alternative initial states. Principal limitation: Simplicity and explanatory adequacy motivate the proposal without deductively establishing that every metaphysically possible world actually shares the stipulated history. Live burden: The naturalist must articulate the relevant simplicity measure, identify the explanatory data selecting this modal theory, and address rival equally economical necessity assignments. Locked score: The very strong band reflects coherent constructive development, explicit calibration as a theory, and central support for the necessary initial-state account, while preserving an evidential gap between comparative theoretical attractiveness and demonstrated metaphysical necessity itself overall.

Beginninglessness, Timelessness, Events, and Change

6:26–124:55

Andrew Loke — section score 76/100

Because whatever begins has a cause and every change is a beginning event, a first cause must be beginningless and initially changeless.

Strongest feature: The move efficiently connects the causal principle, the characterization of change as a beginning event, and the two route-critical properties of beginninglessness and initial changelessness. Principal limitation: Its universal treatment of every change as beginning remains compressed, leaving the relevant scope of beginning and the ontology of instantaneous events insufficiently differentiated. Live burden: The derivation must explain why every temporally initial or instantaneous change possesses a distinct beginning covered by the causal principle, rather than merely an earliest boundary. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects direct central relevance and an intelligible derivation, moderated by partially unstable scope, implicit qualification, and underdeveloped support for its universal classificatory premise overall unacceptable perhaps.

Graham Oppy — section score 79/100

Changelessness requires duration; an infinite duration restores regress, whereas a finite duration begins and therefore forfeits beginninglessness.

Strongest feature: The objection presents a sharply recoverable finite-or-infinite dilemma once changelessness is understood as persistence without alteration across an extension whose measure can be queried. Principal limitation: The dilemma presupposes precisely what the timeless account denies, namely that changelessness must occupy duration rather than characterize an entity lacking temporal extension altogether. Live burden: The con side must justify the semantic or metaphysical requirement of duration and explain why atemporal invariance is incoherent rather than simply outside ordinary temporal usage. Locked score: The mixed band reflects direct engagement, memorable structure, and genuine pressure on the timeless model, offset by an unestablished gateway premise that prevents the proposed alternatives from being demonstrably exhaustive overall here decisively.

Personal Agency and Comparative Explanatory Burden

8:25–145:08

Andrew Loke — section score 72/100

An initially changeless first cause is transcendent and immaterial, rather than material or quantum, because physical systems are continually changing.

Strongest feature: The move directly addresses the indispensable transition from initial changelessness toward transcendence and immateriality, supplying an intelligible contrast with familiar physical and quantum systems. Principal limitation: Its definition of transcendence is unusually permissive, while the categorical generalization that material things constantly change lacks domain restrictions and exceeds the empirical consideration presented. Live burden: The inference must exclude unfamiliar material foundations, static physical boundary conditions, and nonstandard quantum states before changelessness can warrant an immaterial ontological classification. Locked score: The mixed band reflects substantial motion relevance and recoverable reasoning, counterbalanced by unstable terminology, missing qualification, and a materially overstated conclusion resting on an insufficiently comprehensive physical generalization.

Graham Oppy — section score 90/100

The necessary-initial-universe theory explains the relevant facts with fewer entities, while adding a personal God supplies no demonstrated explanatory advantage.

Strongest feature: The comparison aligns each theory's foundational item and subsequent causal explanations, then identifies the personal creator as an additional posit requiring compensating explanatory benefit. Principal limitation: Equal explanatory coverage is not exhaustively demonstrated, because the relevant fact set remains implicit and the creator theory claims selection and purposive explanation. Live burden: The naturalist account must specify comparative criteria, show that its necessary initial state explains every targeted datum, and demonstrate that personal agency contributes no independently supported coverage. Locked score: The very strong band reflects economical theory comparison, excellent motion relevance, and forceful responsiveness, with a bounded reservation concerning the incompletely itemized explananda and still-contested equivalence between rival explanatory achievements overall.
