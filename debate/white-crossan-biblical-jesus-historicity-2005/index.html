Overall assessment

James White — The canonical Gospel accounts of Jesus are authentic and historically accurate: 78/100

Published strengths

  • White repeatedly answered Crossan’s concrete examples, especially Markan agency, Johannine signs, Emmaus prophecy, and the Synoptic staff instructions.
  • His distinction between theological purpose and historical revision gave the affirmative a clear, stable framework across otherwise diverse exchanges.
  • The strongest pro moves combined close textual reference with direct rebuttal, producing decisive advantages in Passion harmony and parable interpretation.

Published weaknesses

  • The worldview argument risked making authenticity depend on prior Christian theism instead of independently demonstrating the disputed events.
  • Calling source reconstructions hypothetical did not adequately confront the observable literary relationships that powered Crossan’s strongest attestation argument.
  • Several rebuttals established possible harmonization or compatibility without showing that White’s historical reading was comparatively more probable.

John Dominic Crossan — The Gospels are developing theological interpretations rather than one uniformly historical account: 77/100

Published strengths

  • Crossan’s dependence argument sharply distinguished multiple documents from independent witnesses and generated the debate’s strongest sustained evidential challenge.
  • His Mark–John comparison used concrete narrative details to show how distinct communities could receive deliberately different portrayals of Jesus.
  • The retrospective-prophecy account directly answered White’s Emmaus challenge while clearly separating broad scriptural hope from event-specific prediction.

Published weaknesses

  • Denying a singular orthodox account partly reframed the motion without proving that canonical plurality defeats authenticity or historical accuracy.
  • The claim that Gospel writers could invent authentic stories was asserted more confidently than its historical evidence in the selected exchange warranted.
  • Parabolic readings of Emmaus and the loaves supplied theological meaning but did not decisively establish that the narrated events were nonhistorical.
