Overall assessment

Dustin Crummett — Substance dualism: 82/100

Published strengths

  • The hemispherectomy sequence progressively controls timing, destruction, symmetry, and causal continuity, yielding a precise fission trilemma whose transitivity pressure becomes the affirmative’s strongest route beyond material identity.
  • The fixed-physical-facts argument transforms personal-identity intuition into an explicit supervenience challenge, connecting alternative determinate survival outcomes to a nonphysical ground instead of leaving the transplant case merely puzzling.
  • Crummett repeatedly distinguishes empirical brain–mind regularities from metaphysical necessity, supplementing that point with disembodiment, zombie, and inverted-qualia conceivability rather than relying upon one modal illustration.
  • His interaction reply is notably calibrated: he concedes some evidence against dualism, questions detectability, and argues that conservation concerns require a comparative analysis of Watkins’s causally efficacious anti-reductionism.

Published weaknesses

  • The simple-subject opening moves from unified first-person ownership to metaphysical indivisibility without adequately excluding an organized composite, process, or constituted material subject as the bearer of unified experience.
  • The theistic explanation of contingent psychophysical laws remains insufficiently constrained; without independent expectations about divine choices, it risks relocating arbitrariness from brute laws into unexplained intentions.
  • The extrinsic-identity objection assumes personal identity must depend only upon intrinsic features of a continuer, yet does not defend that requirement against relational or branching accounts of persistence.

Ben Watkins — Anti-reductionist monism: 81/100

Published strengths

  • Watkins offers a substantive anti-reductionist alternative rather than retreating to reductive physicalism, preserving real consciousness and mental causation through mutually irreducible aspects of a single substance.
  • His neuroscience, genetics, and morphology case supplies the debate’s broadest empirical base, linking localized impairment, inherited psychological traits, and graded animal capacities to physical organization.
  • The no-self response answers all three fission inferences within one framework: numerical identity ends at branching, psychological connectedness continues, and neither successor receives an unexplained further identity fact.
  • Watkins’s theistic concession is carefully limited and highly charitable, granting that irreducible consciousness could surprise naturalism while refusing to infer immaterial substances before their existence is independently established.

Published weaknesses

  • The closure case slides among physical sufficiency, causal completeness, and energy conservation without establishing their equivalence, weakening what should have been the central empirical objection to interaction.
  • The claim that nonphysical causation must inject net energy is asserted too strongly, neglecting redistribution, constraint, and nonenergetic causal models while underdefending the closed-system premise.
  • Necessary supervenience is presented as explaining brain–mind pairing, but declaring the relation necessary may simply redescribe its invariance unless Watkins explains why those particular dependencies obtain.
