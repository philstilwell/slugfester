Overall assessment

Dr Jonathan McLatchie — Historical resurrection case: 78/100

Published strengths

  • Established a clearly relevant early-proclamation route through Paul, Peter, James, and the Twelve.
  • Presented a coherent case-specific method for assessing extraordinary testimony instead of rejecting unusual claims by category.
  • Correctly explained that cumulative evidence can in principle overcome a low prior and that probability shifts when genuine alternatives lose support.
  • Used reported witness conduct to target knowing fabrication while acknowledging that sincere believers may accept severe costs.

Published weaknesses

  • The case repeatedly relied on disputed narrative characteristics before independently establishing their reliability, weakening its comparison with legend, error, and transmission-based alternatives.
  • Source interconnections and mundane confirmations were not adequately bridged to independence or resurrection-specific truth.
  • The alternative set remained too narrow, leaving legend, mixed causes, dependence, memory distortion, and inaccurate transmission insufficiently modeled.

Matt Dillahunty — Skeptical historical standard: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Distinguished explaining reported events from explaining how the resurrection story formed, preserving legend as an upstream alternative.
  • Separated mundane narrative agreement from both source independence and the truth of resurrection.
  • Clarified that sufficient evidence could warrant belief without identifying a cause, defeating the claim of a priori supernatural exclusion.
  • Maintained cross-claim consistency pressure and emphasized the limited testability of the surviving source chain.

Published weaknesses

  • Several source objections used categorical descriptions such as anonymous, altered, or wholly unsupported where source-specific distinctions were required.
  • The preferred standard sometimes leaned too heavily on modern forensic features without fully explaining how legitimate ancient historical inference should proceed.
  • Preserving ordinary alternatives as live did not always supply a developed positive account of the reports' formation.

Reported Encounters and Competing Explanations

19:35–49:53

Dr Jonathan McLatchie — section score 76/100

Reported prolonged, collective, and multisensory appearances would be difficult to explain through ordinary misidentification, sincere error, or hallucination alone.

Strongest feature: The argument correctly observes that prolonged, collective, and multisensory encounter reports would, if historically dependable, resist explanations tailored to brief confusion, solitary perception, or routine misidentification. Principal limitation: It moves from characteristics inside the narratives to broad inadequacy claims about error or hallucination without independently establishing that those characteristics accurately describe the originating experiences. Live burden: The pro case must first justify source reliability and then compare psychologically, socially, and transmissionally realistic alternatives, including expectation, memory distortion, shared interpretation, and later elaboration. Locked score: The mixed placement recognizes direct engagement with a central alternative and an intelligible comparative inference while preserving the substantial calibration and evidential deficiencies created by treating disputed narrative features as established observations.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 88/100

Strongest feature: The reply diagnoses a foundational sequencing problem: legend may explain how the reported events entered the tradition, so those events cannot automatically become fixed data for downstream explanation. Principal limitation: Preserving legend as a live possibility does not independently demonstrate a specific developmental pathway, chronology, mechanism, or comparative likelihood for the surviving resurrection reports. Live burden: The skeptical case should connect identifiable transmission conditions to plausible story formation while the pro case must establish reliability before using detailed encounter features to constrain alternatives. Locked score: The very strong placement reflects exceptional diagnostic relevance, careful calibration, conceptual precision, and decisive contact with the pro framework, while appropriately stopping short of treating the availability of legend as affirmative proof that legendary development occurred.

Testimonial Standards and Comparative Consistency

34:44–116:31

Dr Jonathan McLatchie — section score 80/100

Extraordinary reports should be tested case by case for trustworthiness, deception, error, alternative experiences, and prior probability rather than rejected by content alone.

Strongest feature: The reply articulates a sensible case-specific method incorporating witness trustworthiness, deception, honest mistake, dreamlike experience, and prior probability instead of rejecting testimony merely for unusual content. Principal limitation: Naming appropriate assessment factors does not demonstrate that the Christian sources satisfy them, particularly where witness identification, examination, independence, and transmission remain contested. Live burden: The pro case must operationalize each criterion against the actual documents and compare competing explanations under the same evidential discipline demanded for non-Christian claims. Locked score: The strong placement reflects conceptual clarity, methodological fairness, good calibration, and direct contact with categorical skepticism while preserving the crucial gap between proposing a reasonable evaluative framework and successfully applying that framework to establish strong resurrection evidence.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 76/100

A standard accepting uninvestigable ancient testimony as strong evidence risks admitting comparably weak supernatural or fraudulent claims without consistent discrimination.

Strongest feature: The argument introduces a valuable consistency test, requiring any testimonial standard used for resurrection to discriminate responsibly among analogous extraordinary, supernatural, and potentially fraudulent claims. Principal limitation: It presents physical evidence, questioning, cross-examination, and demonstrated impartiality as paradigms of strong evidence without sufficiently defending their necessity for ancient historical inquiry. Live burden: The skeptical standard must accommodate legitimate source criticism where modern investigation is impossible while showing that more permissive historical criteria would generate unacceptable or inconsistent cross-claim results. Locked score: The strong placement recognizes clear methodological relevance, coherent comparative pressure, and a materially important investigability concern while preserving the qualification that modern forensic conditions cannot simply become universal prerequisites for warranted historical conclusions.
