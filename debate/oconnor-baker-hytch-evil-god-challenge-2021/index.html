Overall assessment

Alex O'Connor — Evil-god symmetry challenge: 84/100

Published strengths

  • Separates evidence for a designer from evidence of loving intentions, forcing the defense to supply a genuinely character-sensitive connection.
  • Applies competing noncreation motives to benevolence as well as malice, directly challenging unequal confidence in the psychology of creation.
  • Concedes the divine-command consequence while preserving the distinct malice question, and gives a clear reversal of the free-will defense.

Published weaknesses

  • Moves from conceivable conscious-victim motives toward parity without fully establishing how competing malicious and benevolent psychologies should be compared.
  • Treats resistance to evil inclinations as potentially superior character without resolving the distinction between overcoming imperfection and possessing maximal goodness.
  • The late cruel-mixture example leaves aggregate value and the requested positive mirror insufficiently addressed after the defender narrows his comparison.

Max Baker-Hytch — Divine-goodness asymmetry defense: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Distinguishes the limited inconsistency challenge from ambitious parity and grants broad theodicy reversibility, keeping disagreement focused on independent asymmetries.
  • Separates objective moral evil from malicious character and accurately identifies what the divine-command concession establishes without personally endorsing every theory.
  • Clarifies that torture need not be worst possible and restricts his final comparison to a selected middle-spectrum world rather than every mixture.

Published weaknesses

  • Offers self-hatred and immediate destruction as competing psychologies without establishing their comparative weight or applying equal scrutiny to benevolent creation.
  • The cumulative metaphysical strategy lacks defended weights for rival theories, while intrinsic existential goodness and universal privation remain insufficiently supported.
  • Acknowledges having no developed response to the character-based reframing, leaving conceptual goodness disconnected from assurance that the creator is loving.
