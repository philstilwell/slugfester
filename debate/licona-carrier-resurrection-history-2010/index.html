Overall assessment

Mike Licona — Resurrection hypothesis: 80/100

Published strengths

  • Established a coherent explanatory framework and consistently connected testimony, bodily belief, cumulative evidence, and source analysis to the resurrection inference.
  • Directly answered naturalistic challenges through the intentional-action distinction, phenomenological mismatch argument, and the concession that disappearance alone proves nothing extraordinary.
  • Handled Gospel variation with useful precision by conceding embellishment and unresolved contradictions while resisting an unjustified whole-source dismissal.
  • Preserved important qualifications concerning martyrdom evidence and the limited conclusion that costly conduct principally supports sincere belief.

Published weaknesses

  • The central explanatory-fit claim was stated with substantially greater confidence than the developed comparison of priors and multi-mechanism natural alternatives warranted.
  • The Pauline case insufficiently separated early belief content, sparse appearance reports, and historically established physical encounters.

Richard Carrier — Natural explanation: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Made antecedent probability a governing issue and precisely showed that a unique intentional act still depends on an agent's existence and specific purpose.
  • Supplied several natural pathways, including religious visions, charismatic suggestion, restricted movement dynamics, and belief maintenance without grave verification.
  • Granted probable sincerity while demonstrating that costly conviction does not discriminate between veridical resurrection and sincere mistake.
  • Challenged reliance on later Gospel phenomenology and empty-grave detail through multiple source-critical considerations.

Published weaknesses

  • The restricted-disclosure argument relied too confidently on an underdefended account of what a compassionate deity pursuing salvation would necessarily do.
  • The Gospel critique generalized from selected alleged problems to categorical historical unreliability without sufficiently bounding or demonstrating that inference.
  • The contagion reconstruction supplied more historical specificity than the sparse appearance traditions and unspecified empirical studies securely support.

Historical Method, Reference Classes, and Prior Probability

9:01–75:01

Mike Licona — section score 82/100

Historical explanations should be compared through explanatory scope, explanatory power, plausibility, and avoidance of ad hoc assumptions, rather than dismissed through subjective prior estimates.

Strongest feature: The proposed criteria establish a transparent comparative framework, directly organize the historical inquiry, and keep attention on how comprehensively and economically each hypothesis accommodates the identified testimony. Principal limitation: The presentation insufficiently distinguishes subjective intuitions from defensible background probabilities, so its methodological contrast risks marginalizing relevant antecedent information without demonstrating that explanatory virtues can replace it. Live burden: The affirmative must integrate scope, power, plausibility, and ad hocness with principled prior assessment, then show why that combined method favors resurrection over carefully specified natural alternatives. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects excellent burden relevance and a coherent organizing method, moderated by incomplete warrant for sidelining priors and limited engagement with their strongest objective formulation.

Richard Carrier — section score 88/100

Because resurrection lacks established background precedent, affirming that Jesus rose requires unusually strong evidence comparable to what would be demanded for other extraordinary, unsupported claims.

Strongest feature: The starship comparison directly confronts both the proposed historical method and resurrection's asserted explanatory fit by emphasizing that background expectations appropriately influence the evidential threshold. Principal limitation: The analogy leaves extraordinary evidence operationally unspecified and treats a purposive singular event too similarly to unsupported ownership, without fully considering evidence for an intentional agent. Live burden: The negative must translate low precedent into a disciplined comparative standard, incorporate possible divine agency fairly, and explain precisely how strong the surviving testimony would need to be. Locked score: The very strong band reflects incisive responsiveness, central methodological relevance, coherent probabilistic reasoning, and substantial charity, with modest restraint for an analogy somewhat stronger than its supporting demonstration.

Pauline Testimony and the Bodily Character of Resurrection

12:25–36:00

Mike Licona — section score 78/100

Early Pauline traditions report appearances to individuals and groups and support an early belief that Jesus, like future Christians, had been bodily raised.

Strongest feature: The move identifies the earliest central evidential material, connects individual and group appearance traditions with resurrection belief, and directly supplies the affirmative route's indispensable testimonial foundation. Principal limitation: It moves too readily among reported teaching, communal belief, and established occurrence, while the quoted Pauline basis alone does not document detailed transformed-corpse encounters with comparable certainty. Live burden: The affirmative must separate what Paul attests, what his vocabulary reasonably implies, and what later narratives add, while establishing that bodily belief arose from physical encounters rather than visionary interpretation. Locked score: The strong/competent band recognizes substantial relevance, recoverable claims, and historically important evidence, while reflecting materially overstated calibration, sparse textual demonstration, and insufficient differentiation between belief content and event phenomenology.

Richard Carrier — section score 82/100

Pauline communities display ecstatic and visionary practices, while Paul's sparse appearance references do not independently establish detailed bodily encounters and remain compatible with religious hallucinations.

Strongest feature: The reply directly contests the physical-encounter interpretation of the earliest evidence and supplies a culturally situated visionary alternative, thereby contacting two central affirmative claims simultaneously. Principal limitation: It uses hallucination broadly across distinguishable ecstatic phenomena and invokes scientific studies without representative details, leaving the inference from general vision-proneness to these particular appearances underdemonstrated. Live burden: The negative must identify relevant Pauline passages, distinguish hallucination from other altered experiences, and establish why their frequency and characteristics make the reported appearances more likely visionary than physical. Locked score: The strong/competent band recognizes high responsiveness, strong relevance, and a plausible alternative interpretation, while withholding stronger endorsement because textual documentation, empirical specificity, and case-level causal linkage remain incomplete.

Visionary, Psychological, and Social-Contagion Explanations

22:21–99:37

Mike Licona — section score 79/100

Resurrection offers a unified explanation for reported appearances to individuals, groups, followers, and opponents, purportedly covering the evidence without strained accommodation or improper auxiliary assumptions.

Strongest feature: The move reaches the motion directly by applying explanatory scope and power to diverse appearance reports and presenting one unified causal account rather than disconnected mechanisms. Principal limitation: Declaring that resurrection could not fit better substantially outruns the demonstrated comparison, because neither antecedent plausibility nor a developed multi-mechanism natural alternative receives sustained evaluation. Live burden: The affirmative must specify the evidence requiring explanation, compare predictive expectations under resurrection and natural competitors, and justify divine agency without treating unified coverage itself as decisive superiority. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects exceptional motion relevance and a clear explanatory proposal, tempered by assertive calibration, comparatively thin evidential warrant, and incomplete charitable treatment of the strongest rival reconstruction.

Richard Carrier — section score 81/100

Appearances restricted largely to followers and one exceptional outsider fit a localized visionary movement better than the expected public disclosure of a compassionate deity pursuing universal salvation.

Strongest feature: The distributional comparison generates a genuine prediction under competing hypotheses, asking why a compassionate saving deity would reveal the event narrowly while a localized movement naturally remains restricted. Principal limitation: The argument assumes an insufficiently defended model of divine purposes and disclosure, since selective witnesses could conceivably serve intentions other than immediate universal manifestation. Live burden: The negative must justify its theological expectation independently, compare selective-witness and continuing-revelation models, and show that the observed distribution is appreciably more expected under ordinary movement dynamics. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects outstanding motion contact and a productive comparative challenge, moderated by materially overstated confidence, underdeveloped psychological evidence, and incomplete charity toward purposive explanations of restricted disclosure.

Empty Grave, Investigation, and Proclaimer Sincerity

65:39–105:16

Mike Licona — section score 78/100

A missing body alone establishes no resurrection, but its significance could increase when combined with prior predictions, miracle reputation, and sincere individual and group appearance reports.

Strongest feature: The reply explicitly concedes that ordinary disappearance proves nothing extraordinary, then reframes the evidential question cumulatively by joining bodily absence with predictions, reputation, and varied sincere testimony. Principal limitation: The argument shifts among assumed and established components without demonstrating their reliability, independence, or incremental contribution, so the cumulative package remains more enumerated than analytically measured. Live burden: The affirmative must establish each component from usable sources, address dependence and embellishment, and explain why their conjunction favors resurrection rather than several interacting ordinary causes. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects coherent cumulative reasoning, commendable qualification, strong charity, and direct threshold contact, tempered by limited evidential warrant and an unfinished account of how much the added considerations change the inference.

Richard Carrier — section score 86/100

Vision-driven believers could infer bodily resurrection without locating a grave, or preserve that inference against contrary evidence through denial, assumptions, and conspiratorial rationalization.

Strongest feature: The reply modestly demonstrates possibility rather than certainty, directly challenging the assumption that bodily-resurrection belief required an accessible grave check and identifying recognizable belief-preservation mechanisms. Principal limitation: Modern cult analogies and Matthew's narrative illustrate possible rationalization but do not establish that the earliest Christians lacked grave knowledge or actually used these specific responses. Live burden: The negative must investigate burial accessibility, distinguish initial inference from later defensive reasoning, and show how visionary belief could acquire bodily content within the relevant Jewish conceptual setting. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects matched calibration, strong coherence, excellent charity, and highly responsive treatment of verification, balanced against subsidiary burden placement and evidence supporting possibility more securely than historical actuality.

Historical Usability of the Gospel Narratives

144:08–147:53

Mike Licona — section score 86/100

Ancient literary freedom, occasional embellishment, and unresolved peripheral contradictions do not alone discredit a separately supported central account, which requires claim-specific historical assessment.

Strongest feature: The reply makes a logically careful core-versus-periphery distinction, expressly concedes embellishment and unresolved contradictions, and correctly insists that identified variations do not automatically falsify every central claim. Principal limitation: The historical core remains insufficiently delimited, and the move offers limited positive evidence showing which central elements survive the strongest examples of literary construction and external conflict. Live burden: The affirmative must define the core precisely, establish it through claim-specific and preferably independent support, and demonstrate why acknowledged narrative development does not materially affect empty-grave or embodied-appearance content. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent calibration, coherence, responsiveness, qualification, and charity, with modest restraint because the defense defeats an overbroad inference more securely than it independently proves the proposed core.

Richard Carrier — section score 76/100

External conflict, redactional embellishment, literary construction, historical implausibility, and contradictions reduce confidence that Gospel narratives reliably preserve empty-grave details and original appearance phenomenology.

Strongest feature: The move identifies several claim-relevant reliability tests and directly challenges the later narrative evidence supporting both an empty grave and ordinary-consciousness features of the appearances. Principal limitation: It moves from briefly asserted problems in selected episodes to categorical unreliability of whole documents, without demonstrating each example or bounding how literary shaping affects distinct historical claims. Live burden: The negative must substantiate external conflicts and borrowing, separate pericope-level criticism from global verdicts, and explain why the specific resurrection core lacks independent or multiply constrained support. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects substantial relevance and responsive source criticism, tempered by materially unstable scope, overstated calibration, compressed treatment of mixed historical memory, and evidence that is more catalogued than demonstrated.
