Overall assessment

Sean McDowell — Apostolic suffering supports resurrection belief: 86/100

Published strengths

  • Consistently limits suffering evidence to sincerity and anti-conspiracy support within a larger resurrection case.
  • Uses graded confidence in death traditions instead of depending on every traditional apostolic martyrdom.
  • Concedes reasonable initial skepticism and clearly separates sincere belief from the truth of a miracle.

Published weaknesses

  • The Bulls analogy illustrates collective reference but poorly matches ancient transmission, membership, and source-reliability conditions.
  • The cultural-resistance argument compresses broad population assertions, practical investigability, surviving writings, and church growth into an underdemonstrated chain.

Paulogia — The martyrdom evidence is insufficient: 86/100

Published strengths

  • Separates the definition of martyrdom from case-specific evidence about preaching, causation, recantation, and death.
  • Concedes Peter and Paul’s probable sincerity and deaths while resisting unsupported extension to the entire group.
  • Immediately retracts the nursery-rhyme label, accepts the creed’s earliness, and preserves the narrower firsthand-status objection.

Published weaknesses

  • The two-person alternative offers possible guilt and visionary pathways without demonstrating that either caused the accepted beliefs.
  • The status-motivation analogy establishes mixed motives as possible but supplies no direct evidence about apostolic motivation.
