Overall assessment

Aron Ra — Christianity poses serious dangers: 75/100

Published strengths

  • Connected abstract dangerousness to concrete mechanisms, including coercive law, treatment refusal, family rejection, climate obstruction, and interpretations used to justify atrocities.
  • Used specific scriptural commands and institutional examples, then repeatedly returned to harms suffered by identifiable people rather than debating theology alone.
  • Made useful concessions and qualifications, excluding political imperialism, acknowledging national exceptions, and admitting uncertainty about specialist Greek support.

Published weaknesses

  • Relied on vivid cases and unadjusted national comparisons without adequately establishing prevalence, denominators, confounders, or Christianity-specific causal contribution.
  • Overstated disputed generalizations about religious rule, faith, placebo effects, and parental causation beyond the documentation supplied in the assessed rounds.
  • Left Jones's aggregated beneficial evidence largely unanswered, substituting doctrinal examples, personal experience, and reasonable caution about an unseen study for comparative analysis.

Michael Jones — Christianity is not intrinsically dangerous: 82/100

Published strengths

  • Consistently separated Christian identity, textual misuse, association, and causal responsibility, giving the con case a stable answer across heterogeneous harms.
  • Brought aggregated studies, methodological criticism, historical interpretation, polling, and alternative explanations into direct contact with Aron's central evidence.
  • Won the cross-national and faith-science exchanges through precise analysis of confounders, careful covenantal distinctions, and evidence-responsive accounts of how trust is justified.

Published weaknesses

  • Sometimes narrowed the motion from dangerousness to intrinsic or necessary causation, risking an excessively demanding standard that overlooks probabilistic contribution and enablement.
  • Treated favorable religiosity meta-analyses as broadly dispositive without fully addressing construct validity, causal direction, heterogeneous subgroups, or severe minority harms.
  • Used imperfect analogies for white-supremacist comparisons and misused speech, and the final analogy did not secure Aron's concession on causal responsibility.

Scripture, History, and Coercive Power

2:23–1:04:44

Aron Ra — section score 80/100

Aron distinguishes political imperialism from genuinely theological conflict, arguing that this causal filter still leaves Christianity with a substantial record of religious violence.

Strongest feature: Aron does not simply count every death involving Christians, because his exclusion of British imperial conflict as political supplies a recognizable causal filter and improves the opening's intellectual fairness. Principal limitation: the Washington Times death comparison remains thinly documented, mixes an enormous historical category with an unspecified Muslim comparison, and never demonstrates which remaining episodes were produced by Christian teaching rather than nationalism, institutions, or opportunism. Live burden: To establish dangerousness, he still needs representative cases, denominators, and a defensible method for assigning causal responsibility when religious and political motives overlap. Locked score: The 77 rewards relevant framing, unusually explicit classification, and useful motion contact, while withholding stronger evidence-and-warrant credit because the quantitative headline and the move from selected history to Christianity generally remain substantially underdeveloped.

Michael Jones — section score 81/100

Jones argues that Christ fulfilled the old covenant, leaving Christians governed by commands of love rather than ancient Israel's coercive legal code.

Strongest feature: Jones attacks the prescriptive bridge in Aron's scriptural case, explaining that ancient Israelite penalties do not become contemporary Christian commands and contrasting them with new-covenant duties of love. Principal limitation: His reply establishes an internal Christian distinction but does not address whether scripture's retention of divinely authorized violence can shape interpretation, political imagination, or claimed exceptions after the legal code becomes formally obsolete. Live burden: Jones must show not only doctrinal supersession but why recurrent Christian appeals to these passages are sufficiently aberrant that their foreseeable effects should not count against the tradition. Locked score: The 86 reflects excellent responsiveness, covenantal structure, and strong burden contact, moderated because the argument defeats present prescription more decisively than Aron's broader theory of scriptural danger.

Health, Family, and Social Harm

18:22–1:25:36

Aron Ra — section score 79/100

Aron argues that Christian authority spreads preventable medical harm through anti-condom misinformation, anti-vaccination teaching, and advice followed by a measles outbreak.

Strongest feature: Aron provides a concrete behavioral pathway from trusted religious authority through false prevention advice to increased exposure, with condom misinformation, vaccination resistance, and a reported measles outbreak making the public-health risk readily intelligible. Principal limitation: The examples are asserted with limited documentation, combine different Christian actors and health controversies, and do not establish prevalence, countervailing institutional conduct, or whether theology rather than ordinary misinformation produced the outcomes. Live burden: He needs reliable sourcing and comparative evidence showing that Christian authority systematically increases medically dangerous behavior rather than occasionally transmitting errors also found in secular communities. Locked score: The 78 recognizes coherent mechanism, specificity, and strong relevance, while evidential and calibration limits prevent vivid adverse cases from carrying an unsupported general conclusion about Christianity across populations and institutions.

Michael Jones — section score 77/100

Jones answers isolated harms with meta-analyses associating intrinsic religiosity with lower crime and substance use, better health, prosociality, and psychological benefits.

Strongest feature: Jones replaces memorable anecdotes with aggregated research, names multiple outcome domains, and explains how meta-analysis identifies broader patterns across varied studies. Principal limitation: Most cited associations concern intrinsic religiosity rather than Christianity specifically; favorable correlation does not establish causation; and study quality, effect magnitude, publication bias, adverse subgroups, and the rapid inventory's details remain largely unexamined. Live burden: He must connect the measured construct to the motion, compare reported benefits with severe minority harms, and show that confounding or selection does not generate the favorable pattern. Locked score: The 84 rewards the debate's strongest positive empirical counterweight, broad evidential coverage, and direct pressure on Aron's generalizations, while reserving higher credit because synthesis breadth alone cannot resolve construct validity or causal attribution.

Cross-National Outcomes and Climate Policy

23:18–1:19:15

Aron Ra — section score 76/100

Aron cites cross-country patterns linking lower religiosity with peace, equality, prosperity, rights, and health, while expressly acknowledging China and Vietnam as exceptions.

Strongest feature: Aron presents multiple observable social outcomes rather than relying only on moral intuition, and his explicit acknowledgment of China and Vietnam as exceptions usefully calibrates the claim as a tendency rather than a universal law. Principal limitation: Country-level religiosity is entangled with wealth, governance, education, conflict history, regional culture, and measurement choices, so the unadjusted association cannot identify Christianity as the cause or distinguish individual from national effects. Live burden: He needs multivariable analysis, clear comparison rules, and evidence that the association survives plausible confounders while remaining specific to the motion's Christian target. Locked score: The 75 credits breadth, relevance, and qualified wording, but the central ecological inference—moving from national averages to religious causation—remains insufficiently defended against the strongest live methodological objection.

Michael Jones — section score 87/100

Jones argues that national comparisons confound many influences, require multivariable individual analysis, and cannot override evidence that more religious individuals offend less.

Strongest feature: Jones pinpoints the ecological and confounding problems in Aron's national comparison, explicitly demands multivariable individual-level analysis, and offers crime findings that illustrate how national and individual associations can diverge. Principal limitation: The analogy to white-supremacist reasoning is rhetorically distracting, and citing favorable individual correlations does not itself prove causation, represent every social outcome, or establish that intrinsic religiosity maps cleanly onto Christianity. Live burden: He should specify the decisive covariates, explain the individual-level studies' design and effect sizes, and compare like outcomes rather than treating one crime association as a universal rebuttal. Locked score: The 88 reflects exceptional responsiveness, methodological precision, and direct engagement with the inference's weakest bridge, while modest reservations remain about the counterevidence's own causal limits and construct specificity.

Faith, Science, and Evidence

20:21–1:14:33

Aron Ra — section score 73/100

Aron defines faith as authority-bound belief resistant to scientific correction, linking supernatural commitments and rejected scientific conclusions to impaired reasoning.

Strongest feature: Aron offers a genuine epistemic mechanism: commitments protected from contrary evidence can prevent correction, and his examples of rejected scientific conclusions make the proposed danger more concrete than a bare definitional dispute. Principal limitation: He treats faith, Christianity, supernatural belief, fallacious reasoning, and science rejection as nearly interchangeable, without establishing that biblical faith requires evidence resistance or that representative Christians cannot hold commitments as defeasible trust. Live burden: He needs a stable definition, authoritative textual support, prevalence evidence, and a demonstrated connection between that form of faith and consequential reasoning failures. Locked score: The 69 preserves credit for relevance, an intelligible correction-failure pathway, and several recognizable examples, but conceptual conflation, thin documentation, and uncharitable generalization weaken logical precision, warrant, and calibration at the argument's central bridge.

Michael Jones — section score 85/100

Jones argues that religion-intelligence correlations weaken, disappear, or reverse across periods and cultures, fitting social conformity better than religious causation.

Strongest feature: Jones engages the research at several levels—its explicit causal limitation, methodological criticism, declining recent effects, and cross-cultural reversal—then offers conformity to local norms as a coherent alternative explanation. Principal limitation: Intelligence correlations are only one indicator of Aron's broader claim about evidence-resistant reasoning, and Jones's compressed survey of criticisms does not provide effect sizes, study quality, or enough detail to compare the conformity hypothesis against other explanations. Live burden: He must show that the qualifications materially undermine the relevant cognitive-harm mechanism, not merely that one psychometric association is context-sensitive and noncausal. Locked score: The 87 rewards excellent responsiveness, calibration, and explanatory competition grounded in research limitations, while the remaining gap reflects incomplete coverage of Aron's wider examples involving evolution, supernatural commitments, and resistance to scientific evidence.

Causation, Benefits, and Interpretive Responsibility

27:48–1:27:22

Aron Ra — section score 67/100

Aron argues that subjective benefits may be placebo-like and coexist with harmful conduct, so favorable associations do not vindicate specific doctrines.

Strongest feature: Aron identifies a legitimate relevance gap: subjective well-being can coexist with false belief or harmful behavior, and broad beneficial associations do not automatically answer dangers attributed to particular doctrines. Principal limitation: Calling the findings placebo effects is unsupported, the response does not engage Jones's crime, substance-use, health, and prosocial outcomes as more than feelings, and listing doctrines does not demonstrate their harmful consequences. Live burden: Aron needs evidence that the measured benefits are merely interpretive artifacts and separate causal studies connecting scapegoating, original sin, or flood belief to dangerous conduct. Locked score: The 62 retains credit for distinguishing truth from benefit and general outcomes from doctrine-specific harms, but weak warrant, partial responsiveness, and dismissive characterization leave the meta-analytic evidence substantially unanswered rather than successfully rebutted.

Michael Jones — section score 80/100

Jones formalizes the missing causal steps between Christian identity and harmful action, arguing that examples and associations alone cannot establish doctrinal responsibility.

Strongest feature: Jones reconstructs Aron's inference into distinct steps and repeatedly separates actor identity, association, shared interpretation, and doctrinal causation, giving the con case a clear standard that applies across several opening examples. Principal limitation: The demand sometimes approaches necessary or intrinsic causation when the ordinary motion could be satisfied by Christianity materially increasing risk, and the atheism-autism analogy illustrates attribution error without matching Aron's proposed textual and institutional mechanisms. Live burden: Jones must state an appropriately comparative causal threshold and address evidence of contribution, enablement, or predictable amplification rather than requiring Christianity to be the sole indispensable cause. Locked score: The 81 rewards coherence, broad responsiveness, and disciplined burden analysis, with reservations for potentially narrowing the motion and for treating heterogeneous harm claims through one generalized causal objection.
