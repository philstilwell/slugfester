Overall assessment

Nathan Hawkins — Libertarian freedom: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Hawkins consistently accepted biological and social constraints, integrating them into live-option limits and a degree-sensitive account of responsibility.
  • The cumulative self-formation argument supplied a concrete mechanism by which repeated choices and learning can reshape capacities and enlarge later possibilities.
  • His constrained-indeterminacy model directly rejected the crude equation of indeterminism with reasonless chaos and proposed deliberative evaluation within probabilistic limits.
  • The Libet reply appropriately narrowed what readiness-potential evidence can establish and offered a competing explanation for the observed temporal pattern.

Published weaknesses

  • The central mechanism never fully explained how agent-owned criteria author one particular stochastic outcome rather than merely constraining the possibilities among which chance resolves.
  • Phenomenological and evolutionary usefulness were repeatedly pressed toward metaphysical openness without adequately comparing deterministic counterfactual representation as a rival explanation.

Joe Folley — Compatibilist agency: 89/100

Published strengths

  • Folley established a modest but substantive compatibilist standard by identifying important notions of freedom and willing that survive determinism.
  • The embodied-causation reply clearly showed why neural realization does not automatically replace the person as the proximal causal source.
  • His formative-constraint cases directly pressured libertarian sourcehood by tracing criteria and accessible possibilities to genetics, upbringing, and restrictive environments.
  • The live-option challenge precisely identified an unresolved criterion and tested it against hard cases rather than asserting that no answer was possible.
  • The final choice-distinction reply directly showed that determinism need not erase every intelligible difference between choosing and nonchoosing conduct.

Published weaknesses

  • The practice-based reconstruction of responsibility remained normatively incomplete because it did not specify how defective reactive attitudes or inherited practices should be identified and revised.
  • The stochastic-authorship objection isolated a serious gap but left its decisive sense of randomness and its positive authorship standard insufficiently developed.

Principal limitation: Phenomenology establishes how agency appears from within more readily than it establishes metaphysical indeterminacy, and the inference remains insufficiently differentiated from deterministic counterfactual representation. Live burden: The position must explain why first-person openness reliably tracks categorical possibilities rather than uncertainty about a determined outcome, while preserving its appropriately defeasible formulation. Principal limitation: Showing that readiness activity is not invariably sufficient for deciding or moving does not exclude its operating as one probabilistic, contributory, or context-dependent cause. Indeterminism, Randomness, and Agential Selection

25:16–57:22

Nathan Hawkins — section score 83/100

Indeterminism can involve constrained probability distributions rather than reasonless chaos, with deliberation evaluating and selecting among the resulting possibilities.

Strongest feature: The move usefully separates probabilistic indeterminacy from reasonless chaos and embeds variation within deliberative constraints, directly answering the elementary determined-or-random dilemma. Principal limitation: Constraining and evaluating possible outcomes does not yet explain how the particular stochastic resolution is authored by the agent rather than merely occurring within agent-owned parameters. Live burden: The model must specify a selection relation that preserves rational explanation and genuine agent contribution without allowing prior criteria to determine the result or chance to complete it. Locked score: The strong band reflects coherent possibility-level reasoning, central relevance, explicit qualification, and a serious response to randomness, while retaining substantial pressure on the unresolved mechanism of outcome authorship.

Joe Folley — section score 83/100

Weighted stochastic choice may remain random in the authorship-relevant sense when antecedent criteria merely constrain which result can occur.

Strongest feature: The objection grants rational weighting and constrained variation, then isolates the remaining authorship question instead of caricaturing indeterminism as wholly chaotic or reasonless. Principal limitation: The phrase identifying randomness in an authorship-relevant sense remains underdeveloped, leaving unclear which property of stochastic resolution defeats agency after deliberative constraints operate. Live burden: The challenge must articulate a principled authorship condition and explain why identification of the agent with the criterial system cannot satisfy it without covertly demanding deterministic control. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, representational charity, and central diagnostic value, while its tentative presentation, conceptual rather than evidential support, and partly unstable randomness standard limit completion.

Sourcehood, Character, and Formative Constraints

16:26–44:06

Nathan Hawkins — section score 84/100

If the self is the brain's valitional executive system, the agent participates in applying the criteria that constrain indeterminate choice.

Strongest feature: The reply offers a naturalistic identification of the choosing self with an executive valitional system, showing how agent-level criteria can belong to the embodied person. Principal limitation: Ownership and application of constraints do not establish authorship of whichever permissible outcome materializes, so the decisive residual-selection challenge remains substantially unanswered. Live burden: The account must explain how the valitional system contributes specifically to one undetermined result beyond defining probabilities, admissibility conditions, or evaluative rankings inherited from prior formation. Locked score: The strong band recognizes direct contact, recoverable physicalist compatibility, and a plausible system-level proposal, but reflects limited specification of the self, modest evidential support, and incomplete representation of the strongest objection.

Joe Folley — section score 91/100

Under monism or physicalism, neural states belong to the embodied person, so brain-level causation does not automatically displace personal agency or sourcehood.

Strongest feature: The part-whole analysis directly defeats a simplistic brain-versus-person contrast by explaining why neural realization can constitute, rather than replace, causation by an embodied agent. Principal limitation: Establishing that brain states belong to the person does not independently establish that conscious deliberation is efficacious, reasons-responsive, or the relevant source of action. Live burden: The compatibilist account must supplement personal identity with a causal explanation linking deliberation, character, and executive processing to conduct without merely redescribing neural determination. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise conditional reasoning, decisive contact with the displacement inference, excellent calibration, and substantial charity, while preserving the unresolved distinction between embodied ownership and conscious control.
