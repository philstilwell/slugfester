Overall assessment

Frank Turek — Moral argument: 76/100

Published strengths

  • Carefully limited the moral argument's conclusion to a generic divine moral ground rather than claiming that morality alone establishes Christianity.
  • Correctly distinguished disagreement about moral reality from the separate ontological question whether such a reality exists.
  • Charitably accepted that bare nonbelief carries no positive explanatory burden while assigning burdens to additional affirmative naturalistic claims.
  • Pressed substantive questions about evolutionary cognitive reliability, logical normativity, and the evidential standing of immediate moral obligation.

Published weaknesses

  • The foundational move identified goodness with God's nature without independently defending that identity, leaving the divine-grounding bridge under-supported.
  • The evolutionary defeater repeatedly expanded causal influence into global unreliability without addressing truth-tracking, correction, or domain-specific reliability.
  • The claim that comparative improvement implies a unique best and then a divine ontological ground compressed several unsupported transitions.

Alex O'Connor — Subjective morality: 87/100

Published strengths

  • Separated causal explanations of moral experience from justification of moral truth with exceptional clarity and disciplined scope.
  • Conceded the explanatory significance of demonstrated objective morality while exposing the assumed value premise in prosperity-based scientific accounts.
  • Explained how evolved dispositions can generate conditional practical rules without claiming that agreement creates mind-independent morality.
  • Distinguished psychological certainty from objectivity and showed why cultural variation undercuts any simple inference from compelling experience.
  • Consistently rejected emotional revulsion as a substitute for evidence, applying that principle symmetrically across worldviews.

Published weaknesses

  • The burden clarification did not answer the earlier privation and divine-grounding inference, limiting its immediate responsiveness.
  • Calling reason subjective and conditional moral rules objective blurred premise dependence, logical validity, and mind-independent normativity.
  • The circularity objection to divine grounding assumed too quickly that the proponent possessed no independent route to God.

Objective Moral Obligation and Divine Grounding

7:17–38:21

Frank Turek — section score 78/100

Evil exists parasitically as a lack within good; because goodness is God's nature, recognizing evil presupposes an ultimate divine standard of goodness.

Strongest feature: The privation analogy intelligibly explains evil as corruption rather than an independently existing substance, directly connecting experienced wrongdoing with the debate's central demand for an ultimate standard. Principal limitation: The reasoning identifies goodness with God's nature without independently establishing that identity, so the illustrative rust comparison cannot substantiate the decisive metaphysical transition from goodness generally to divine goodness. Live burden: The argument must distinguish conceptual dependence on good from ontological dependence on God and demonstrate why secular moral realism, natural properties, or irreducible normative facts cannot satisfy that explanatory requirement. Locked score: The mixed band appropriately recognizes exceptional motion relevance and a recoverable constructive structure while registering materially overstated necessity, unstable terminology, absent qualification, and insufficient independent warrant for the divine-grounding premise.

Alex O'Connor — section score 89/100

If objective morality were demonstrated, naturalism would need further explanation; showing that conduct produces prosperity does not prove prosperity itself morally good.

Strongest feature: The reply combines a meaningful evidential concession with a precise diagnosis of the gap between objectively measurable social consequences and the unproved evaluative premise that prosperity is morally good. Principal limitation: Criticizing one scientific proposal does not establish subjectivism, exhaust naturalistic moral realism, or demonstrate that every nondriven account of objective normativity contains the same assumption. Live burden: The con case must preserve this causal-normative distinction while explaining its own moral vocabulary and clarifying whether it merely suspends judgment about objectivity or positively locates value within attitudes. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional calibration, stable scope, direct responsiveness, and a well-supported assumption-gap objection, while appropriately stopping short of treating the failure of a particular proposal as comprehensive evidence against objective morality.

Evolutionary and Psychological Explanation of Moral Experience

25:03–47:23

Frank Turek — section score 73/100

If evolution explains every thought, it threatens trust in every belief; obvious moral knowledge should not be dismissed merely to preserve an evolutionary account.

Strongest feature: The reply identifies an important self-referential reliability question for any evolutionary explanation that purports to encompass cognition generally, and it states the alleged defeating consequence with unmistakable dialectical directness. Principal limitation: Evolutionary causation is treated as automatically hostile to truth without considering selection for reliably successful representation, cultural correction, domain-specific error, or independent methods for evaluating cognitive performance. Live burden: The pro case must establish a principled connection between fitness-directed origins and epistemic unreliability, distinguish moral cognition from cognition generally, and avoid treating the vivid certainty of a moral judgment as validation of its objective status. Locked score: The mixed band recognizes diagnostic responsiveness and central relevance while reflecting materially overstated necessity, unstable scope, missing qualification, and inadequate support for the proposed global defeater.

Alex O'Connor — section score 91/100

Evolution supplies survival-oriented dispositions from which conditionally objective behavioral rules follow once well-being is adopted, although consensus cannot create mind-independent morality.

Strongest feature: The move offers a concrete evolutionary explanation for shared dispositions while limiting behavioral rules to survival or well-being objectives and denying that unanimity establishes mind independence. Principal limitation: Calling goal-relative conclusions objective destabilizes the central terminology, because conditionally valid guidance remains different from categorical moral truth and objectivity independent of valuing consciousness. Live burden: The account must explain why ends possess authority, how conflicts among survival, flourishing, and preference are resolved, and whether conditional prescriptions merit specifically moral rather than merely instrumental standing. Locked score: The very strong band recognizes explanatory possibility, substantial motion contact, and explicit qualifications, while terminological instability and incomplete normative grounding prevent the evolutionary account from resolving the ontological dispute.

Reason, Logic, and Evolutionary Reliability

29:00–32:47

Frank Turek — section score 75/100

Assuming evolved reason does not explain its trustworthiness, while objective and immaterial logical laws remain unexplained if natural reality consists only of material objects.

Strongest feature: The reply distinguishes presupposing reason from explaining its trustworthiness and raises an ontological question about logical laws, contacting epistemic reliability and naturalistic grounding with interrogative force. Principal limitation: Calling logical laws nonmolecular does not establish their need for supernatural ontological explanation, while leaving unspecified which version of naturalism confronts the objection. Live burden: Clarify whether logical laws are propositions, relations, abstractions, or norms, demonstrate why nominalist, conceptualist, structural, or realist naturalistic treatments are inadequate, and explain why divine cognition provides a superior account. Locked score: The strong band recognizes excellent responsiveness, relevance, and proportionate questioning, moderated by partly unstable terminology, implicit qualification, and no developed inference from nonmateriality to a divine ground.

Alex O'Connor — section score 83/100

Reason cannot be certified without using reason, yet inquiry necessarily begins by assuming reason, existence, and an external world rather than proving them noncircularly.

Strongest feature: The reply candidly acknowledges that consensus cannot prove reason true and presents rational practice as an unavoidable shared starting point, avoiding a pretended noncircular validation that the position cannot provide. Principal limitation: Practical indispensability alone does not answer the deeper explanatory challenge about why evolved reasoning is generally reliable, nor does comparison with existence or an external world establish parity among these assumptions. Live burden: The account must distinguish entitlement to employ reason from a causal explanation of its reliability and show how error detection, prediction, and intersubjective correction provide warrant beyond unavoidable reliance. Locked score: The very strong band reflects a direct, stable, explicitly qualified, and appropriately calibrated answer to the justificatory demand, while leaving the proponent's separate ontological and evolutionary reliability questions only partially addressed.

Moral Disagreement, Cultural Variation, and Practical Judgment

35:38–50:04

Frank Turek — section score 73/100

Calling one society better than another supposedly implies an objective best beyond preference, prompting the question of what ontologically grounds that ultimate standard.

Strongest feature: The reply recognizes that comparative moral language can conceal evaluative criteria and properly asks what makes social improvement more than the satisfaction of presently shared preferences. Principal limitation: It moves from better to a unique best, then from that superlative to an objective standard and finally to an external ontological ground, treating distinct comparative and metaphysical concepts as interchangeable without argument. Live burden: The pro case must exclude plural goods, incomplete orderings, local comparisons, and criterion-relative improvement before inferring an absolute optimum, then independently connect any objective standard to God. Locked score: The weak band reflects meaningful relevance and a recoverable challenge but appropriately records material terminological instability, missing qualification, overstated necessity, limited responsiveness to the causal-normative distinction, and inadequate warrant for each transition in the inference.

Alex O'Connor — section score 90/100

Racism's wrongness may feel as instinctively certain as elementary arithmetic while remaining culturally contingent, because psychological compulsion does not establish mind-independent truth.

Strongest feature: The comparison carefully separates the psychological force of a judgment from its objective status, using historical variation to demonstrate that confidence and cultural convergence cannot independently settle metaethical reality. Principal limitation: Cultural contingency shows that moral intuitions vary and may be unreliable, but it neither proves subjectivism nor explains why some judgments improve through criticism, information, or widening consideration. Live burden: The con case must provide criteria for distinguishing distortion from justified revision, clarify whether variation is merely underdetermining evidence or positive evidence against objectivity, and preserve grounds for condemning entrenched injustice. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise terminology, stable scope, explicit qualification, direct responsiveness, and excellent calibration, while appropriately recognizing that the example blocks one inference without establishing a complete subjective metaethics.
