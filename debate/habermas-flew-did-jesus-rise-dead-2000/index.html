Overall assessment

Gary Habermas — Resurrection is the best explanation: 87/100

Published strengths

  • Established an early Pauline transmission argument linked, with qualification, to Peter and James.
  • Presented a cumulative bodily case using seeing, touching, flesh-and-bones language, eating, and Pauline transformation language.
  • Defended crucifixion death through multiple evidential categories and directly challenged the fit of psychological alternatives.
  • Accepted the relevance of prior probability and explained how convergent evidence could in principle overcome an ordinary presumption of continued death.

Published weaknesses

  • The cumulative case sometimes moved too quickly from what the narratives depict to what their historical sources independently establish.
  • The hypothetical case for overcoming skeptical priors was considerably richer than the surviving ancient evidence it was meant to illuminate.
  • The claim that resurrection would authenticate Jesus within Mosaic theism left divine intention and alternative interpretations insufficiently defended.

Antony Flew — Resurrection is not established: 86/100

Published strengths

  • Distinguished sincere appearance experiences from the inference to an externally present resurrected body.
  • Pressed precise source, independence, chronology, and eyewitness limitations underlying the historical core.
  • Identified the textual ambiguity between Thomas being invited to touch and actually completing the contact.
  • Defended historical restraint without pretending to possess a complete natural reconstruction, and required agent-specific theological warrant for miracle attribution.

Published weaknesses

  • The grief-vision account did not adequately cover Paul, James, groups, or the diversity of reported circumstances.
  • The spiritual-body objection treated spiritual and immaterial as equivalent without establishing that Pauline usage supported the equation.
  • Body-removal possibilities preserved underdetermination but were not developed enough to establish comparative historical credibility.

Historical Sources and Early Tradition

6:28–10:45

Gary Habermas — section score 88/100

The tradition preserved in First Corinthians 15 is early, was received and transmitted by Paul, and was probably linked through Jerusalem to Peter and James.

Strongest feature: The reply answers source-proximity concerns with a dated Pauline document, identifiable transmission terminology, and a qualified historical connection between Paul, Jerusalem, Peter, and James. Principal limitation: Early reception and eyewitness association do not independently verify the reported appearances, establish the complete tradition's original wording, or demonstrate the independence of subsequent Gospel narratives. Live burden: The affirmative must show precisely what historical content the early formula secures and why its transmission history supports accuracy beyond the comparatively modest conclusion that resurrection claims circulated early. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because the argument is specific, responsive, and responsibly qualified, while its evidential reach remains narrower than a comprehensive vindication of the resurrection narratives.

Antony Flew — section score 85/100

Agreement on proposed facts does not cure uncertain chronology, partisan documentary sources, absent Gospel eyewitnesses, or the limited independence of the surviving testimony.

Strongest feature: The reply directly interrogates the evidential foundation beneath the proposed historical core, identifying chronology, partisanship, eyewitness access, and testimonial independence as distinct reliability considerations. Principal limitation: Its reference to a documentary record risks excessive compression, and uncertainty about an exact crucifixion year contributes considerably less than the stronger questions about provenance, transmission, and corroboration. Live burden: The skeptical case must demonstrate that these limitations materially weaken the particular appearance and empty-tomb claims, rather than merely restating familiar difficulties attending nearly all ancient historical investigation. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because the response is highly relevant, multifaceted, and charitable, while its strongest force comes from source dependence and eyewitness limitations rather than chronological imprecision.

Death, Burial, and the Empty Tomb

Gary Habermas — section score 88/100

Medical considerations, broad critical acceptance, non-Christian attestation, and early Pauline preaching cumulatively support Jesus' actual death by crucifixion.

Strongest feature: The move supports the indispensable death premise through several converging categories, including medical considerations, critical historical acceptance, non-Christian references, and early Pauline proclamation. Principal limitation: The independence, dating, and exact probative contribution of the cited categories remain compressed, while broad scholarly acceptance cannot substitute entirely for presenting the underlying evidence. Live burden: The affirmative must preserve the distinction between strong support for crucifixion and the more specific conclusion of death beyond survival, while documenting how the separate sources reinforce rather than merely repeat one another. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because the proposition is narrow, cumulatively supported, and centrally relevant, with remaining limitations concerning evidential exposition rather than basic coherence.

Appearances and Psychological Alternatives

5:09–20:05

Gary Habermas — section score 86/100

The historical core includes transformative experiences the disciples understood as appearances of the risen Jesus, alongside the notable conversions of James and Paul.

Strongest feature: The move carefully identifies transformative experiences and unexpected conversions as historical data requiring explanation while preserving the distinction between what witnesses believed and what actually occurred. Principal limitation: Its evidential foundation remains comparatively compressed, because reported conviction, subsequent transformation, and proclamation do not independently establish either an external appearance or the reliability of every relevant tradition. Live burden: The affirmative case must connect these experiences to sufficiently early, independent, and bodily evidence while showing why psychological, literary, or cumulative natural explanations fit the varied witnesses less satisfactorily. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because the proposition is coherent, appropriately qualified, and centrally relevant, although its warrant depends substantially on later source analysis and comparative explanation.

Antony Flew — section score 81/100

The disciples' experiences could have been grief-related visions resembling bereavement experiences, without an externally visible risen body as their object.

Strongest feature: The reply accepts the reported experiences for argumentative purposes and supplies an intelligible ordinary mechanism that could explain sincere conviction without presupposing deception or an external resurrected body. Principal limitation: The bereavement analogy is generalized across relevant disciples without detailed subject-specific evidence, and it does not substantially address groups, Paul, James, physical-interaction reports, or differences among the alleged circumstances. Live burden: The skeptical case must explain how grief-related experience scales across the evidential variety or combine mechanisms without making the alternative increasingly conjectural and insufficiently constrained. Locked score: The supplied strong band is understandable because the hypothesis is relevant, coherent, and appropriately noncomprehensive, but its warrant and explanatory coverage remain materially thinner than its central importance requires.

Nature of the Resurrection Body

26:53–39:27

Gary Habermas — section score 91/100

Johannine seeing and touching reports, combined with Luke's flesh-and-bones language and eating scene, depict a tangible body rather than a merely ghostly manifestation.

Strongest feature: The reply broadens the bodily case beyond Thomas by combining multiple sensory features, including seeing, touching, flesh-and-bones language, denial of ghostliness, and eating. Principal limitation: It establishes what the narratives depict more readily than their historical reliability, and differences among texts, speakers, chronology, and possible literary development receive limited analysis. Live burden: The affirmative must demonstrate that these accounts preserve sufficiently early and independent observational content, not merely that their final literary forms unmistakably portray tangible embodiment. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because the response directly and cumulatively answers the bodily-evidence challenge with clear qualification, while the unresolved transition from narrative content to historical event prevents a stronger evidential conclusion.

Antony Flew — section score 84/100

The Thomas account reports an invitation to touch and a subsequent response, but does not clearly state that Thomas actually touched Jesus.

Strongest feature: The reply isolates an exact textual distinction between an invitation to touch, visual recognition, and an explicit narrative statement that bodily contact actually occurred. Principal limitation: Its narrow success concerning Thomas does not answer other Johannine touching language, Luke's flesh-and-bones description, eating reports, or the broader literary depiction of tangible embodiment. Live burden: The skeptical case must extend its source criticism beyond this omission and explain whether the remaining sensory narratives are dependent, theological, developmental, or otherwise inadequate as historical evidence. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because this is an unusually precise, well-calibrated, and charitable textual objection, even though its evidential scope is deliberately limited to one contact narrative within a much broader bodily case.

Comparative Explanation and Burden of Reconstruction

21:35–78:21

Gary Habermas — section score 86/100

Natural alternatives require separate accounts for death, disciple experiences, Paul's experience, and the empty tomb, whereas resurrection offers a unified explanation.

Strongest feature: The reply evaluates explanatory scope across distinct data classes and correctly observes that the proposed psychological accounts do not automatically explain death, Paul, the disciples, and the empty tomb together. Principal limitation: Numerical multiplication of mechanisms is not independently probative, because a composite ordinary explanation may remain more probable than a unified miraculous explanation given background assumptions and evidential dependencies. Live burden: The affirmative must compare overall likelihood, fit, simplicity, independence, and antecedent plausibility rather than allowing theoretical unity alone to determine the inference. Locked score: The supplied strong band is understandable because the comparative challenge is relevant and clearly expressed, while its warrant remains incomplete until unification is integrated with source reliability and prior probability.

Antony Flew — section score 92/100

Given extensive historical ignorance and sparse independent evidence, restraint favors a tentative natural interpretation over postulating a physical body publicly visible after death.

Strongest feature: The move proportionately converts historical ignorance into a recommendation for interpretive restraint, expressly offering a tentative conclusion rather than pretending to reconstruct a complete alternative history. Principal limitation: General scarcity alone does not determine the comparative force of the specific early, bodily, conversion, and empty-tomb considerations assembled by the affirmative case. Live burden: The skeptical position must show that concrete ambiguities and dependencies prevent those considerations from jointly distinguishing resurrection, rather than allowing background uncertainty to substitute for direct comparative analysis. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because the move is precise, calibrated, and connected directly to the motion, while its ultimate persuasiveness still depends on detailed undercutting of the cumulative evidence.

Miracle Priors and Worldview Evidence

Gary Habermas — section score 84/100

Evidence for God, contemporary anomalies, and survival after death could alter background assumptions and increase the prior plausibility of resurrection.

Strongest feature: The reply accepts the relevance of prior probability and identifies several categories of background evidence that could make divine action and postmortem survival less antecedently improbable. Principal limitation: Evidence for God, contemporary miracles, near-death experiences, and other anomalies is grouped loosely, without sufficient evaluation of independence, reliability, explanatory alternatives, or relevance to bodily resurrection. Live burden: The affirmative must establish these background claims with proportionate rigor and explain how they support a purposeful resurrection by God rather than merely generic survival or unexplained phenomena. Locked score: The supplied strong band is understandable because the conditional reasoning is coherent, relevant, and carefully modest, although the actual evidential strength of the proposed prior-shifting material remains substantially underdeveloped.

Antony Flew — section score 91/100

Rational response to alleged miracle evidence depends on antecedent beliefs, so identical reports may warrant skepticism unless unusually strong evidence changes those priors.

Strongest feature: The move correctly places miracle assessment within an updating framework, recognizing both antecedent probability and the possibility that sufficiently spectacular evidence could rationally overcome initial skepticism. Principal limitation: It leaves unspecified which prior beliefs are justified, how low the relevant prior should be, and what comparative likelihood the historical evidence has under resurrection and alternative explanations. Live burden: The skeptical case must defend its background assumptions independently and show that the actual evidence remains insufficient after updating, rather than treating personal starting suspicion as dispositive. Locked score: The supplied very strong band is understandable because the epistemic principle is coherent, explicitly qualified, and central to the motion, although applying it requires a substantially more developed comparative framework.
