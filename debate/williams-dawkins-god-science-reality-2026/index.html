Overall assessment

Rowan Williams — Christian theism: 76/100

Published strengths

  • Williams decisively answers the historical-Adam objection by distinguishing allegorical theological teaching from literal biography, achieving exceptional responsiveness while preserving compatibility between Genesis and evolution.
  • His inherited-disability account gives original sin a concrete social mechanism, replacing congenital personal guilt with culturally transmitted constraints on vision and choice.
  • The composer analogy and whole-process account consistently protect complete scientific causation while locating divine creativity at a noncompetitive level, directly answering shortcut objections.
  • Williams’s classical-simplicity clarification sharply distinguishes an uncomposed, unconditioned source from a computationally limited agent, compelling Dawkins to defend the relevance of finite-agent complexity analogies.

Published weaknesses

  • The consciousness argument moves too quickly from intentional subject matter to nonreducibility, without explaining why physical organization cannot generate content or how the gap supports theism.
  • Continuous divine sustaining remains evidentially thin and partly obscure, because nonphysical generative energy redescribes natural processes without establishing God or identifying additional explanatory work.
  • Incarnation and resurrection offer solidarity and promised healing but do not explain why beneficent authorship permits suffering-intensive evolution, leaving Dawkins’s strongest challenge substantially unanswered.

Richard Dawkins — Scientific naturalism: 77/100

Published strengths

  • Dawkins’s evolutionary-suffering argument directly compares ruthless selection with beneficent authorship, combining concrete evidence, strong logical relevance, and the debate’s most demanding burden for theism.
  • His evolved-brain account links gradually complex nervous systems to consciousness and makes postmortem survival carry the burden of supplying a mechanism or supported continuity.
  • The evolutionary-by-product model explains artistic and intellectual excess through general cognitive capacities, offering a natural mechanism without requiring separate selection for every achievement.
  • His challenge to fall and redemption grants ordinary fallibility before isolating the unsupported theological additions, producing a focused and responsive demand for further justification.

Published weaknesses

  • The historical-fall objection acknowledges allegorical interpretation yet continues as though Williams affirmed a literal first couple, damaging coherence, evidence, responsiveness, and interpretive charity.
  • The natural-composer reply explains artistic production but misses Williams’s distinction between causal mechanics and creative context, so it answers an adjacent supernatural-inference claim.
  • Dawkins calls the God hypothesis erroneous but supplies no predictions, falsifiers, comparative likelihoods, or discriminating observations, leaving his closing scientific-testability framework materially under-supported.
