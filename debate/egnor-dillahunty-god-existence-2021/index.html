Overall assessment

Michael Egnor — Thomistic theism: 71/100

Published strengths

  • Egnor presented multiple independent routes through change, contingency, natural regularity, moral objectivity, evil, and interpersonal knowledge rather than relying on one isolated consideration.
  • His morality-beyond-opinion reply clearly exposed the unresolved distinction between objective facts relative to well-being and categorical authority for selecting well-being itself.
  • The interpersonal analogy and greater-goods discussion were appropriately framed as compatibility or possibility claims, showing more calibration than several earlier metaphysical assertions.
  • The affirmative consistently connected its central constructions to the motion and maintained a recognizable cumulative explanatory strategy across the debate.

Published weaknesses

  • The central metaphysical arguments repeatedly asserted that explanatory chains must terminate and then identified the terminus as God without supplying the decisive attribute derivations.
  • The burden replies conflated withheld belief, categorical atheism, and metaphysical naturalism, thereby answering a stronger position than the opponent's stated revisable nonbelief.
  • The comparison between Thomistic reasoning and scientific abduction established a shared abstract form but not equivalent candidate constraints, predictions, or comparative evidential support.

Matt Dillahunty — Skeptical atheism: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Dillahunty most effectively isolated the transition from any first cause or necessary foundation to a timeless, intelligent, recognizably divine agent.
  • His distinction between present ignorance and positive supernatural evidence supplied a proportionate skeptical conclusion without pretending that an unknown natural answer had been established.
  • The secular well-being account demonstrated how objective physical facts can support moral assessments relative to a transparent nondivine standard.
  • His nonbelief framework was explicitly tentative, evidence-sensitive, and revisable, giving the skeptical motion-level position unusually clear calibration.
  • The causal and methodological replies directly contacted major affirmative bridges and generally preserved the limited conclusion that the offered support was insufficient.

Published weaknesses

  • The causal extrapolation objection leaned on pre-Planck temporal access and therefore did not fully engage the strongest Thomistic claim about presently sustaining dependence.
  • The rule excluding undemonstrated candidates from abduction was stated too categorically, since novel entities can acquire support through constrained explanatory success.
  • The claim that invoking God solves no problem for secular morality exceeded the narrower demonstration that disagreement and contested foundations persist.

Burden of Proof, Definition, and Rational Nonbelief

20:46–123:53

Michael Egnor — section score 64/100

Because nature allegedly contains extensive evidence of an underlying intellect, Egnor argues that atheists cannot merely dismiss it and that both sides must defend their evidential conclusions.

Strongest feature: The response directly contests asymmetric burden allocation and correctly observes that anyone asserting a substantive contrary ontology or categorical denial must support those additional commitments. Principal limitation: It treats tentative nonbelief, positive atheism, and metaphysical naturalism as interchangeable while repeatedly asserting massive evidence without identifying or defending that evidence within the response. Live burden: The affirmative needed to distinguish burdens generated by withholding, denial, and naturalistic explanation, then show that specified evidence creates a rational obligation extending beyond evaluation of the original theistic claim. Locked score: The mixed band reflects exceptional relevance and partial contact with the burden dispute, but materially overstated certainty, unstable terminology, thin evidence, limited charity, and failure to address revisable nonbelief substantially weaken the attempted symmetry.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 79/100

Those asserting God's existence retain the evidential burden, whereas atheists need not prove universal nonexistence and cannot rationally affirm an undefined or unfalsifiable deity without adequate support.

Strongest feature: The move clearly assigns evidential responsibility to the existence claimant, acknowledges the difficulty of universal disproof, and directly advances the skeptical motion-level case for withholding assent. Principal limitation: Comparing God with fairies while also saying there is no God blurs the careful distinction between lacking belief and affirmatively denying existence, and the passage does not examine any offered proof's internal premises. Live burden: The skeptical side still needed to preserve its qualified nonbelief consistently, define what evidential threshold the affirmative must meet, and demonstrate specifically why each metaphysical, natural-order, or moral inference fails that threshold. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent motion relevance, coherent burden allocation, useful qualification, and substantial charity, tempered by categorical phrasing, imperfect scope discipline, and limited responsiveness to the affirmative arguments themselves.

Thomistic Change, Causation, and Necessary Foundation

8:37–51:06

Michael Egnor — section score 72/100

Change and essentially ordered causal dependence require an actual transcendent source rather than an endless regress of merely derivative causal powers, and Egnor identifies that source as God.

Strongest feature: The argument presents a recognizable metaphysical structure, connects observable change with derivative causal activity, and directly supports the indispensable Thomistic foundation of Egnor's affirmative case. Principal limitation: Its compressed presentation interchanges possibility, actuality, causal power, and essentially ordered dependence, while neither demonstrating the relevant regress impossibility nor deriving recognizably divine attributes from the proposed terminus. Live burden: The affirmative still needed to distinguish sustaining dependence from temporal cosmology, defend why every instrumental hierarchy requires a nonderivative actualizer, and explain why that actualizer possesses intellect, agency, or other God-identifying characteristics. Locked score: The mixed band reflects substantial relevance and an intelligible argumentative outline alongside materially overstated necessity, unstable terminology, incomplete warrant, and an unsupported transition from foundational actuality to God.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 88/100

Causal relations observed within the universe do not by themselves demonstrate equivalent relations outside accessible cosmological conditions, beyond the universe, or independently of temporal processes.

Strongest feature: The reply identifies a decisive scope problem in moving from familiar intracosmic causation to inaccessible extra-universal or atemporal conditions and directly challenges both foundational metaphysical arguments. Principal limitation: Its appeal to pre-Planck inaccessibility risks construing the First Way as a temporal cosmological argument, although a Thomistic formulation may instead concern presently sustaining, essentially ordered dependence. Live burden: The skeptical side needed to engage the strongest non-temporal formulation, separate empirical extrapolation from metaphysical principle, and show why ordinary dependency provides insufficient warrant even under the affirmative's intended causal framework. Locked score: The strong band reflects exceptional responsiveness, high central relevance, clear scope discipline, and substantial evidential support, while partly unstable causal terminology, categorical accessibility language, and incomplete charity toward sustaining-cause interpretations limit the objection.

Natural Order and the Method of Extranatural Inference

11:22–72:29

Michael Egnor — section score 70/100

Stable, lawlike behavior by mindless natural objects is presented as purposive activity requiring direction from an intellect that understands the ends toward which those objects regularly act.

Strongest feature: Concrete examples of falling objects and regular atomic behavior make the natural-order thesis readily intelligible and connect observable consistency directly to the affirmative's intelligent-direction bridge. Principal limitation: The presentation moves from descriptive regularity to purposive ends and then to a directing mind without distinguishing those concepts or comparatively excluding dispositional, structural, or other nonmental explanations. Live burden: The affirmative needed to show why lawlike behavior constitutes genuine teleology, identify predictions or explanatory advantages supplied by intelligence, and establish that the proposed director is sufficiently characterized as God rather than an unspecified ordering principle. Locked score: The mixed band reflects strong relevance and clear illustrative accessibility, counterbalanced by unstable terminology, materially overstated necessity, absent qualification, undeveloped alternatives, and an evidential bridge substantially weaker than the asserted conclusion.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 87/100

Inference to the best explanation cannot select an unconstrained supernatural candidate merely because an observation remains unexplained; candidate explanations require independent definition, possibility, and comparative evidential discipline.

Strongest feature: The move directly challenges candidate admission within the natural-order inference and usefully insists that explanatory comparison requires constraints beyond an observation's current lack of explanation. Principal limitation: Its categorical rule risks circularly excluding novel theoretical entities whose evidential support initially arises through successful abduction, while the comparison with magic understates legitimate exploratory hypothesis formation. Live burden: The skeptical side needed to distinguish prior demonstration from definability, predictive constraint, comparative likelihood, and explanatory success, thereby excluding arbitrary supernatural proposals without prejudging every unfamiliar explanatory category. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, methodological relevance, stable scope, and a coherent warning against unconstrained gap filling, tempered by slightly overstated necessity, an underspecified admissibility threshold, and incomplete charity toward disciplined theological abduction.

Objective Morality and Divine Grounding

15:15–125:37

Michael Egnor — section score 76/100

Objective right and wrong are said to transcend human opinion, require existence within an intellect beyond humanity, and consequently provide strong evidence for God's divine mind.

Strongest feature: The move states a complete and easily recoverable moral argument, connecting mind-independent obligation to a divine-intellect hypothesis through a central, motion-relevant explanatory route. Principal limitation: It assumes without adequate defense that objective moral truths must exist within a mind, and it does not compare secular realism, naturalist normativity, contractual reasons, or other nondivine accounts. Live burden: The affirmative needed to establish objective categorical obligations, explain why abstract or natural moral facts are inadequate, resolve whether goodness depends on divine nature or command, and connect the resulting ground specifically to God. Locked score: The mixed band reflects conceptual clarity, stable scope, and indispensable relevance, while the unsupported mind-dependence premise, missing qualifications, limited rival comparison, and materially overstated description of the argument as very strong evidence constrain its warrant.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 83/100

Once human or sentient well-being is adopted as morality's foundation, objective physical facts can establish whether particular actions advance or frustrate that specified secular standard.

Strongest feature: The move supplies a clear secular mechanism for objective assessment, carefully making factual evaluation conditional on an identified well-being standard and illustrating it through unmistakable bodily harm. Principal limitation: Objective facts about means do not establish that every agent is categorically obligated to adopt well-being as the governing end, leaving foundation selection and normativity incompletely defended. Live burden: The skeptical side needed to justify well-being through impartial reasons, interests, agency, cooperation, or another nonarbitrary principle, while distinguishing moral objectivity from merely instrumental correctness under a chosen goal. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, stable conditional reasoning, appropriate qualification, strong evidence, and direct central relevance, tempered by the unresolved is-to-ought bridge and incomplete treatment of the affirmative's categorical-authority concern.

Divine Hiddenness, Religious Knowledge, and Evil

28:36–135:54

Michael Egnor — section score 72/100

Evil is understood as privated goodness within finite creation, while God permits moral and natural suffering for freedom, responsible agency, challenge, and character-forming greater goods.

Strongest feature: The move offers several recognizable compatibility mechanisms for divine goodness and suffering, distinguishes moral agency from natural hardship, and explicitly presents its explanation with some humility. Principal limitation: The compressed theodicy does not demonstrate that the proposed goods require actual suffering of the observed kinds or magnitudes, nor that less destructive alternatives would be unavailable to God. Live burden: The affirmative needed proportionality constraints, separate accounts of moral and natural evil, evidence connecting particular harms to greater goods, and an explanation of apparently excessive or non-character-forming suffering. Locked score: The mixed band reflects coherent possibilities, explicit qualification, direct subsidiary relevance, and acknowledged difficulty, balanced against speculative warrant, incomplete scope discipline, limited engagement with the strongest evidential problem, and insufficient justification for universal divine permission.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 71/100

A God who interacts detectably, or both wants and can produce belief, should be demonstrable; continued reasonable nonbelief therefore supports withholding assent under those stated conditions.

Strongest feature: The argument carefully limits itself to a God who interacts detectably or both desires and can secure belief, thereby presenting a coherent conditional challenge rather than universalizing hiddenness. Principal limitation: Personal continued nonbelief supplies only limited evidence without establishing representativeness, nonresistance, expected divine aims, or the inadequacy of possible reasons for permitting epistemic distance. Live burden: The skeptical case needed broader evidence of reasonable nonbelief, a clearer likelihood comparison between interactive theism and alternatives, and an answer to accounts emphasizing freedom, relationship, maturation, or noncoercive revelation. Locked score: The mixed band reflects clear scope, explicit qualification, respectable coherence, and genuine subsidiary relevance, balanced against slightly overstated evidential force, incomplete alternative comparison, and failure to contact the natural-order inference assigned as its decisive target.
