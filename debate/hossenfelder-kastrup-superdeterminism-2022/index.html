Overall assessment

Sabine Hossenfelder — Superdeterminism is a justified research route: 85/100

Published strengths

  • Hossenfelder grounded the program in Bell’s conditional tradeoff, then explained why locality’s success in general relativity makes statistical-independence violation a scientifically motivated option rather than an arbitrary escape.
  • She decisively corrected two mismatched targets: Kastrup attacked a non-hidden-variable paper and later analyzed a simplified protocol instead of the proposed same-state, noncommuting-measurement sequence.
  • Her class-versus-model distinction conceded that superdeterminism itself is not falsifiable while correctly requiring specific models to define hidden-variable properties, generate predictions, and face model-specific empirical rejection.
  • She paired candid admissions about her unfinished detector evolution law with a narrower defense of the research program, and identified an earlier paper allegedly supplying concrete temperature, timing, size, and complexity estimates.

Published weaknesses

  • Her claim that statistical-independence violation allows a local law selecting detector eigenstates described the desired mechanism functionally without supplying the law that would perform this explanatory work.
  • The detector account located hidden variables in apparatus degrees of freedom but never fully answered how remote amplification variables locally influence the microscopic interaction without merely encoding the required result.
  • She asserted that repeated noncommuting measurements and cited numerical thresholds suffice, yet the exchange never displayed predicted effect sizes, rejection rules, or protection against disturbance and drift.

Bernardo Kastrup — Superdeterminism lacks sufficient scientific justification: 82/100

Published strengths

  • Kastrup formulated the detector problem physically: a tiny interaction surface appears causally separated from broader amplification machinery, so the proposed local theory owes a precise connecting mechanism.
  • His strongest experimental reply identified a concrete sample-size and timing dilemma, where short series lack statistical power while longer series create room to attribute null results to hidden-state drift.
  • He set a carefully qualified evidential threshold, explicitly allowing superdeterminism as research while insisting that claims of natural statistical-independence violation require stronger rationale than locality preference alone.
  • His operational challenge specified exactly what an experimentalist needs—temperature, repetition rate, observables, and permitted detector degrees—thereby turning general testability concerns into answerable technical demands.

Published weaknesses

  • He claimed hidden variables had never been defined, but Hossenfelder immediately identified a relevant toy model and supplied its explicit unit-complex-disk distribution, defeating the objection’s universal scope.
  • His concrete placeholder criticism targeted a paper that did not claim to be the hidden-variable theory, substantially reducing the relevance of an otherwise specific objection to theoretical incompleteness.
  • The camera-aperture analogy blurred familiar instrument invariance with Bell’s formal statistical-independence condition, weakening the historical argument precisely where a rigorous equivalence was most needed.
