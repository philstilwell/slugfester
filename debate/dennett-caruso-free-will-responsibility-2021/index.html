Overall assessment

Daniel Dennett — Compatibilist free will is worth wanting: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Developed a concrete compatibilist account of agency through learning, reflection, moral education, reasons-responsiveness, and unimpaired self-control.
  • Distinguished deliberate interpersonal manipulation from ordinary deterministic causation by focusing on whether another person displaces the agent’s control.
  • Offered a coherent system-versus-act distinction explaining how consequentialist institutional design can coexist with rule-bound adjudication.
  • Connected sanctions to publicity, proportionality, legal stability, and impairment-sensitive exceptions rather than to metaphysical indeterminism.

Published weaknesses

  • Practical changeability answered fatalism more effectively than the skeptic’s distinct demands for ultimate sourcehood and outcome settlement.
  • The account repeatedly invoked ordinary or consequentialist desert without clearly identifying what desert adds beyond forward-looking institutional benefits.
  • The defense of moral anger relied on an intuitive adult-wrongdoing example without establishing a fittingness standard or functional advantage over alternatives.

Gregg Caruso — Basic-desert free will is unjustified: 89/100

Published strengths

  • Carefully limited skepticism to basic-desert responsibility while preserving causal attribution, deliberation, moral engagement, and practical freedom.
  • Supplied substantive non-desert grounds for coercion through self-defense, public safety, proportionality, rehabilitation, and least-restrictive intervention.
  • Granted the practical force of reasons-responsive agency and precisely isolated the remaining dispute over desert-grounding control.
  • Pressed a powerful dispensability dilemma against backward-looking desert within an account presented as consequentialist.

Published weaknesses

  • The sourcehood case did not fully specify a positive, non-question-begging condition for the additional control that basic desert would require.
  • The quarantine analogy and risk-sensitive framework left important due-process, equality, prediction, and offense-recognition safeguards underdeveloped.
  • The proposed replacement attitudes were supported mainly through a parental example and did not establish equivalent performance in serious adult cases.

Reasons-Responsive Control and the Ability to Do Otherwise

13:31–73:26

Daniel Dennett — section score 77/100

Determinism does not prevent responsibility-relevant change: people can respond to persuasion, revise conduct, develop competence, and turn their lives around through reasons and reflection.

Strongest feature: The reply vividly separates determinism from fatalism by emphasizing observable persuasion, behavioral revision, moral development, and the continuing practical significance of reasons-responsive human capacities. Principal limitation: Change across altered reasons and circumstances does not answer whether agents ultimately author themselves or could settle alternatives under identical conditions, so the decisive skeptical qualification remains unaddressed. Live burden: The compatibilist route must explicitly connect conditional changeability to the particular control sufficient for desert, rather than treating practical flexibility as automatically resolving the sourcehood controversy. Locked score: The mixed band reflects strong relevance to natural self-control and accessible experiential support, offset by scope instability, overstated rhetoric, comparatively weak representational charity, and classification as a relevant nonanswer to both targeted objections.

Gregg Caruso — section score 90/100

Adding indeterminacy at a strategically important point in deliberation does not provide desert-grounding control, because the agent still cannot settle which supported outcome ultimately occurs.

Strongest feature: The argument precisely confines itself to event-causal libertarian accounts and identifies settlement, rather than mere unpredictability, as the responsibility-relevant deficiency requiring explanation. Principal limitation: Its compact presentation establishes a substantial conceptual objection but supplies neither sustained engagement with agent-causal alternatives nor an independent demonstration that settlement is necessary for basic desert. Live burden: The skeptical route must connect this bounded indeterministic shortfall to its broader sourcehood thesis while allowing that practical deliberation, reasons-responsiveness, and ordinary agency can remain consequentially significant. Locked score: The very-strong band reflects excellent central relevance, coherent qualification, and direct conceptual leverage, tempered by comparatively limited evidential development and minimal consideration of philosophically developed opposing models.

Sourcehood, Determinism, and Manipulation

17:51–63:28

Daniel Dennett — section score 82/100

Autonomous adults become largely self-made through sustained learning, reflection, practice, exposure to reasons, and moral education, notwithstanding the deterministic histories shaping those developmental processes.

Strongest feature: The reply replaces instantaneous or ultimate authorship with a temporally extended account of self-formation involving learning, practice, reflection, moral education, exposure to reasons, and recognized impairment exceptions. Principal limitation: Calling mature agents “largely self-made” does not explain why causally inherited capacities and environments become desert-conferring authorship, so the skeptic’s regress concerning formative conditions persists. Live burden: The compatibilist must defend practical, historical sourcehood as normatively sufficient without implying impossible self-creation, and must identify when development genuinely belongs to the agent rather than manipulation or malfunction. Locked score: The strong band recognizes a coherent developmental mechanism, substantial direct responsiveness, clear burden contact, and reasonable charity, while preserving the material bridge problem between acquired autonomy and backward-looking desert.

Gregg Caruso — section score 87/100

Because agents’ characters and actions ultimately result from factors beyond their control, they lack moral responsibility in the specifically identified basic-desert sense under determinism or indeterminism.

Strongest feature: The thesis directly states the skeptical route’s motion-level foundation and carefully restricts its denial to pervasive basic-desert responsibility rather than indiscriminately rejecting agency or accountability. Principal limitation: The categorical transition from ultimately uncontrolled constitutive factors to absent desert receives compressed justification, leaving the necessity of ultimate sourcehood substantially asserted rather than independently established. Live burden: The case must explain why developed reasons-responsive self-government cannot provide sufficient authorship, while preserving the explicitly acknowledged causal, attributive, communicative, and forward-looking forms of responsibility. Locked score: The strong band recognizes exceptional burden relevance, stable terminology, and an intelligible sourcehood principle, while registering thinner argumentative support, limited comparison with compatibilist sufficiency standards, and somewhat overstated conclusiveness.

Basic Desert and Ordinary Compatibilist Desert

30:20–108:12

Daniel Dennett — section score 76/100

Basic desert is the wrong threshold; sufficient ordinary moral responsibility can instead justify proportionate coercion, imprisonment, fines, and reputational consequences for responsible legal violations.

Strongest feature: The reply directly contests the skeptic’s required threshold and insists that ordinary responsibility, rather than ultimate self-creation, is the normatively relevant basis for lawful consequences. Principal limitation: The phrase “enough moral responsibility” lacks a developed sufficiency criterion, and permissible coercion is asserted without independently deriving proportionality, imprisonment, or desert from reasons-responsive competence. Live burden: The compatibilist case must distinguish its justification from the skeptic’s public-protection rationale and identify exactly what ordinary desert contributes beyond causal attribution, deterrence, rehabilitation, and prevention. Locked score: The strong band reflects immediate motion contact and meaningful responsiveness, but its lower boundary records unstable terminology, materially overstated entitlement, limited warrant, and incomplete engagement with the strongest non-desert alternative.

Gregg Caruso — section score 91/100

Free-will skepticism rejects basic-desert responsibility but preserves causal attribution and forward-looking moral engagement grounded in future reconciliation, public safety, and moral formation.

Strongest feature: The reply sharply delimits skepticism, preserves legitimate accountability functions, and supplies reconciliation, safety, and moral formation as explicit non-desert foundations rather than merely denying compatibilist claims. Principal limitation: These enumerated purposes establish a credible framework but do not alone demonstrate that every defensible blaming, sanctioning, or communicative practice can be reconstructed without residual desert commitments. Live burden: The skeptical route must show how its alternatives retain proportionality, moral address, institutional stability, and interpersonal seriousness while avoiding the backward-looking justification it rejects. Locked score: The very-strong band reflects outstanding motion relevance, disciplined qualification, exceptional charity, and a successful justified reframe, with only bounded evidential incompleteness concerning implementation and comparative institutional performance.

Punishment, Public Safety, and Institutional Justification

36:51–111:41

Daniel Dennett — section score 85/100

Public, proportionate punishment can preserve respect for valuable legal institutions and instantiate consequentialist desert, regardless of whether human action is deterministically or indeterministically caused.

Strongest feature: The reply connects responsibility practices to publicly known, proportionate legal sanctions and grounds their preservation in institutional consequences rather than dependence on a particular metaphysics of causation. Principal limitation: Calling the resulting account “consequentialist desert” leaves desert’s independent contribution unclear, while the claimed superiority of punishment over less restrictive alternatives receives insufficient comparative support. Live burden: The compatibilist must explain why respected rules require deserved punishment specifically, rather than proportionate prevention, restitution, rehabilitation, or deterrence administered without backward-looking desert. Locked score: The strong band recognizes complete motion contact, institutional concreteness, stable public-law considerations, and meaningful responsiveness, while recording conceptual instability, limited evidence, incomplete charity toward least-restrictive alternatives, and an unresolved dispensability problem.

Gregg Caruso — section score 90/100

Significant public danger can justify proportionate, least-restrictive liberty limitations through self-defense and harm prevention, without invoking free will, retribution, or basic desert.

Strongest feature: The reply provides a concrete non-desert justification for coercion and expressly constrains governmental authority through significant danger, proportionality, self-defense, harm prevention, and least-restrictive intervention. Principal limitation: The quarantine analogy leaves morally important differences between disease transmission and intentional criminal conduct underdeveloped, including communication, equal treatment, evidentiary standards, and agency-sensitive legal classification. Live burden: The alternative framework must demonstrate that calibrated prevention and rehabilitation can govern actual institutions without collapsing persons into predicted risks or disregarding legitimate distinctions among offenses. Locked score: The very-strong band reflects complete motion contact, careful qualification, strong coherence, substantial evidential warrant, and charitable responsiveness, moderated only by bounded analogical and implementation gaps.

Reactive Attitudes and Reform of Responsibility Practices

98:55–125:43

Daniel Dennett — section score 74/100

Disappointment may guide children, but terrible wrongdoing by competent adults can warrant moral anger that publicly communicates seriousness and discourages comparable misconduct by others.

Strongest feature: The reply directly distinguishes developmental guidance from responses to competent adult wrongdoing and identifies moral anger’s possible condemnatory and public-warning functions in exceptionally serious cases. Principal limitation: The asserted entitlement to anger rests mainly on an intuitive political example and does not show why firm protest, sorrow, disappointment, or sanctions cannot communicate equivalent seriousness. Live burden: The compatibilist must provide a fittingness standard for moral anger, distinguish it from hostility or retribution, and demonstrate benefits unavailable through non-desert reactive attitudes. Locked score: The mixed band reflects immediate contact with the decisive replacement challenge and a recognizable adult-wrongdoing distinction, offset by materially overstated certainty, weak evidential warrant, incomplete charity, and failure to establish functional uniqueness.

Gregg Caruso — section score 82/100

Responsibility practices have changed historically, including movement beyond honor cultures, so society can further reform them by relinquishing potentially harmful concepts of just desert and retribution.

Strongest feature: The reply uses historical movement from honor cultures toward individualistic practices to establish that responsibility conventions are socially evolved, revisable, and not fixed by psychological familiarity. Principal limitation: Past change demonstrates reform’s possibility but not the superiority, feasibility, or harmlessness of abandoning desert, and the asserted dangers of retained concepts receive limited evidential support. Live burden: The reformist case must compare concrete replacement practices with existing institutions and show that they preserve condemnation, deterrence, moral communication, fairness, and protection across serious cases. Locked score: The strong band reflects careful modal calibration, a relevant historical example, charitable recognition of continuing functions, and effective subsidiary burden contact, tempered by limited evidence for harmfulness and replacement effectiveness.
