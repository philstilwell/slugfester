Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Evidence for theism: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Established a coherent probability-raising framework that connected every affirmative route to the motion.
  • Presented clear, recoverable constructive arguments across contingency, cosmic origins, fine-tuning, morality, and resurrection.
  • Answered multiverse objections by pressing both observer prediction and higher-level fine-tuning of generating laws.
  • Broadened the evidential framework effectively by distinguishing scientific evidence from moral, philosophical, metaphysical, and historical considerations.
  • Responded directly to arbitrariness and ordinary-history objections with necessary-character and comparative-explanation accounts.

Published weaknesses

  • The moral argument assigned certainty-level force without establishing that objective morality uniquely requires God.
  • Several cosmological moves reached a necessary or transcendent terminus more readily than they identified that terminus as God.
  • The resurrection comparison did not fully incorporate composite ordinary hypotheses or explicit prior probabilities.

Lawrence Krauss — Empirical naturalism: 81/100

Published strengths

  • Correctly placed the burden of establishing evidence on the affirmative without assuming an obligation to disprove every God concept.
  • Supplied concrete natural alternatives from quantum gravity, inflation, multiverse cosmology, dynamics, and observer selection.
  • Delivered a particularly strong moral-independence dilemma against unconstrained divine-command accounts.
  • Introduced prior probability and ordinary testimonial fallibility into the resurrection comparison.
  • Repeatedly treated unresolved physical questions as grounds for investigation rather than premature supernatural conclusions.

Published weaknesses

  • The infinity response moved too freely between formal mathematical infinity and an actually instantiated temporal past.
  • The natural necessary-terminus reply conflated an eternal multiverse with a metaphysically necessary reality.
  • Natural moral cognition and conduct were developed more fully than a secular ontology of objective moral truth.
  • The ordinary resurrection alternatives lacked a unified, source-specific account of all the affirmative's proposed facts.

Evidential Standards and the Cumulative Inference

0:22–126:59

William Lane Craig — section score 87/100

Facts about cosmic origins, order, morality, and history jointly increase the probability of God relative to the same background information without those facts.

Strongest feature: The move establishes a precise comparative-probability framework, identifies several evidential domains, and connects their cumulative relevance directly to the motion rather than claiming that any isolated consideration deductively proves theism. Principal limitation: The assertion that the probability increase is indisputable outruns the presentation because neither evidential independence nor an aggregation method, background prior, or comparative likelihood is supplied for the announced considerations. Live burden: The cumulative case must demonstrate that its components remain independently informative, survive the strongest natural alternatives, and jointly favor theism without repeatedly counting the same explanatory intuition. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional motion-level relevance, explicit qualification, stable terminology, and coherent evidential architecture, moderated by incomplete probabilistic warrant and implicit cumulative bridges.

Lawrence Krauss — section score 82/100

Craig bears the affirmative burden to provide recognizable empirical evidence for God, whereas Krauss need not establish that no God exists.

Strongest feature: The move correctly locates the affirmative burden, distinguishes failure to establish the motion from disproving God, and directly tests whether Craig's cumulative considerations genuinely discriminate in favor of the proposed conclusion. Principal limitation: Recognizable empirical evidence is never given a stable criterion, and the presentation sometimes suggests a scientific restriction broad enough to exclude legitimate philosophical, moral, or historical evidence without defending that exclusion. Live burden: The negative must articulate a defensible evidential standard that demands discrimination and testability where appropriate while permitting nonlaboratory reasoning capable of supporting metaphysical conclusions. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects clear burden allocation, direct responsiveness, and important methodological pressure, moderated by uncertain scope, incomplete justification of empirical exclusivity, and vulnerability to the self-defeat objection.

Contingency and the Necessary Explanatory Ground

2:20–123:10

William Lane Craig — section score 82/100

Contingent beings require explanation either through their own nature or an external cause, and the universe therefore points toward a necessarily existing transcendent ground.

Strongest feature: The move presents a recoverable explanatory principle, distinguishes necessary existence from externally caused contingency, and applies that distinction directly to the universe and the affirmative cosmological route. Principal limitation: The reasoning reaches a necessary explanatory terminus more readily than it identifies that terminus with a transcendent divine person, while the governing explanatory principle is asserted without comparison against natural necessary candidates. Live burden: The argument must establish both that the relevant totality is contingent and that a specifically divine ground has explanatory advantages unavailable to a necessary physical structure, law, or multiverse. Locked score: The strong and competent band recognizes clear logical organization, substantial responsiveness to the central burden, and a disciplined proposition, while preserving the material identification gap and comparatively limited evidential warrant.

Lawrence Krauss — section score 80/100

Unknown explanations and contingent causal processes invite further physical investigation rather than an inference that their still-undiscovered explanation is God.

Strongest feature: The move identifies the unsupported transition from explanatory ignorance to a divine conclusion, while recommending investigation and acknowledging that an account may be entirely physical. Principal limitation: It narrows the affirmative’s argument about contingent reality into a claim about unknown processes producing human beings, so its warning against gap reasoning does not directly defeat the principle demanding an ultimate explanation. Live burden: The negative must isolate the divine-identification gap even if that explanatory principle is granted, then explain how a physical terminus could meet the relevant modal requirement. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects central diagnostic pressure, disciplined uncertainty, and relevance, tempered by target slippage, limited warrant, and no developed natural necessary-ground account.

Cosmic Beginning, Infinity, and Transcendent Causation

5:13–49:07

William Lane Craig — section score 84/100

Whatever begins to exist has a cause; the universe began to exist, so its cause transcends physical space and time and supports theism.

Strongest feature: The move states a compact causal syllogism whose premises, conclusion, and relevance are readily recoverable, then clearly explains why a cause of physical spacetime would not itself be spatial or temporal. Principal limitation: Even if the causal conclusion follows, the move compresses the further transition from a transcendent cause to God and does not yet address competing interpretations of a cosmic boundary or beginning. Live burden: The affirmative must defend the universe's finite past across applicable cosmological models and show why the inferred cause possesses distinctively theistic rather than merely nonclassical causal properties. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects excellent burden contact, coherent structure, and substantial scientific-philosophical relevance, tempered by the underdeveloped divine identification and incomplete engagement with alternative cosmologies.

Lawrence Krauss — section score 81/100

Mathematics and successful physics routinely use infinities, so counterintuitive examples alone cannot establish that an actual infinite cosmic past is impossible.

Strongest feature: The reply usefully challenges any inference from intuitive oddity to impossibility and invokes mathematics and empirically successful physics as disciplined contexts in which infinite quantities can be handled consistently. Principal limitation: The discussion shifts among mathematical sets, series, physical quantities, singular behavior, and an actually instantiated temporal history, so success with one category does not establish the physical possibility of another. Live burden: The negative must address the specific metaphysical and cosmological arguments against an actual past infinity while distinguishing formal mathematical coherence from physical realization and temporal traversal. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects a relevant and intellectually serious correction to intuition-based reasoning, moderated by partly unstable terminology, limited model-specific evidence, and incomplete contact with actual temporal instantiation.

Fine-Tuning, Design, and Multiverse Selection

9:29–82:03

William Lane Craig — section score 85/100

Life-permitting constants and quantities invite comparison among physical necessity, chance, and design, with design offered as the superior explanation.

Strongest feature: The move organizes the fine-tuning dispute around explicit competing explanations, identifies the alleged independence of constants from known laws, and advances a directly relevant comparative inference rather than a bare expression of wonder. Principal limitation: The design preference lacks stated priors, likelihoods, and a sufficiently specified design hypothesis, while independence from currently known laws does not establish independence from every deeper physical account. Live burden: The affirmative must compare design against dynamical generation, multiverse selection, and observational conditioning under defensible probability measures rather than treating the initial trilemma as automatically exhaustive. Locked score: The strong and competent band rewards clear alternatives, strong relevance, and charitable attention to necessity and chance, while retaining significant reservations about calibration, exhaustiveness, and quantitative warrant.

Lawrence Krauss — section score 82/100

Quantum-gravitational origins, variable physical laws, and observer selection offer physical routes to apparent fine-tuning without supernatural imposition.

Strongest feature: The move supplies several concrete natural alternative classes, emphasizes observer selection, and uses the broader phenomenon of naturally generated apparent design to challenge a premature inference from suitability to purpose. Principal limitation: Biological selection and cosmological observational selection are partly blended, and listing possible mechanisms does not furnish a likelihood comparison explaining the complete distribution of constants or initial conditions. Live burden: The negative must specify a generating model, probability measure, and selection effect capable of making the observed life-permitting conditions materially less surprising than they are under the relevant design hypothesis. Locked score: The strong and competent band recognizes direct responsiveness, calibrated possibility claims, and meaningful physical alternatives, while preserving substantial limitations in quantitative warrant, model specificity, and the reach of the evolutionary analogy.

Moral Objectivity and Divine Grounding

12:53–79:42

William Lane Craig — section score 80/100

Some acts are objectively wrong, and objective moral values and duties allegedly require God because atheism cannot furnish their ontological foundation.

Strongest feature: The move sharply distinguishes moral objectivity from personal preference, supplies vivid examples of apparent objective wrongness, and connects the claimed moral facts to a central theistic grounding argument. Principal limitation: The inference from objective wrongness to God's existence is insufficiently defended, distinctions among values, duties, and ontology become unstable, and the certainty-level probability claim radically exceeds the presented dependence premise. Live burden: The affirmative must show why secular realism, rational normativity, or other nondivine moral ontologies fail, while explaining how divine nature grounds authority without circularity or arbitrariness. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects direct relevance and a recognizable syllogistic structure, but its lower boundary preserves substantial penalties for overstatement, incomplete warrant, and limited representation of serious secular alternatives.

Lawrence Krauss — section score 83/100

If grave wrongdoing remains wrong despite divine approval, moral truth constrains God and cannot originate merely in God's discretionary commands.

Strongest feature: The move formulates a sharp counterfactual test that exposes arbitrariness in unconstrained divine-command theories and clearly explains why fixed moral truth would be independent of divine choice. Principal limitation: The dilemma directly defeats voluntarism more readily than a necessary-divine-nature theory, which denies that contrary commands are genuinely possible and relocates rather than simply accepts the authority question. Live burden: The negative must show that appealing to God's essential character either remains explanatorily circular or fails to improve upon independent moral realism, without representing necessary-character accounts as discretionary command theories. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise recoverability, excellent diagnostic relevance, well-matched asserted force, and a powerful objection, with only the nature-based response and its residual authority problem left for further comparison.

Resurrection and Competing Historical Explanations

15:52–114:25

William Lane Craig — section score 82/100

The empty tomb, postmortem appearances, and transformed disciples constitute historical facts that Craig argues are best explained by Jesus's divine resurrection.

Strongest feature: The move supplies a concrete three-part historical dataset, identifies resurrection as a comparative explanation, and makes the route testable against recognizable historical alternatives rather than relying only on confessional affirmation. Principal limitation: The asserted degree of scholarly agreement, independence of the traditions, and reliability of each alleged fact remain insufficiently developed, while acceptance of the dataset does not independently establish a miraculous explanation. Live burden: The case must justify the sources, transmission, dating, and dependence behind every core fact and compare resurrection with both individual and composite ordinary hypotheses using priors as well as explanatory scope. Locked score: The strong and competent band credits clear structure, high relevance, and a substantive abductive case, while retaining reservations about historical warrant, implicit qualifications, and the extraordinary explanatory leap.

Lawrence Krauss — section score 80/100

Fallible human testimony is antecedently more plausible than miracle, so resurrection reports require source-specific reliability before supporting God.

Strongest feature: The reply properly introduces ordinary testimonial fallibility and prior plausibility into the historical comparison, directly challenging any automatic transition from reported experiences to a supernatural event. Principal limitation: Labeling the material anecdotal and invoking generic human error does not analyze these sources' dating, dependence, transmission, incentives, or particular reliability, so it cannot independently resolve the disputed reports. Live burden: The negative must develop a source-specific error model capable of explaining the principal traditions while comparing its explanatory scope and evidential probability with the resurrection hypothesis. Locked score: The strong and competent band recognizes coherent probabilistic pressure, high relevance, and a readily understood ordinary alternative, moderated by limited historical detail, compressed characterization of the evidence, and incomplete engagement with the affirmative's multi-fact comparative case.
