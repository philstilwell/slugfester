Overall assessment

Sharon Dirckx — Christian suffering framework: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Dirckx decisively separated genuine evil from the claim that every tragedy must be God's will or produce an individualized greater good.
  • Her causal-versus-normative distinction exposed why evolutionary selection alone cannot rank compassionate norms above exploitative strategies with objective authority.
  • The equal-dignity reply coherently distinguished foundational Christian teaching from historical noncompliance while explicitly acknowledging failures by Christian institutions.
  • Her account of costly forgiveness addressed offender impunity by retaining consequences, victim recognition, moral transformation, and the claimed cost borne by Christ.

Published weaknesses

  • Divine solidarity and eventual restoration remained relevant responses to suffering but did not explain why an omnipotent, loving God initially permits preventable atrocities.
  • The partial-kingdom explanation accommodated unanswered prayer without providing observable criteria that distinguish selective divine healing from ordinary medical uncertainty.
  • The judgment account called God's invitation inclusive without specifying how sincere, involuntary unbelief affects final outcomes or supporting the promised restoration independently.

Stephen Woodford — Atheist problem of evil: 80/100

Published strengths

  • Woodford's evolutionary account supplied a plausible naturalistic explanation for widespread aversion to suffering without requiring a theological causal mechanism.
  • His permission-based reply preserved the problem of evil after Dirckx denied divine authorship by focusing attention on God's knowing allowance of preventable suffering.
  • The selective-healing challenge offered the debate's clearest comparative test by contrasting ambiguous remissions with outcomes ordinary biology does not produce.
  • His sincere-unbelief argument precisely distinguished inability to become convinced from deliberate refusal and directly challenged the fairness of belief-conditioned judgment.

Published weaknesses

  • The repeated claim that every permitted evil must produce a superior outcome conflated morally sufficient permission with instrumental benefit from each particular tragedy.
  • The historical moral-change argument underplayed the distinction between an allegedly unchanging standard and fallible interpretation, while leaving objective normativity unresolved.
  • The salvation asymmetry assumed simplified, definite outcomes for converted offenders and decent atheists despite the opposing account's qualifications about transformation and heart-sensitive judgment.

Suffering, naturalism, and the coherence of a broken creation

Christianity portrays creation as simultaneously beautiful and broken, accommodating natural causes of suffering while affirming that humanity correctly experiences suffering as genuinely wrong.

Strongest feature: The reply gives Christianity a coherent conceptual vocabulary for recognizing natural mechanisms, existential rawness, and genuine moral wrongness without dismissing suffering as spiritually insignificant or merely illusory. Principal limitation: Calling creation broken remains underdeveloped as a causal explanation, and the selected reasoning largely asserts the framework instead of demonstrating how divine goodness, natural regularity, and destructive outcomes interconnect. Live burden: The account still must answer why perfect power and love permit the broken condition, especially because validating human protest does not independently resolve the opponent's narrower permission-based challenge. Locked score: The strong classification reflects substantial coherence, central relevance, and faithful proposition recovery, moderated by incomplete responsiveness, compressed explanatory warrants, and an important unanswered inference concerning particular permitted evils.

Stephen Woodford — section score 77/100

Naturalism treats suffering as an ordinary feature of nature, while an omnipotent and perfectly loving God appears to require an explanation for permitted evil.

Strongest feature: The opening contrast directly reaches the motion, differentiating naturalism's explanatory expectations from the apparent theological difficulty created by combining unlimited divine power, perfect love, and pervasive suffering. Principal limitation: Its universal formulation overstates what those attributes independently entail, because morally sufficient permission might involve freedom, regular natural order, or systemic constraints without every individual evil producing compensating benefit. Live burden: The argument must distinguish demanding an adequate justification for divine permission from claiming, without an additional doctrinal premise, that every tragedy necessarily contributes to a superior outcome. Locked score: The strong classification reflects exceptional relevance and clear recoverability alongside materially weakened warrant, incomplete qualification, and limited representational charity toward more sophisticated permission-based Christian alternatives.

Evolution, objective morality, and human dignity

15:17–22:27

Sharon Dirckx — section score 87/100

Evolution can explain the origin of moral dispositions, but selection alone cannot determine why compassionate cooperation is objectively preferable to an exploitative strategy that also promotes survival.

Strongest feature: The reply precisely separates causal genealogy from normative authority, showing that evolutionary selection can generate competing survival strategies without, by itself, establishing which strategy deserves objective moral endorsement. Principal limitation: Successfully identifying the descriptive-normative gap does not yet demonstrate that the required external standard is divine, because secular moral realism or independent practical reasoning could occupy that explanatory position. Live burden: The Christian case must positively connect objective ranking to God while addressing non-theistic alternatives, rather than treating evolution's incompleteness as sufficient evidence for its preferred metaphysical foundation. Locked score: The very strong classification reflects exceptional responsiveness, conceptual precision, calibrated scope, and substantial charity, with only a bounded unsupported bridge from the demonstrated limitation of evolutionary mechanism to specifically Christian moral grounding.

Stephen Woodford — section score 81/100

Natural selection plausibly produced inherited aversion to suffering and cooperative social dispositions because they frequently support survival, reproduction, longevity, and collective prosperity.

Strongest feature: The evolutionary account supplies a concrete naturalistic counterexplanation for widespread aversion, connecting inherited social dispositions with survival and reproduction while directly addressing whether Christianity is causally necessary. Principal limitation: Explaining why organisms possess aversive intuitions does not establish that suffering is objectively wrong, so the move answers the psychological-origin question more completely than the distinct normative-authority question. Live burden: The naturalistic case must either defend an independent account of moral objectivity or explicitly limit its conclusion to evolutionary explanation, avoiding an unsupported transition from adaptive disposition to justified obligation. Locked score: The very strong classification reflects coherent mechanism, appropriate calibration, stable scope, and excellent contact with the causal challenge, while preserving a significant philosophical gap between descriptive ancestry and normative truth.

Divine presence, permitted evil, and future restoration

29:27–41:12

Sharon Dirckx — section score 78/100

Some atrocities are simply abject evils rather than necessary means to benefits, while humanity's inability to identify every divine reason does not demonstrate that no reason exists.

Strongest feature: The reply explicitly refuses to instrumentalize genocide while adding a modest epistemic point: limited human access cannot support certainty that every possible reason for divine permission is absent. Principal limitation: Mere possibility of unknown reasons answers a deductive contradiction more effectively than an evidential probability argument, and the term reasons sometimes shifts between permission-justifications and goods resulting from evil. Live burden: The Christian case must offer positive considerations making morally sufficient permission credible, rather than relying primarily on human ignorance when the opponent asks whether observed suffering lowers divine goodness's probability. Locked score: The strong classification reflects direct contact, disciplined qualification, and valuable rejection of universal greater-good reasoning, moderated by limited evidential warrant, semantic instability, and incomplete engagement with the stronger probabilistic objection.

Stephen Woodford — section score 80/100

Future-healing promises require evidential support, and conditioning their benefits on Christian belief would treat sincere unbelievers in a manner difficult to reconcile with perfect divine love.

Strongest feature: The reply directly tests both the warrant and beneficiary scope of promised restoration, identifying a serious tension between perfect love and excluding conscientious people who remain sincerely unconvinced. Principal limitation: It generalizes strict exclusivism across varied Christian accounts and briefly groups Christianity with Islam, underrepresenting inclusivist, individualized, or postmortem approaches to judgment and reconciliation. Live burden: The objection must specify which salvation doctrine it targets and show that Dirckx's own inclusive formulation still entails belief-conditioned exclusion, rather than importing that conclusion from a broader religious category. Locked score: The strong classification reflects excellent responsiveness, central relevance, and a forceful moral question, moderated by doctrinal compression, missing scope qualifications, and only partial warrant for treating one contested interpretation as definitive.

Unanswered prayer and evidence of divine healing

44:30–50:25

Sharon Dirckx — section score 72/100

Because God's kingdom has begun but remains incomplete within a broken world, unanswered healing prayer indicates neither deficient faith, inadequate prayer, nor punishment for personal wrongdoing.

Strongest feature: The reply offers a pastorally careful internal explanation of unanswered prayer, explicitly rejecting victim-blaming interpretations involving deficient faith, inadequate persistence, or divine punishment for personal wrongdoing. Principal limitation: Attributing non-healing to continuing brokenness lacks discriminating evidence and omits competing natural explanations, while the move does not support the future completion or beneficiary scope challenged earlier. Live burden: The framework must identify observable expectations distinguishing partial-kingdom action from ordinary medical uncertainty and explain why selective intervention coheres with equal divine love toward similarly situated sufferers. Locked score: The mixed classification reflects clear doctrine, stable scope, and meaningful subsidiary relevance, outweighed by low responsiveness to the earlier warrant demand, asserted causal explanation, and absence of comparative evidential criteria.

Stephen Woodford — section score 88/100

Ambiguous remissions fit ordinary medical uncertainty, whereas divine healing should sometimes produce unmistakable outcomes, such as restored limbs, that natural processes do not plausibly generate.

Strongest feature: The reply supplies a discriminating comparison between naturally variable remissions and conspicuous biological restoration, directly diagnosing why the partial-kingdom account risks accommodating every possible healing outcome. Principal limitation: The proposed expectation assumes divine intervention should prioritize publicly spectacular events, whereas a theist might predict rare, medically mediated, non-coercive, or purpose-sensitive healing without uniform violations of ordinary biology. Live burden: The evidential argument must justify its miracle expectations and compare actual outcome distributions, while explaining why increasingly subtle theistic predictions lose confirmatory power rather than merely remaining logically possible. Locked score: The very strong classification reflects exceptional responsiveness, coherent comparative reasoning, calibrated probability language, and substantial charity, with remaining uncertainty concerning the independently justified frequency and form of expected intervention.

Principal limitation: It treats eternal punishment for nonbelief as the operative Christian outcome without adequately qualifying doctrines that incorporate knowledge, motives, conduct, opportunity, inclusivism, or individualized divine judgment. Live burden: The objection must establish that the defended framework condemns sincere unbelief itself, then show why its proposed heart-sensitive judgment cannot resolve the tension without becoming evidentially or doctrinally indeterminate.
