Agent and Appraiser Relativism

59:08–74:19

Lance Bush — section score 93/100

Agent relativism varies obligations with the agent's standards, whereas appraiser relativism indexes moral evaluations to an appraiser's perspective without equivalently binding the evaluated agent.

Strongest feature: The semantic contrast clearly separates the standard governing an agent's obligations from the perspective indexing an appraiser's evaluation, preserving a shared relativist genus while identifying different truth and consequence structures. Principal limitation: The presentation remains largely conceptual and would benefit from a more explicit formal semantics demonstrating how identical utterances receive different evaluations under each theory across contested cases. Live burden: The anti-realist alternative needed stable indexing rules, an account of disagreement between appraisers, and a fuller explanation of how practical guidance follows when appraiser-relative truth does not itself generate agent-relative obligation. Locked score: The very strong band reflects outstanding precision, coherence, central burden completion, responsiveness, and charity, while limited formalization and residual explanatory questions prevent the distinction from resolving every challenge to relativism.

Derek McAllister — section score 76/100

Principal limitation: A single individual can occupy both roles while role-indexed truth conditions, normative sources, and practical consequences remain distinct, so token-level overlap does not establish theoretical collapse or ontological identity. Live burden: The argument needed a stable definition of perspective and a formal bridge showing why simultaneous agency and appraisal eliminate semantic indexing differences rather than merely allowing one person to instantiate both relations. Locked score: The strong band reflects meaningful central relevance, a clear recoverable proposition, and plausible experiential support, while unstable perspective language, slightly overstated implications, and incomplete contact with the preceding challenge limit its effectiveness.
