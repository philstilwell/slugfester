Overall assessment

Jonathan Pageau — Participatory religious patterns as enduring truth: 74/100

Published strengths

  • Pageau’s strongest exchange exposed the missing normative premise in selective revision: calling inherited patterns better, worse, or harmful requires an identifiable standpoint, goal, and substantive account of good.
  • He consistently reframed scripture as participatory and experiential rather than failed mechanical science, offering a coherent explanation for why religious language might remain meaningful across scientific change.
  • His late concessions materially improved calibration: Christianity can deteriorate, historical practices can become unsuitable, and reform can occur while participants remain embedded within inherited stories.
  • Pageau’s answer to tribal morality used enemy-love and humanization to present Christianity as transforming boundaries and hierarchy rather than simply preserving exclusion or denying moral development.

Published weaknesses

  • The census interpretation and Genesis mappings repeatedly expanded suggestive analogies into universal claims without enough textual or empirical support, weakening the evidentiary foundation of enduring disclosure.
  • On homosexuality, Pageau distinguished pattern from application and behavior from identity but never clearly answered whether the contested application should be rejected, leaving Woodford’s concrete harm challenge standing.
  • His final metaphysical reversal labeled reality’s patterns divine without defending that inference, so the deepest explanatory claim remained substantially less warranted and less precise than his standpoint critique.

Stephen Woodford — Evolutionary scrutiny and contextual revision: 78/100

Published strengths

  • Woodford’s central case combined preservation with revision: religious motifs can encode genuine evolved insight while changed environments make particular inheritances obsolete, maladaptive, or destructive.
  • He repeatedly answered the charge of abstract detachment by locating criticism within shared evolutionary psychology, conceding permanent constraints while preserving cross-tradition comparison and contextual scrutiny.
  • Concrete ethical examples—tribal prescriptions, slavery, outsiders, and homosexual experience—forced the debate from symbolic generalities toward the actual consequences of allowing inherited patterns to guide present life.
  • His final clarification was exceptionally responsive and calibrated: Christianity contains valuable patterns, but a deeper embodied evolutionary standpoint permits symmetrical criticism of Christianity and competing traditions.

Published weaknesses

  • The opening survival-versus-truth claim became unnecessarily categorical, overlooking the possibility that adaptive cognition can generally track environmental truth even without direct selection for abstract truth.
  • His goal-relative account never fully resolved conflicts among goals or explained why evolved intuitions authorize particular revisions, leaving Pageau’s strongest normative-standpoint challenge only partially answered.
  • The dogma argument overgeneralized from popular literalism, supplied no representative evidence, and used an inflammatory cancer metaphor despite acknowledging that Pageau’s theology differed from its principal target.
