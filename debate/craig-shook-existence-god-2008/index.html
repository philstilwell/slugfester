Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Theistic supernaturalism: 76/100

Published strengths

  • Presented a cumulative case spanning cosmic origins, fine-tuning, morality, history, and experience rather than depending upon one evidential route.
  • Clearly distinguished deductive inference from best-explanation reasoning and correctly returned attention to the warrant for premises.
  • Separated moral ontology from moral knowledge and identified why evolutionary explanations of belief do not themselves settle moral truth.
  • Offered recognizable historical criteria for evaluating New Testament sources instead of demanding their automatic acceptance.

Published weaknesses

  • The cosmic argument repeatedly moved from a beginning or model boundary to a personal transcendent cause without fully defending the intervening modal and agency bridges.
  • The moral constructive treated naturalism too readily as relativism and did not eliminate serious nontheistic accounts of objective normativity.
  • The fine-tuning replies narrowed the life target usefully but left probability measures and design's distinct predictions insufficiently developed.
  • The resurrection case asserted established facts and explanatory superiority without enough displayed source analysis or comparison with natural alternatives.

John Shook — Philosophical naturalism: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Maintained a carefully limited evidential posture that distinguished comparative reasonableness from proof of universal naturalism.
  • Pressed both timeless personhood and further natural cosmology as separate weaknesses in the cosmic inference.
  • Distinguished objective morality from absolute or human-independent morality and supplied constructive human practices supporting moral correction.
  • Applied underdetermination consistently to historical miracles and conflicting religious experiences while demanding comparative corroboration.

Published weaknesses

  • The hostile-universe objection generalized theology's flexibility without distinguishing unconstrained accommodation from independently specified design hypotheses.
  • The collective morality account did not fully bridge socially defensible standards to the stronger ontological objectivity disputed by Craig.
  • The miracle-parity reply charged hypocrisy without comparing the evidential quality of particular rival traditions.
  • General confidence in nature occasionally stood in for the required individual analysis of each proposed supernatural bridge.

Epistemic Burdens, Inference Form, and Naturalism

25:45–117:40

William Lane Craig — section score 73/100

If naturalism claims that physical reality exhausts existence, conceding that no method proves this allegedly leaves its positive ontology unsupported.

Strongest feature: The challenge properly observes that categorical naturalism carries a positive burden and asks what warrants treating physical reality as exhaustive rather than merely familiar. Principal limitation: It inadequately represents Shook's explicit comparative posture, converting acknowledged inability to prove universal naturalism into faith, self-defeat, or evidential equivalence with supernaturalism. Live burden: The reply must distinguish ontological certainty from defeasible confidence and show why established natural evidence cannot rationally support the latter without proving the former. Locked score: The mixed band reflects meaningful contact with a legitimate burden question but also substantial scope instability, reduced charity, and an inference from absent proof to absent justification that leaves the opponent's central evidential distinction largely unanswered.

John Shook — section score 84/100

Confidence in established natural knowledge is reasonable even without proving universal naturalism, while supernatural claims require positive evidential bridges.

Strongest feature: The position carefully distinguishes comparative reasonableness from proof, expressly acknowledges uncertainty beyond established knowledge, and provides the con side's clearest epistemic foundation. Principal limitation: General confidence in expanding natural explanation cannot independently defeat particular supernatural evidence and may become overly conservative if applied without case-specific comparison. Live burden: Naturalistic restraint must evaluate every proposed bridge on its merits, explain an appropriate evidential threshold, and remain open to evidence that genuinely favors supernatural agency. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional calibration, precision, charity, and motion relevance while preserving the important qualification that methodological confidence in nature supplies a prior posture rather than an automatic refutation of positive theistic arguments.

Cosmic Beginning and Transcendent Causation

4:24–87:59

William Lane Craig — section score 75/100

Whatever begins to exist has a cause, and because the universe began, it has a transcendent cause claimed to be timeless, spaceless, immaterial, powerful, and personal.

Strongest feature: The explicit causal syllogism is readily recoverable, centrally relevant, and forcefully connects a cosmic beginning with explanatory reality beyond the universe itself. Principal limitation: The transition from a generic cause to timeless, spaceless, immaterial, powerful, and especially personal agency compresses indispensable modal and metaphysical bridges without comparable demonstration. Live burden: The argument must exclude impersonal or further natural causation and explain coherently how timeless personal agency originates a temporal effect without presupposing its conclusion. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent burden contact and recognizable deductive organization alongside consequential underqualification concerning the cause's attributed properties, personhood, and exclusivity over alternatives within the presented evidence.

John Shook — section score 86/100

A cosmic beginning does not establish God when timeless personhood remains problematic and further natural cosmological reality remains possible.

Strongest feature: The reply simultaneously challenges the coherence of timeless personal agency and preserves further natural reality as a live alternative to the proposed transcendent conclusion. Principal limitation: Describing timeless agency as problematic does not establish incoherence, and merely retaining a natural possibility cannot by itself explain the universe's apparent beginning. Live burden: The objection must articulate a contradiction or explanatory deficiency in timeless choice and develop natural alternatives beyond bare possibility while respecting the cosmological evidence presented. Locked score: The very strong band reflects decisive contact with both crucial bridging steps, precise limitation of its conclusion, and disciplined calibration, notwithstanding remaining constructive obligations concerning the feasibility and comparative explanatory quality of alternative cosmologies.

Fine-Tuning, Alternative Life, and Design

9:42–89:14

William Lane Craig — section score 77/100

Life-permitting constants appear extraordinarily restricted; physical necessity and chance are rejected as inadequate, leaving design as the preferred explanation.

Strongest feature: The fine-tuning case identifies concrete sensitivity among physical constants and organizes necessity, chance, and design into a clearly intelligible explanatory comparison. Principal limitation: Its asserted trilemma leaves probability measures, prior expectations, physical possibility ranges, and alternative cosmological mechanisms insufficiently specified before design receives preferential treatment. Live burden: The inference must establish a defensible probability space, compare strengthened natural alternatives, and show that intentional design predicts observed conditions more distinctly than rival explanations. Locked score: The strong band recognizes direct central relevance, coherent explanatory structure, and quantitative illustration while preserving substantial reservations about exhaustiveness, probabilistic calibration, and the compressed elimination of necessity and chance.

John Shook — section score 84/100

A largely life-hostile universe weakens design if theology can accommodate either abundant life or overwhelmingly lifeless conditions.

Strongest feature: The response tests design's discriminatory power by contrasting narrow life-permitting conditions with a universe overwhelmingly inhospitable to life and adaptable theological expectations. Principal limitation: Its generalized treatment of theology does not distinguish unconstrained after-the-fact accommodation from specific design hypotheses that might independently predict rarity or cosmic scale. Live burden: The objection must identify the relevant design model, state what observations it predicts, and demonstrate that flexibility actually prevents evidential discrimination rather than merely permitting multiple purposes. Locked score: The strong band recognizes a relevant and coherent challenge to explanatory flexibility while retaining reservations about broadened claims, underdeveloped theological comparisons, and the inference from widespread hostility to diminished intentional design.

Objective Morality, Knowledge, and Grounding

14:01–82:20

William Lane Craig — section score 80/100

Objective moral values and duties exist, naturalism allegedly cannot ground normative obligation, and God is proposed as their plausible foundation.

Strongest feature: The argument directly addresses moral ontology and offers a recognizable route from objective duties to a transcendent foundation, contacting an indispensable theistic burden. Principal limitation: Its sweeping characterization of naturalism as permitting everything conflates relativism, normativity, objectivity, and grounding while leaving secular realist possibilities substantially unexamined. Live burden: The case must distinguish moral knowledge from ontology, defeat serious nontheistic accounts, and establish why divine personhood uniquely explains binding obligations without definitional shortcuts. Locked score: The mixed band reflects high relevance and a clear proposed foundation but also materially overstated claims, unstable terminology, and insufficient support for moving from rejected relativism to exclusively divine grounding within this exchange.

John Shook — section score 84/100

Morality may be human-relative yet objective rather than absolute, so defining objectivity as human-independent prejudges the grounding dispute.

Strongest feature: The reply sharply distinguishes objectivity from absoluteness and identifies how defining objective morality as already human-independent can prejudge the disputed grounding question. Principal limitation: Human-relative objectivity remains underspecified, and exposing a tendentious definition does not itself provide an ontology adequate for categorical moral obligation. Live burden: The account must clarify what makes collective judgments truth-apt, correctable, and authoritative beyond preference while explaining how genuine objectivity survives dependence upon human life. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional diagnostic precision, direct responsiveness, and well-calibrated criticism, with a residual constructive obligation to demonstrate rather than merely assert that human-centered standards possess the objectivity required by the debate.

Resurrection, Historical Sources, and Miracle Claims

16:34–114:35

William Lane Craig — section score 80/100

The empty tomb, reported appearances, and disciples' resurrection belief are presented as established facts best explained by God's raising Jesus.

Strongest feature: The argument plainly identifies three historical claims, supplies a specific resurrection hypothesis, and connects that hypothesis directly to the debate's divine-action burden. Principal limitation: Calling those claims established and resurrection their best explanation outruns the displayed documentation, source-independence analysis, comparative alternatives, and treatment of prior improbability. Live burden: The case must substantiate each fact historically, evaluate dependence among sources, compare natural combinations fairly, and explain why extraordinary agency provides superior explanatory performance. Locked score: The strong band acknowledges a coherent, relevant inductive structure and clearly recoverable conclusion while retaining serious evidential reservations about corroboration, comparative testing, historical qualification, and the warranted force of its best-explanation claim.

John Shook — section score 79/100

The resurrection case lacks sufficient independent corroboration and remains compatible with ordinary alternatives concerning burial, testimony, or narrative development.

Strongest feature: The reply directly contests both historical establishment and explanatory superiority by questioning independent corroboration and preserving ordinary alternatives to the empty-tomb narrative. Principal limitation: Its circularity concern can overgeneralize relations among distinct New Testament texts whose genre, dating, dependence, and evidential value require individual assessment. Live burden: The critique must analyze particular sources and natural explanations comparatively rather than treating internal religious provenance as automatic disqualification or bare possibility as adequate rebuttal. Locked score: The strong band recognizes sustained contact with the central historical bridge, appropriate attention to source independence, and relevant alternatives while retaining reservations about compressed textual analysis and the evidential development of those competing accounts.

Religious Experience and Conflicting Revelations

20:22–132:06

William Lane Craig — section score 71/100

Immediate experience of God may rationally disclose an encountered reality rather than merely an adopted religious idea.

Strongest feature: The move presents a distinct noninferential route to theistic warrant and modestly claims that immediate encounter can provide knowledge rather than universal demonstration. Principal limitation: It leaves the experience-interpretation distinction, reliability conditions, psychological alternatives, religious disagreement, and the scope of private entitlement largely unspecified. Live burden: The experiential case must explain when a seeming receives prima facie warrant, identify appropriate defeaters, and distinguish divine disclosure from sincere but incompatible interpretations. Locked score: The mixed band reflects a recognizable subsidiary contribution and tempered possibility claim, counterbalanced by limited public evidence, missing qualification, and inadequate engagement with conflicting experiences that materially constrain movement from personal immediacy to warranted supernatural identification.

John Shook — section score 79/100

Profoundly incompatible religious experiences undermine reliable supernatural identification unless a sound method selects among rival revelations.

Strongest feature: The reply directly identifies incompatible religious reports as a defeater for unrestricted experiential reliability and invokes consistency as a necessary constraint upon supernatural identification. Principal limitation: Disagreement can reduce confidence without proving every claimant mistaken or eliminating all prima facie private warrant, especially where experiences differ in evidential quality. Live burden: The critique must formulate a parity or selection argument, distinguish public truth from initial personal entitlement, and explain how conflict affects differently corroborated experiences. Locked score: The strong band reflects clear, relevant, charitable pressure on the experiential route and strong logical coherence, tempered by slightly overstated force and the need for a more discriminating account of when disagreement defeats rather than merely weakens experiential warrant.
