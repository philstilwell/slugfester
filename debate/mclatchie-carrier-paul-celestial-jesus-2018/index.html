Overall assessment

Jonathan McLatchie — Pauline historicity: 80/100

Published strengths

  • The immediate Corinthian context strongly supported identifying the crucifying rulers as human authorities.
  • The mixed-agency qualification preserved spiritual participation without surrendering the grammatical case for human rulers.
  • Specific Pauline biographical markers materially challenged any claim of complete terrestrial silence.
  • The Acts and Gospel arguments supplied concrete correspondences rather than relying only on generalized source confidence.

Published weaknesses

  • The corpus challenge relied on criticism of consensus formation without supplying representative evidence authenticating the disputed letters.
  • The biological-brother case depended substantially on later family traditions whose independence from Paul's usage was not established.
  • The Acts defense reconciled Arabia but left the Judean-recognition and Gentile-mission discrepancies substantially unanswered.
  • The Gospel-coincidence case assumed independence without adequately comparing literary adaptation or selection effects.

Richard Carrier — Celestial mythicism: 83/100

Published strengths

  • The thesis clearly separated Jesus's mortal embodiment from the disputed location of his death and resurrection.
  • The Pauline-silence argument identified numerous concrete absences while restricting its textual corpus explicitly.
  • The rulers argument connected concealed divine wisdom with hostile cosmic agents and directly challenged a terrestrial inference.
  • The source-separation objections sharply distinguished Paul's language from later Acts and Gospel traditions.
  • Specific conflicts between Acts and Paul's letters placed substantial pressure on companion authorship and broad reliability.

Published weaknesses

  • The silence argument did not sufficiently compare epistolary expectations or integrate indirect terrestrial markers.
  • The heavenly-counterpart argument moved too quickly from general cosmological copies to a copied crucifixion.
  • The Acts characterization was broader than the concrete evidence initially supplied and did not itself establish pervasive unreliability.
  • The Johannine dependence and character-selection accounts were asserted with greater certainty than their passage-level demonstration supported.

The Rulers and the Realm of Crucifixion

Jonathan McLatchie — section score 90/100

Strongest feature: McLatchie grounds the rulers' identity in the immediate argumentative environment of First Corinthians rather than isolated vocabulary, connecting worldly wisdom with recognizably human social actors and authorities. Principal limitation: Contextual predominance does not make an exclusively human referent necessary, particularly where Paul's theology can associate earthly structures with spiritual powers or layered agency. Live burden: He must explain how the local contrast controls the disputed phrase, address cosmic readings of rulers elsewhere, and show why human identity materially locates the execution on Earth. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise contextual reasoning, direct responsiveness, calibrated probabilistic force, and exceptional motion relevance, while leaving a limited but important interpretive opening for spiritual participation or overlapping referents.

Richard Carrier — section score 82/100

Carrier argues that hostile demonic rulers, ignorant of God's concealed salvific plan, fit Paul's crucifixion passage better than generally obedient earthly authorities.

Strongest feature: Carrier supplies a focused theological mechanism linking the rulers' ignorance, hostile motivation, and God's concealed plan, thereby directly challenging their use as an unambiguous terrestrial marker. Principal limitation: The contrast with Romans 13 does not exclude corrupt human authorities, exceptional misconduct, or coordinated human and spiritual agency, so the proposed incongruity cannot independently decide identity. Live burden: He must show that Paul's immediate Corinthian context and usage favor demonic referents specifically, rather than merely demonstrating that a cosmic interpretation remains possible. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, stable terminology, interpretive clarity, and considerable theological coherence, while retaining a qualification for overstated exclusivity and the unresolved alternatives of human corruption or mixed agency.

James and the Brothers of the Lord

33:47–39:29

Jonathan McLatchie — section score 81/100

McLatchie combines Paul's references to James and the Lord's brothers with later traditions naming James and grouping Jesus's mother beside his brothers.

Strongest feature: McLatchie offers a cumulative biological interpretation in which James's name, plural brothers, and the later mother-and-brothers grouping mutually reinforce an ordinary familial reading. Principal limitation: The convergence combines Paul's earlier wording with later Gospel and Acts traditions without establishing their independence, preservation history, or direct relevance to what Paul understood. Live burden: He must show that Pauline usage itself favors biological kinship and then justify treating later family narratives as corroboration rather than dependent elaboration or retrospective identification. Locked score: The strong band reflects a clear, responsive, and intuitively powerful familial pattern, while accounting for the unproven source bridge and the possibility that later narratives cannot determine the earlier phrase's meaning alone.

Richard Carrier — section score 83/100

Carrier argues that Paul's baptismal kinship language permits “brother of the Lord” to identify James as an ordinary cultic brother rather than Jesus's biological sibling.

Strongest feature: Carrier identifies an internally Pauline semantic alternative, distinguishing apostles, ordinary baptized Christians, cultic brethren, and biological relatives while appropriately concluding that the expression remains unclear. Principal limitation: General Christian brotherhood does not automatically explain the marked construction attached to James, nor why this designation would distinguish him from other believers in context. Live burden: He must establish the title's Pauline usage, explain its selective application, and show that a cultic reading matches the syntax and discourse better than biological kinship. Locked score: The strong band recognizes direct responsiveness, stable distinctions, explicit uncertainty, and a plausible ambiguity argument, while preserving the unresolved explanatory burden created by James's specifically marked identification.

Principal limitation: One correspondence cannot independently establish both substantial accuracy and companion authorship, especially without excluding direct literary dependence, coincidence, selection effects, or reconstruction from Pauline materials. Live burden: He must demonstrate that the fit is genuinely indirect, multiply attested, improbable under source use, and representative enough to support conclusions extending beyond the particular episode. Locked score: The strong band recognizes specificity, relevance, and meaningful contrary evidence, while reflecting only partial responsiveness, an implicit bridge between separate conclusions, and insufficient comparative analysis of dependence and background frequency.

Principal limitation: “Fictionalized history” covers many degrees of alteration, and this opening provides few concrete examples demonstrating that Acts's relevant claims are unreliable or unrecoverable. Live burden: He must specify diagnostic criteria, apply them to chronology and companion claims, and distinguish selective embellishment from the broader unreliability needed to neutralize Acts as corroboration. Locked score: The strong band reflects a coherent, qualified, and directly relevant reliability challenge, while recognizing underdeveloped evidence, broad terminology, and the invalidity of inferring wholesale historical failure merely from theological purpose.
