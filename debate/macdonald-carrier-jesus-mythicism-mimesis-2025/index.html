Overall assessment

Dennis R. MacDonald — Historical Jesus mimesis: 79/100

Published strengths

  • MacDonald repeatedly accepted substantial mythologization while explaining it as transformation of a mortal teacher, thereby engaging rather than evading the central literary evidence.
  • His Josephus argument supplied a specific external passage and received further support from ordinary kinship usage and a cautiously framed Acts-reception hypothesis.
  • The occasional-letter explanation and Paul’s meetings with Cephas and James directly addressed both biographical silence and possible interpersonal access to earlier tradition.
  • The Q-based portrait connected Jesus with Jewish law reform and mortal prophetic models, providing a concrete alternative to an exclusively celestial earliest layer.

Published weaknesses

  • Several arguments moved too quickly from compatibility with a historical teacher to positive historical discrimination, especially the appeals to mortal literary models and reconstructed Q.
  • The Johannine and Papian evidence did not establish the date, independence, or transmission chain necessary for external historical corroboration.

Richard Carrier — Mythicist challenge: 81/100

Published strengths

  • Carrier’s Mosaic-reification reply precisely demonstrated that literary modeling does not uniquely require memories of a historical teacher.
  • His challenges to Josephus addressed kinship, identification, textual transmission, and narrative context, culminating in a concrete ben Damneus alternative.
  • The Pauline case identified the debate’s earliest-evidence asymmetry and supplemented silence with a positive, clearly specified celestial mechanism.
  • Carrier consistently pressed source independence, distinguishing the existence of shared or later material from proof that it preserves an earthly biography.

Published weaknesses

  • The categorical assertion that no corroborating sources exist outran the source-by-source dependency analysis actually supplied in the locked evidence.
  • The later-polemics argument compressed docetism, narrative disagreement, and denial of historical existence into a single category despite their different implications.

Paul, revelation, and traces of an earthly life

9:21–88:42

Dennis R. MacDonald — section score 81/100

Paul’s law-centered theology requires a mortal crucified Jesus, while his focus on faith and resurrection explains sparse discussion of earthly biography.

Strongest feature: The reply directly addresses both Pauline silence and crucifixion location by integrating letter purpose, Jewish-law controversy, mortality, resurrection, and acknowledged theological mythologization into one coherent historicist explanation. Principal limitation: Mortality does not independently establish an earthly location or human executioners, and claiming Paul had no theological reason for biography overstates the explanatory reach of genre. Live burden: The case must compare expected Pauline references under earthly and celestial hypotheses while demonstrating that law-related crucifixion language specifically anchors the death in recent human history. Locked score: The strong band recognizes broad responsiveness and an intelligible functional explanation, but materially overstrong necessity language and limited textual comparison restrict the argument’s evidentiary warrant.

Richard Carrier — section score 79/100

Paul and other earliest Christian writings principally describe Jesus through revelations and do not clearly locate him in recent earthly history.

Strongest feature: The argument isolates an evidentially central asymmetry: Paul repeatedly discusses revelation while supplying remarkably little unmistakable chronological, geographical, or biographical placement for Jesus. Principal limitation: Its description of the earliest literature as only presenting revelatory encounters generalizes beyond the displayed Corinthian example and underdevelops the competing occasional-letter explanation. Live burden: The inference becomes discriminating only after specifying which earthly references Paul should reasonably have supplied, given his genres, audiences, controversies, and theological purposes. Locked score: The strong band reflects exceptional relevance, intelligible probabilistic framing, and coherent burden contact, moderated by compressed textual coverage and an incompletely demonstrated expectation behind the argument from silence.

Q, Jewish teaching, and kingdom traditions

Strongest feature: The reply presents an early, recognizably Jewish portrait centered on legal interpretation and mortal prophetic models, directly challenging the claim that earliest recoverable Christianity knew only a celestial savior. Principal limitation: It leaves implicit the decisive source-critical qualifications that Q is reconstructed, contested, stratified, and potentially shaped by the same literary development needing explanation. Live burden: The historicist inference requires establishing Q’s early date, independence, and recoverability, then showing why comparison with mortal figures favors remembered biography over literary characterization. Locked score: The strong band reflects clear proposition recovery, central responsiveness, and substantive contrary evidence, tempered by an underdeveloped independence argument and a slightly overstated movement from literary modeling to historicity.

Richard Carrier — section score 84/100

Principal limitation: Establishing compatibility defeats necessity but does not establish comparative probability, chronology, or the actual source from which Matthew’s teaching complex developed. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional precision, restrained modal force, comprehensive contact with the opponent’s inference, and a decisive exposure of non-uniqueness, while leaving the positive mythicist genealogy still to be independently supported.

Gospel mythmaking and literary reification

10:51–79:17

Dennis R. MacDonald — section score 77/100

Mark heavily mythologizes Jesus through mortal epic models, plausibly elevating an underlying Jewish teacher into a hero superior to Hector and Odysseus.

Strongest feature: The response concedes extensive literary construction while offering an intelligible direction of transformation, thereby avoiding the simplistic claim that mythological embellishment itself guarantees complete fictional invention. Principal limitation: Mortal epic models are compatible with either elevating remembered history or manufacturing an earthly protagonist, so their use does not independently discriminate between the rival pathways. Live burden: The historicist model needs residual material more economically explained as teacher memory, together with criteria separating inherited biography from Markan literary architecture and scriptural imitation. Locked score: The strong band reflects charitable engagement, clear literary reasoning, and direct relevance, while the non-exclusive evidential pattern and only partly specified necessity claim keep the conclusion appropriately limited.

Richard Carrier — section score 78/100

The Gospels first portray Jesus as an earthly biographical figure, while alleged outside corroboration derives directly or indirectly from their repeatedly rewritten narratives.

Strongest feature: The move squarely identifies the evidential question surrounding Gospel historicization and challenges opponents to demonstrate genuinely independent corroboration rather than merely later narrative repetition. Principal limitation: Categorical claims that no other sources exist and that outside testimony derives from Gospel myth collapse heterogeneous Pauline, Josephian, interpersonal, and reception traditions without individualized demonstration. Live burden: The argument must establish dependency source by source, with chronological and transmission criteria, while the opposing case must establish both independence and identification with a recent earthly Jesus. Locked score: The strong band recognizes central relevance and a coherent source-critical challenge, but substantial overstatement, missing qualifications, and limited comparative evidence constrain its evidentiary force.

Josephus, James, and independent attestation

Principal limitation: It treats disputed wording, kinship meaning, textual transmission, and identification as substantially settled while leaving the Mosaic-law extension less directly supported by the cited excerpt. Live burden: External attestation requires showing that the relevant phrase is authentic, identifies the Christian Jesus, uses biological kinship, and remains independent of Christian interpretive influence. Locked score: The strong band reflects unusually direct burden contact, precise citation, and potentially consequential evidence, moderated by missing qualifications and unresolved alternatives involving cultic brotherhood, interpolation, or another Jesus.

Richard Carrier — section score 84/100

Principal limitation: Terminological movement among brother of Christ, Jesus Christ, and Jesus called Christ risks obscuring which transmitted wording and historical explanation the argument specifically addresses. Live burden: A defeater must remain textually and historically credible, while the historicist reading must show that ordinary identification, transmission history, and narrative context jointly favor biological kinship. Locked score: The strong band reflects direct burden contact, calibrated modal force, and consequential source criticism, with a limitation for unstable wording and the still-unquantified plausibility of the proposed alternatives.
