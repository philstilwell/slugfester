Question and assessment context

Overall assessment

John Lennox — Christian theism: 75/100

Published strengths

  • Clearly identified the resurrection as the indispensable bridge from general theism to the biblical God.
  • Directly corrected the characterization of Christian faith as necessarily evidenceless belief.
  • Presented an integrated worldview connecting intelligibility, cognition, value, purpose, solidarity, and hope.
  • Clarified that stable natural laws do not logically exclude exceptional divine agency.

Published weaknesses

  • The cognition argument overreached from possible fitness-truth divergence to global epistemic unreliability without defeating correlation-based evolutionary accounts.
  • The morality case treated unguided origins as though they exhausted secular metaethical alternatives and did not answer rational moral realism.
  • The suffering replies offered solidarity and compensation more successfully than an explanation of divine permission.

Peter Singer — Secular naturalism: 86/100

Published strengths

  • Supplied evolution and shared ancestry as a specific, well-supported explanation of apparent biological design.
  • Used first-cause parity with appropriately limited possibility language and a clear demand for principled causal asymmetry.
  • Isolated natural and animal suffering that human free will does not explain.
  • Presented rational impartiality as a serious secular account of objective moral reasons.
  • Directly challenged resurrection evidence, scriptural authority, divine compensation, and claims that secular life lacks hope or purpose.

Published weaknesses

  • The initial definition of faith excluded evidence-responsive religious trust and therefore missed the actual historical-evidence dispute.
  • Cultural contingency raised a legitimate evidential burden but did not itself evaluate whether the Christian evidence met that burden.
  • Some criticism of New Testament authority moved too quickly between disputed moral teachings, failed expectations, and conclusions about divinity.

Science, Origins, Design, and Rational Mind

7:02–30:31

John Lennox — section score 75/100

The historical emergence of modern science and nature's apparent fine-tuning support belief that an intelligible universe originates in a rational creator.

Strongest feature: The move directly connects expected natural regularity, scientific intelligibility, and the proposed rational source of nature, thereby contacting a central explanatory bridge with a historically recognizable and philosophically relevant line of support. Principal limitation: Its historical generalization is insufficiently qualified, the fine-tuning component receives little representative development, and neither consideration is systematically compared with naturalistic explanations that may also accommodate orderly investigation. Live burden: The case still needs specific fine-tuning evidence, an account of the relevant probability space, and a careful demonstration that theism explains scientific intelligibility more successfully than competing accounts without merely redescribing it. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects substantial relevance and coherent explanatory direction alongside noticeably incomplete evidential development, restricted alternative testing, and slightly overstated inferential force.

Peter Singer — section score 89/100

Science does not establish creation from nothing, and any causal exemption granted to God may also leave room for an uncreated universe.

Strongest feature: The reply precisely distinguishes a cosmological beginning from creation out of nothing and identifies the explanatory asymmetry when God, unlike reality itself, receives exemption from causal requirements. Principal limitation: It develops causal parity more completely than the fine-tuning consideration and leaves unresolved whether a necessary creator and an uncreated physical universe have comparable explanatory characteristics. Live burden: The secular case must extend logical possibility into explanation by identifying a natural cosmology and showing why the necessary-being distinction either fails or contributes no explanatory advantage. Locked score: The very strong band reflects scope, restrained possibility language, direct engagement with a creator inference, and charitable recognition of the theistic distinction, alongside bounded incompleteness about fine-tuning and constructive cosmology.

Objective Morality, Human Worth, and Purpose

13:46–95:26

John Lennox — section score 72/100

Objective morality, equal human worth, and freedom are presented as more coherently grounded in humanity's creation in God's image than in unguided processes.

Strongest feature: The move addresses the foundation of objective value and equal human standing, presenting divine image-bearing as a metaphysical explanation rather than accidental preference. Principal limitation: It shifts among morality, equality, freedom, and political atrocity without distinguishing their inferences, while the illustration cannot establish that rejecting God necessarily eliminates value or liberty. Live burden: The argument must explain how divine creation generates binding moral reasons and equal status, then compare that mechanism with secular moral realism instead of presenting mindless biological origins as the only alternative. Locked score: The mixed band preserves unmistakable motion relevance and an intelligible positive proposal, while recognizing underdeveloped warrant, unstable conceptual transitions, overstated necessity, and limited charity toward the available secular grounding account.

Peter Singer — section score 87/100

Rational impartiality can support objective morality without God, including the judgment that trivial amusement cannot justify substantially greater suffering imposed on another.

Strongest feature: The move rebuts divine necessity by presenting rational impartiality as a secular source of objective reasons, illustrated through the asymmetry between amusement and substantial suffering. Principal limitation: A philosophical defense and one example establish possibility more readily than a metaethical ontology or comprehensive account of equal moral standing for humans and nonhuman animals. Live burden: The position must clarify what makes impartial reasons objectively authoritative, how agents acquire epistemic access to them, and why neither explanation requires a divine foundation. Locked score: The very strong 85–94 band recognizes precise limited force, direct responsiveness, coherent reasoning, representative support, and charitable engagement with the ontological dispute, while preserving bounded incompleteness about metaphysical grounding and secular moral equality.

Jesus, Resurrection, and Scriptural Reliability

17:34–100:00

John Lennox — section score 81/100

The claimed historical resurrection of Jesus is the crucial evidential bridge from general creator arguments to rational belief in the specifically biblical God.

Strongest feature: The move clearly identifies the indispensable bridge from generic theism to Christianity, states the resurrection claim in testable historical terms, and candidly recognizes that the Christian conclusion depends decisively upon it. Principal limitation: It describes resurrection as historical fact and draws sweeping consequences without presenting representative testimony, source dating, independence analysis, or a comparative evaluation of ordinary and extraordinary alternative explanations. Live burden: The case must provide a disciplined historical argument showing why the relevant records are reliable and why resurrection explains them better than legend, error, visionary experience, or other natural accounts. Locked score: The strong/competent band recognizes exceptional relevance, stable conditional structure, and a clearly articulated evidential destination, while retaining substantial reservations about unsupported certainty, incomplete historical warrant, and absent testing of developed skeptical alternatives.

Peter Singer — section score 88/100

The available resurrection case relies on documents and testimony that are too late, dependent, and uncertain to establish an extraordinary supernatural event.

Strongest feature: The reply redirects the exchange from miraculous possibility toward historical warrant, emphasizing testimony's lateness, slenderness, reliability, and the demanding evidential threshold created by conflict with established natural regularities. Principal limitation: Its well-directed skepticism remains incompletely documented, because it does not examine particular texts, dependencies, traditions, reported appearances, legendary-development possibilities, or the strongest cumulative resurrection case. Live burden: The argument must transparently compare ordinary explanations with resurrection across shared historical standards, demonstrating superior explanatory performance without presupposing supernatural causation impossible. Locked score: The very strong band recognizes decisive motion-level relevance, disciplined evidential calibration, and comprehensive responsiveness to Christianity's indispensable bridge, while retaining limitations for absent source-by-source substantiation and insufficiently explicit comparative analysis.

Suffering, Hope, Compensation, and Justice

34:49–85:40

John Lennox — section score 72/100

Christian belief begins a response to moral and natural evil through Jesus and offers enduring hope that suffering and death are not ultimate.

Strongest feature: The move distinguishes moral from natural evil and presents Christ-centered hope as existentially meaningful, while modestly characterizing this contribution as a beginning, not a completed theodicy. Principal limitation: Consolation and eschatological restoration never explain why an omnipotent, knowledgeable, benevolent God permits catastrophic natural and animal suffering, and the Christological mechanism remains unspecified. Live burden: The response must demonstrate how divine solidarity, restoration, or greater goods provide justification alongside endurance, while acknowledging secular practical, interpersonal, and future-directed hope. Locked score: The mixed band reflects relevant distinctions, proportionate asserted force, and meaningful Christian contribution, yet also partial recoverability, explanatory incompleteness, limited responsiveness to the decisive compatibility challenge, and an overgeneralized portrayal of atheism as hopeless.

Peter Singer — section score 87/100

Extensive undeserved natural and evolutionary suffering is difficult to reconcile with an omniscient, omnipotent, wholly good creator and escapes standard free-will explanations.

Strongest feature: The argument isolates natural and nonhuman suffering with precision, thereby bypassing the standard appeal to human freedom and presenting a powerful evidential challenge to the conjunction of divine knowledge, power, and goodness. Principal limitation: The representative evidence is compelling but not systematically quantified, and the move cannot by itself exclude every developed account involving broader goods, creaturely development, regular natural systems, or eventual restoration. Live burden: The challenge must compare the scale, distribution, and apparent gratuity of suffering against specific theistic explanations, asking whether their proposed goods plausibly required costs of this magnitude and duration. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects excellent coherence, calibration, charity, and direct contact with a central burden, while preserving measured reservations about empirical breadth and the incomplete comparative treatment of sophisticated theodicies.

Faith, Cultural Contingency, and Explanatory Burden

30:29–73:54

John Lennox — section score 76/100

Christian faith is distinguished from blind belief as an evidence-based commitment grounded particularly in Gospel testimony and Jesus's claimed resurrection.

Strongest feature: The reply directly rejects the stipulated definition of faith, concedes the danger of blind commitment, and identifies Gospel testimony and resurrection as the concrete evidence on which Christian trust purportedly rests. Principal limitation: Successfully distinguishing faith from evidenceless belief does not demonstrate that the identified evidence is sufficient, independent, early, or reliable enough to warrant the extraordinary historical and theological conclusions attached to it. Live burden: The defense must move beyond labeling Christianity evidence-based and assess authorship, dating, transmission, corroboration, alternative explanations, and the appropriate standard for a resurrection claim. Locked score: The strong/competent band recognizes exceptional responsiveness, conceptual clarity, stable qualification, and fair acknowledgment of the opponent's legitimate concern, while limiting credit because the underlying historical warrant remains announced rather than substantially demonstrated within this move.

Peter Singer — section score 72/100

Faith cannot substitute for rational argument or good evidence, and belief insulated from responsible inquiry can cause grave practical harm.

Strongest feature: The argument presents a defensible evidentialist requirement and illustrates how confidence disconnected from responsible investigation can produce consequential harm, making its epistemological concern pertinent to religious commitment. Principal limitation: Its categorical characterization of faith as evidenceless belief excludes the Christian conception of evidence-responsive trust, overgeneralizes from the illustration, and never evaluates the opponent's resurrection evidence. Live burden: The criticism must differentiate unsupported commitment from insufficiently supported commitment, then demonstrate why Gospel testimony and resurrection claims fail appropriate historical standards instead of prevailing definitionally. Locked score: The mixed band preserves the coherent evidential principle and practical illustration while recognizing unstable terminology, disproportionate generalization, restricted interpretive charity, and nonengagement with the targeted case's decisive historical component.
