Question and assessment context

Is science a better model for free inquiry than philosophy?

Public English captions were checked across the authorized formal debate rounds of the World Humanist Congress, Oxford recording published by Humanists UK, from 2014. Selected passages were checked against the recording; material caption limitations are documented in the evidence record. Short quotations retain the locked caption wording; argument cards are faithful condensations. Only the opening statements and first rebuttals (3:01–28:50) are assessed. Overall assessment

Peter Atkins — Scientific inquiry has primacy: 65/100

Published strengths

  • Atkins consistently identifies evidential correction and cross-field integration as concrete mechanisms behind science’s cumulative and publicly checkable progress.
  • His rebuttal properly resists turning unresolved origins questions into prematurely permanent restrictions on continued scientific investigation into fundamental origins.
  • He names specific empirical resources—anthropology, animal behavior, evolution, and consequences—that genuinely illuminate moral practice’s origins and deliberative components without establishing normative justification.

Published weaknesses

  • He repeatedly substitutes sweeping ridicule of philosophers for comparative evidence, weakening fairness and obscuring distinctions among philosophical methods.
  • His moral reply explains belief origins and deliberative inputs but does not answer Law’s separate question about normative justification.

Stephen Law — Philosophical inquiry remains indispensable: 82/100

Published strengths

  • Law concedes science’s empirical authority, then narrows the disagreement to explanatory, normative, and conceptual questions without constructing a false rivalry.
  • His river and Galileo examples demonstrate how distinctions and contradiction tests can perform substantive work without additional observation.
  • His final moral rebuttal precisely separates causal explanations of belief from reasons establishing whether a moral position is justified.

Published weaknesses

  • The mirror solution and mind-body obstacle are announced rather than developed, leaving their claimed philosophical necessity incompletely demonstrated.
  • His examples show conceptual reasoning resolving ambiguity and refuting a theory, but establish neither professional philosophy’s exclusive ownership of those methods nor institutional indispensability.
