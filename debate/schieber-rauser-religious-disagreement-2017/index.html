Overall assessment

Justin Schieber — Disagreement is evidence against God: 85/100

Published strengths

  • Schieber consistently framed disagreement as defeasible comparative evidence, preserving a modest burden and expressly allowing other theistic arguments to outweigh it.
  • His distinction between God causing contradictions and God failing to correct sincere consequential errors neutralized Rauser’s persistent causal reconstruction.
  • The shift from generic supernatural belief to independently convergent content-rich doctrines gave the convergence argument a precise observable comparison.

Published weaknesses

  • The independent-convergence counterfactual did not itself establish the likelihood ratio needed to show that actual disagreement positively favors atheism.
  • Several serious-harm examples remained illustrative; their prevalence, causal representativeness, and net balance against developmental goods were not established.

Randal Rauser — Disagreement does not count against God: 78/100

Published strengths

  • Rauser supplied coherent permissive reasons—formation, staged teaching, freedom, accommodation, and fair judgment—instead of relying entirely on bare possibility.
  • His propositional-knowledge and acquaintance distinction identified a relationship dimension that Schieber’s doctrinal emphasis could not simply ignore.
  • The closing separation of hypothetical support from actual-world evidential direction exposed a genuine missing step in Schieber’s counterfactual argument.

Published weaknesses

  • Rauser repeatedly treated the analogy as divine delivery of incompatible messages after Schieber located the problem in uncorrected human misunderstanding.
  • His natural-convergence proposal moved from recurring biological forms and broad belief to detailed doctrine without evidence for that transfer.

From fundamental disagreement to evidence

12:39–22:38

Justin Schieber — section score 84/100

Fundamental incompatible beliefs among devout believers resemble tenants receiving conflicting private messages, creating relational tension that a truth-valuing God would seemingly address.

Strongest feature: The landlord analogy concretely narrows the target to fundamental conflicts among willing believers and links confusion with impaired divine and interpersonal relationships. Principal limitation: It leaves partly implicit why divine communication inherits a landlord’s clarification duty, while illustrative incompatibilities establish neither prevalence nor representative distribution. Live burden: Schieber must show consequential disagreement is appreciably less expected under relational theism than atheism after admitting plausible goods from limited uncertainty. Locked score: 81/100 reflects a lucid, relevant, and carefully bounded opening whose landlord analogy clarifies the relational concern, while the missing comparative baseline and unmeasured distribution of conflict keep its evidential warrant suggestive rather than independently demonstrated.

Randal Rauser — section score 76/100

Rauser separates unequal divine disclosure from contradictory revelation and demands evidence that the observed amount and distribution of disagreement exceed what God could permissibly allow.

Strongest feature: Rauser isolates authorship and degree as different questions, thereby pressing the analogy’s transfer and the evidential baseline at genuinely vulnerable points. Principal limitation: His first objection targets a clarification Schieber expressly supplies, while requiring proof that God has no sufficient reason converts a modest likelihood claim into an impossibility burden. Live burden: Rauser must explain why sincere, consequential, persistent error is reasonably expected under divine relationship rather than merely compatible with an unidentified purpose. Locked score: 76/100 rewards a degree challenge that materially sharpens the debate and correctly requests an evidential baseline, while reducing credit because the causal reconstruction misses Schieber’s clarification and the proposed burden sometimes exceeds what a defeasible comparison requires.

Possible reasons to permit disagreement

23:06–32:52

Justin Schieber — section score 86/100

Schieber reverses the team-building analogy: cooperation fails when participants receive incompatible goals, rules, and deadlines, so fundamental religious confusion calls for top-down clarification.

Strongest feature: The reconstructed exercise shows disagreement about the shared task disabling cooperation instead of cultivating it, and its rules-goals-deadlines contrast makes that reversal unusually transparent. Principal limitation: It assumes religions lack enough common purpose for collaboration and does not establish that divine guidance must resemble an instructor’s explicit briefing, especially where traditions share broad moral aims despite doctrinal differences. Live burden: Schieber must connect the model to actual fundamental disputes while allowing that partial shared moral aims and guidance may survive. Locked score: 86/100 credits a transparent and forceful reversal of Rauser’s team-building example with excellent direct engagement, while withholding further credit because the analogy’s fit with actual religious communities and their surviving common purposes remains insufficiently established.

Randal Rauser — section score 76/100

Rauser proposes that interpretive tension can build character and that a master teacher may provide developmentally fitted instruction, making some disagreement compatible with divine pedagogy.

Strongest feature: Rauser supplies two intelligible mechanisms—cooperative formation and staged teaching—rather than resting on a bare assertion that God might possess unknown reasons. Principal limitation: The grammar lesson concerns progressively expanded instruction, whereas disputed doctrines can contradict one another about shared goals, and no evidence connects their distribution to developmental readiness. Live burden: He must show these pedagogical benefits arise from consequential disagreements and plausibly outweigh persistent false belief’s relational costs. Locked score: 77/100 reflects clear and relevant mechanisms offered with appropriately limited confidence, while the loose mapping from progressive classroom instruction to mutually incompatible fundamental doctrines prevents these analogies from adequately explaining the most consequential conflicts in the discussion.

Truth, salvation and kinds of relationship

33:33–44:20

Justin Schieber — section score 85/100

Schieber reiterates that believers sincerely interpret incompatible claims as revelation and argues that an instructor should correct fundamental goal-confusion even without causing it.

Strongest feature: The reply cleanly separates responsibility for producing error from reasons to correct it, preserving the evidential question after conceding that God need not communicate contradictions. Principal limitation: Its instructor model assumes divine purposes prioritize explicit common goals and gives no independent measure of clarification expected amid freedom or cultural mediation. Live burden: Schieber must translate the corrective intuition into a comparative prediction about actual kinds, stakes, and persistence of religious misunderstanding. Locked score: 86/100 rewards an exceptionally precise, responsive, and charitable distinction between causing error and correcting it, while the absence of an independently defended prediction about how purposeful divine communication should correct sincere communities over time limits the ultimate comparison.

Randal Rauser — section score 79/100

Rauser argues that bare theism need not entail exclusivist salvation: an inclusivist God may judge people by their evidence and hearts despite missing detailed doctrines.

Strongest feature: Rauser sharply distinguishes bare theism from exclusivism and presents a coherent available-evidence model that blocks necessary eternal loss from doctrinal error. Principal limitation: Schieber’s case does not depend on exclusivism, and the reply leaves untouched truth, conflict, correction, and the comparative richness of informed relationship, including conflict among believers and diminished awareness of the divine person. Live burden: Rauser must show that inclusivist fairness explains divine permission of consequential false belief, rather than only preventing unjust punishment for it. Locked score: 77/100 credits a logically coherent and clearly expressed correction about the scope of bare theism, while the reply’s narrow focus on salvation, overstatement of Schieber’s dependence, and failure to address non-salvific relationship costs reduce responsiveness and charity.

Independent doctrinal convergence

41:53–55:36

Justin Schieber — section score 84/100

Schieber imagines isolated populations independently reaching the same detailed doctrines, argues that such convergence would favor theism, and infers that actual disagreement favors atheism.

Strongest feature: The independent-continent thought experiment offers a crisp comparative test and explicitly frames the reasoning as abductive evidence rather than contradiction or disproof. Principal limitation: Showing one counterfactual would favor God does not determine actual disagreement’s evidential sign; likelihoods, base rates, independence, and alternative explanations remain unspecified, and the thought experiment does not determine whether the actual observation is neutral or negative. Live burden: Schieber must show the observed world is less probable under theism than atheism, not merely less favorable than imagined convergence. Locked score: 82/100 rewards a clear abductive construction that directly advances the motion, while the absence of likelihoods conditioned on culture, cognition, divine aims, and competing mechanisms leaves the crucial step from imagined convergence to actual atheistic evidence incomplete.

Randal Rauser — section score 79/100

Rauser compares recurring biological forms across isolated lineages with possible theological convergence, arguing that agreement patterns alone cannot reliably distinguish theism from atheism.

Strongest feature: Rauser introduces an alternative mechanism and cautiously concludes underdetermination, challenging the assumption that independent agreement uniquely indicates intelligent revelation. Principal limitation: Similar biological structures do not demonstrate selection toward content-rich doctrine, and the analogy shifts from detailed convergence to broad agreement without a supported bridge, especially for doctrines with no articulated connection to reproductive or social advantage. Live burden: Rauser must identify credible pressures predicting specific doctrines or show the atheistic likelihood is high enough to neutralize Schieber’s comparison. Locked score: 76/100 credits a relevant, calibrated modal challenge and a recognizable alternative mechanism, while the unsupported transfer from biological convergence to stable agreement on content-rich theology leaves the central evidential warrant speculative.

Closing scope and cumulative force

56:31–1:01:02

Justin Schieber — section score 88/100

Schieber characterizes disagreement as one significant but defeasible consideration within a cumulative worldview comparison, explicitly allowing stronger theistic evidence and denying deductive disproof.

Strongest feature: Schieber states the burden with exceptional discipline: the argument contributes weight, may be outweighed, and neither disproves God nor makes theism automatically irrational. Principal limitation: This scope clarification adds little evidence for the claimed significance and relies on earlier moves to establish the disagreement consideration’s magnitude, leaving the word significant dependent on unresolved earlier probability comparisons. Live burden: The pro side must justify that magnitude after underdetermination, permissive reasons, and competing evidence are jointly considered. Locked score: 88/100 reflects exceptional precision, calibration, and charity in separating one argument’s significant local force from a complete worldview verdict, while acknowledging that the preceding exchange rather than this scope statement must establish the claimed evidential magnitude.

Randal Rauser — section score 79/100

Rauser concedes serious Christian divisions, suggests providential ambiguity can promote charitable inquiry, and cites ecumenical listening as relational value realized through continued disagreement.

Strongest feature: Rauser treats the objection seriously, names concrete disputes, and grounds a proposed benefit in ecumenical experience, producing a charitable and limited final response. Principal limitation: Dialogue goods and possible providential reasons do not justify the observed amount or consequences of ambiguity, nor answer every truth-based corrective challenge, particularly where ambiguity concerns initiation, sacrament, or salvation rather than harmless diversity. Live burden: Rauser must show these benefits make fundamental division expected under God rather than merely compatible with permission. Locked score: 79/100 credits a serious concession, concrete relational experience, and notably charitable final posture, while the move offers no distributional account showing why dialogue goods justify the hardest persistent conflicts and does not resolve the earlier truth-based correction burden.
