Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Christian theist: 78/100

Published strengths

  • The cosmological and fine-tuning arguments supplied clear, structurally central explanations and explicitly identified several competing causal possibilities.
  • The resurrection inference gave the cumulative case a specifically Christian bridge rather than leaving the conclusion at generic creator theism.
  • Craig carefully distinguished logical from probabilistic evil and distinguished subjective projects from the objective purpose claimed by his worldview.
  • His truth-versus-social-consequences reply correctly blocked any simple deduction from beneficial or harmful effects to a worldview's truth.

Published weaknesses

  • The opening burden contention exaggerated the historical failure of atheistic argument and risked substituting absence of disproof for affirmative support.
  • Several indispensable transitions were asserted too strongly: timeless cause to personhood, objective morality to God, and selected historical facts to resurrection.
  • The reply to suffering answered logical compatibility more successfully than comparative probability and left apparently gratuitous horror insufficiently explained.

Christopher Hitchens — Atheist critic: 82/100

Published strengths

  • Hitchens sharply clarified that rejecting an unsupported divine hypothesis does not require proving the nonexistence of every proposed deity.
  • The creator-to-Christianity gap and miracle-authentication reply precisely isolated indispensable inferential bridges that cosmology or extraordinary performance cannot complete alone.
  • Evolutionary waste, delayed revelation, divine silence, and prolonged suffering formed a cumulative counterevidential challenge to competent benevolent providence.
  • His secular morality and meaning arguments established substantial human ethical capacity, solidarity, and practical purpose without revealed authority.

Published weaknesses

  • The claim that all observable phenomena are explicable without God exceeded the evidence presented and was broader than his well-defended burden principle required.
  • Secular moral capacity and historical priority challenged dependence on revelation but did not fully answer the ontological question of objective moral grounding.
  • The account of secular meaning demonstrated lived and intersubjective purpose more clearly than it answered Craig's narrower claim about objective cosmic purpose.

Objective Morality and Its Foundation

William Lane Craig — section score 75/100

Strongest feature: Concrete examples make the asserted objectivity of moral value vivid, readily recoverable, and directly relevant to the affirmative's central proposal of a transcendent moral foundation. Principal limitation: The movement from objective moral truths to God's existence is asserted as necessary without explaining why secular realism, natural facts, rational requirements, or other foundations are inadequate. Live burden: Craig must provide the missing grounding principle, distinguish moral epistemology from moral ontology, and comparatively test theistic explanation against serious nontheistic accounts rather than relying on shared condemnation. Locked score: The supplied strong and competent band appropriately recognizes a clear, consequential moral datum and substantial burden contact while preserving the major inferential compression and materially overstated entailment identified in the locked findings.

Live burden: Hitchens must distinguish moral development from moral truth and provide or defend a nontheistic account explaining why ethical judgments possess objective authority beyond adaptive usefulness. Principal limitation: Categorical claims about universal scholarly rejection and the absence of plausible naturalistic explanations exceed the evidence presented, while source independence and report reliability remain substantially unestablished. Live burden: Craig must substantiate the underlying historical facts, compare a sufficiently exhaustive alternative set, incorporate miracle base rates, and explain why resurrection better predicts the evidence. Locked score: The supplied strong and competent band appropriately reflects exceptional structural relevance and a coherent explanatory form while retaining serious limitations in historical warrant, representational charity, exhaustiveness, and calibration.

Live burden: Craig must offer discriminating reasons for permission, address apparently gratuitous horrors and unequal revelation, and avoid treating eventual redemption as sufficient justification for every prior evil.
