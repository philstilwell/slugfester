Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Theism: 77/100

Published strengths

  • Craig repeatedly identifies the precise premise under attack and supplies direct replies rather than relying solely on opening assertions.
  • His marble example gives a vivid, technically relevant reason to distrust exact probabilities manufactured from complete ignorance.
  • The cumulative structure lets cosmology, fine-tuning, morality, resurrection, and experience contribute distinct features to one theistic hypothesis.
  • His distinction between certainty and necessity provides the debate’s clearest analysis of foreknowledge and freedom.

Published weaknesses

  • Craig sometimes treats unverified or compressed evidence, especially the Haitian missionary report, as carrying much greater justificatory weight than its source supports.
  • The move from divine ownership of life to broad permission for suffering remains insufficiently reconciled with the compassion and fairness attributed to God.
  • Several arguments establish at most a creator or supernatural agent before the cumulative bridge to perfect goodness has been independently secured.

Michael Tooley — Atheism: 80/100

Published strengths

  • Tooley consistently preserves the distinction between evidential improbability and strict logical incompatibility throughout his argument from evil.
  • He sharply exposes the gap between evidence for an intelligent creator and evidence for an omniscient, omnipotent, perfectly good God.
  • His actual-infinity response correctly explains why different infinite-set subtractions do not constitute a mathematical contradiction.
  • The irrevocability objection to hell directly addresses a feature left unanswered by describing separation as freely chosen.

Published weaknesses

  • Tooley’s exact probability assignments depend on symmetry, partition, and independence assumptions that his four-case illustration does not adequately establish.
  • He moves invalidly from weaknesses in ontological arguments to the sweeping claim that every necessarily existing temporal person is self-contradictory.
  • His backward-causation objection overlooks Craig’s explicit distinction between logical dependence and efficient causal influence from the future.

Creator, Origin, and Design

11:18–1:59:22

William Lane Craig — section score 80/100

A finite cosmic past and causal principle support a timeless, immaterial, extraordinarily powerful personal origin of spacetime beyond the physical universe itself.

Strongest feature: Craig integrates philosophical objections to an actual infinite past with contemporary cosmological evidence, then carefully limits his causal premise to whatever begins existing and derives several attributes appropriate to a transcendent origin. Principal limitation: Neither the impossibility of an actual temporal infinity nor the interpretation of Big Bang cosmology is established decisively here, and the further transition from an atemporal cause to a personal chooser remains suggestive rather than demonstrative. Live burden: Pro must defend the universe’s absolute beginning and the personal-character inference; con may defeat this move by exposing insufficient support for either bridge without establishing a complete eternal cosmology. Locked score: 76 reflects a structurally ambitious and relevant argument whose evidential premises and final agency bridge remain substantially contestable.

Michael Tooley — section score 83/100

An intelligent immaterial cosmic cause need not be omnipotent, omniscient or good, leaving Craig’s creator arguments short of Tooley’s God, as defined for the central dispute.

Strongest feature: Tooley precisely distinguishes existential conclusions about a designer or first cause from the fuller attribute package at issue, preventing evidential support for some supernatural intelligence from silently becoming support for a perfectly good classical deity. Principal limitation: The criticism evaluates the first two arguments in isolation and initially underweights Craig’s announced cumulative strategy, under which moral and resurrection arguments are intended to supply attributes absent from cosmology and design. Live burden: Pro must show how the cumulative evidence reaches the required attributes; Tooley’s local objection succeeds if creator evidence alone lacks that entailment, without his proving rival creators equally likely after every argument. Locked score: 87 reflects a decisive local objection to overclaiming from creator evidence, tempered by the need to evaluate the complete cumulative case.

Inductive Probability and Property Reasoning

42:09–2:16:48

William Lane Craig — section score 83/100

Craig challenges Tooley’s precise probability result as an artifact of controversial assumptions and arbitrary formal-language choices, when applied to concrete moral evidence.

Strongest feature: Craig identifies the exact inferential hinge rather than disputing the emotional salience of suffering, arguing that an objective greater-than-one-half conclusion cannot rest on probability assignments that vary with arbitrary representational decisions. Principal limitation: Reporting that specialists reject a framework and that alternatives exist is partly an appeal to authority, because the response initially gives neither a worked countermodel nor a precise diagnosis of Tooley’s theorem. Live burden: Pro must exhibit a coherent ignorance model that preserves induction while blocking equiprobability, whereas con must show that its assignments remain invariant under redescriptions and property partitions. Locked score: 75 reflects sharp engagement with the central probabilistic premise but also the incomplete in-debate substantiation of the methodological criticism at this stage.

Michael Tooley — section score 72/100

Symmetric unknown right- and wrong-making properties allegedly make continued overall wrongness more probable, increasingly so across numerous evils considered across independent catastrophic permissions.

Strongest feature: Tooley does not merely appeal to intuition about horrendous suffering; he supplies an explicit four-case model showing how symmetric unknown additions can leave an already negative known balance wrong in three modeled outcomes. Principal limitation: The generalization assumes equal probabilities, comparable weights, independence, and an exhaustive property partition, none of which is secured by the simple illustration and each of which may fail for purposive divine action. Live burden: Con must defend the probability model’s application to morally structured events, while pro must explain which assumption fails and offer a nonarbitrary alternative treatment of ignorance. Locked score: 74 acknowledges valuable formal transparency and cumulative ambition but reflects substantial uncertainty about whether the illustrative theorem represents the real evidential situation.

Moral Reality and Grounding

19:30–1:28:47

William Lane Craig — section score 73/100

Objective moral experience and naturalism’s evolutionary explanation are used to infer a divine ground for binding values and duties that natural selection cannot supply.

Strongest feature: Craig separates an evolutionary explanation of why humans hold moral beliefs from an account of why any moral judgment is objectively true, and he anchors the abstract issue in compelling examples of cruelty. Principal limitation: Showing that evolutionary genealogy does not itself ground moral truth does not eliminate secular realism, constructivism, or other nontheological accounts, so the indispensable divine-ground premise receives less defense than the experiential premise. Live burden: Pro must demonstrate that objective normativity specifically requires personal divine authority; because Tooley affirmatively invokes secular realism, his positive alternative gains force only insofar as at least one such account is substantively credible. Locked score: 74 rewards the argument’s intuitive moral data and clear syllogistic relevance but registers the unsupported exclusivity claim at its decisive inferential step.

Michael Tooley — section score 75/100

Parallel ontological arguments allegedly yield incompatible necessary beings, so Tooley declares necessary temporal personhood incoherent and unusable for moral grounding across every metaphysically possible world.

Strongest feature: Tooley uses vivid parody cases to expose how an unrestricted possibility-to-necessity pattern could license mutually incompatible conclusions, correctly warning that no valid method can establish contradictory necessary beings. Principal limitation: Refuting a particular ontological inference does not establish that every necessarily existing nonabstract person is contradictory, because the relevant possibility premises may differ and no contradiction in necessary personhood itself is derived. Live burden: Con must prove the modal impossibility it later needs for moral truth, while pro need only distinguish coherent divine necessity from the unsupported parody possibilities. Locked score: 55 reflects the argument’s understandable diagnostic force against careless ontological reasoning but marks its major non sequitur from invalid proof to metaphysical impossibility.

Evil, Providence, and Perfection

33:26–2:12:53

William Lane Craig — section score 72/100

Natural and moral suffering may be necessary background for maximizing freely chosen knowledge of God rather than earthly happiness within a providential history extending beyond death.

Strongest feature: Craig supplies a definite higher-order purpose—freely formed relationship with God—and correctly observes that judging gratuitousness requires comparison with feasible worlds, including their long-range consequences for agency, character, and salvation. Principal limitation: The possibility that suffering contributes to optimal salvation does not establish that actual quantities, victims, or distributions are necessary, and the asserted optimization remains unsupported by comparative evidence available within the debate. Live burden: Pro must connect particular severe evils to plausible constraints on freer or less harmful worlds, while con must avoid assuming that any unseen purpose is absent simply because observers cannot identify it. Locked score: 75 recognizes a relevant undercutting explanation with real modal force, while withholding stronger credit because optimality and proportionality are asserted rather than demonstrated.

Michael Tooley — section score 83/100

Extensive natural, moral, animal, and bodily suffering joins hiddenness and conditional scriptural horrors as evidence against perfect goodness across human history and religious traditions.

Strongest feature: Tooley assembles diverse kinds of suffering while carefully marking claims about biblical violence and hell as conditional upon scriptural truth, which preserves the argument against both secular and Christian responses without confusing their evidential status. Principal limitation: A broad catalogue establishes severity and variety but does not yet demonstrate gratuitousness, and several cases require separate analysis because natural suffering, human wrongdoing, hiddenness, and alleged revelation invite importantly different explanations. Live burden: Con must connect representative cases to the probabilistic inference that adequate divine reasons are unlikely, while pro must address the actual distribution rather than offering only abstract logical possibilities. Locked score: 81 reflects substantial evidential breadth and commendable conditional precision, offset by the preliminary nature of the catalogue before its inferential bridge is supplied.

Resurrection and Miracle Evidence

23:40–2:05:54

William Lane Craig — section score 79/100

The empty tomb, reported appearances, and transformed resurrection belief are presented as historical facts best explained by divine action within first-century Jewish religious expectations.

Strongest feature: Craig offers a compact abductive structure built from three independently intelligible historical claims and emphasizes that an adequate explanation must jointly account for the empty tomb, appearances, and sudden resurrection-centered conviction. Principal limitation: The debate largely treats those facts and the inadequacy of naturalistic explanations as settled, although their evidential independence, source reliability, and comparative likelihoods require substantial defense before resurrection becomes the best explanation. Live burden: Pro must establish the historical data and compare resurrection against serious natural hypotheses; con can block the inference by undermining a pivotal fact or comparative claim without furnishing a complete explanation of the entire cluster. Locked score: 74 reflects a coherent and directly relevant historical argument whose crucial factual establishment and comparative-explanation work remain significantly abbreviated.

Michael Tooley — section score 85/100

A genuine resurrection would indicate supernatural action, but identifying its agent as the perfectly good, omnipotent, omniscient God requires further evidence.

Strongest feature: Tooley isolates the indispensable identification premise between extraordinary historical agency and the classical divine attributes, preventing the evidential force of an alleged resurrection from silently carrying conclusions about unlimited power, exhaustive knowledge, and perfect goodness. Principal limitation: The objection initially overlooks how a miracle’s historical and religious setting may identify its intended agent and communicate vindication, leaving Craig room to argue from Jesus’ claims, execution, and Israelite context. Live burden: Pro must show that this context supports the defined God beyond an unspecified supernatural cause; con can reasonably question that identification without supplying an equally detailed alternative agent. Locked score: 88 reflects an exceptionally clear correction of inferential overreach that remains answerable through additional contextual evidence.

Religious Experience and Revelation

27:20–1:33:33

William Lane Craig — section score 71/100

Immediate personal awareness of God may rationally ground belief without inference, provided its undefeated experiential warrant survives challenges from rival culturally conditioned experiences.

Strongest feature: Craig identifies a legitimate epistemic category in which some beliefs are rationally basic rather than inferred, thereby explaining how personal acquaintance could matter even when public arguments remain inconclusive. Principal limitation: The analogy with perception and memory is asserted more than defended, and the account initially lacks criteria distinguishing veridical divine awareness from culturally conditioned, psychologically generated, or mutually incompatible religious experiences. Live burden: Pro must specify reliable markers and answer concrete defeaters, while con must show that disagreement or correlation defeats this claimant’s warrant rather than automatically discrediting every immediate experience. Locked score: 70 credits a philosophically recognizable route to personal justification but reflects its limited public evidential reach and underdeveloped reliability conditions.

Michael Tooley — section score 81/100

Conflicting religious experiences and their close dependence on prior formation undermine their reliability as objective divine disclosure rather than products of enculturation.

Strongest feature: Tooley offers two distinct defeaters—mutually incompatible experiential contents and strong cultural correlation—and charitably allows initial justification before arguing that later awareness of disagreement can undermine confidence. Principal limitation: Disagreement does not automatically defeat every experience, and the asserted strength of cultural dependence is illustrated through examples rather than systematic evidence or comparison with ordinary perception under disagreement. Live burden: Con must show that these patterns are expected more strongly on psychological formation than genuine revelation, while pro must supply discriminating evidence for Christian experience instead of asserting asymmetric defeaters. Locked score: 84 rewards a clear, directly responsive, and appropriately probabilistic critique, with modest deduction for empirical claims that remain largely undocumented in the exchange.

Divine Concept and Modal Coherence

30:36–2:12:07

William Lane Craig — section score 82/100

A necessarily existing God may be timeless without creation yet acquire temporal relations upon creating, without contradiction or loss of metaphysical necessity.

Strongest feature: Craig cleanly distinguishes metaphysical necessity from temporal mode and offers a coherent model in which an uncreated being exists timelessly sans universe but enters temporal relations with creation. Principal limitation: The response establishes conceptual possibility mainly by noting that Tooley never demonstrated contradiction; it does not explain in depth how a person timeless without creation can stand in temporal relations after creation while remaining metaphysically necessary. Live burden: Pro need only defend coherence against Tooley’s impossibility claim, although a fuller positive account would strengthen the model, while con must derive an actual contradiction rather than rely on analogy with abstract numbers. Locked score: 79 rewards a concise and appropriately limited rebuttal that neutralizes the asserted impossibility, with modest reservation about its undeveloped metaphysics.

Michael Tooley — section score 80/100

Tooley defines the target as a perfectly good, omniscient, omnipotent personal creator and argues probabilistically from evil against it through observed apparently gratuitous suffering.

Strongest feature: Tooley states the target attributes and burden with exceptional discipline, explicitly distinguishing evidential improbability from logical incompatibility and thereby preventing stronger conclusions from being mistakenly demanded of his case. Principal limitation: Defining God classically does not by itself show that every ordinary use of the debate motion carries all those attributes, so the later definitional conflict requires an argument about the intended proposition rather than stipulation alone. Live burden: Con must show that observed suffering appreciably lowers the probability of precisely this perfect being, while pro may defend either compatibility with perfection or a justified broader conception of God. Locked score: 89 recognizes unusually clear burden allocation and conceptual framing, with only a residual motion-definition dispute limiting complete decisiveness.
