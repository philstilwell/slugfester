Overall assessment

David Wood — Theist: 73/100

Published strengths

  • Distinguished a positive theistic explanation from a simple appeal to temporary gaps in scientific knowledge.
  • Exposed symmetrical underdetermination by asking whether advanced-agent explanations could permanently displace divine identification.
  • Separated causal accounts of moral belief from the normative justification of moral obligation.
  • Qualified the afterlife response as a possible wider context rather than presenting a complete demonstrated theodicy.

Published weaknesses

  • The problem-of-evil reply concentrated on the skeptic's moral ontology without resolving the internal tension among divine goodness, power, and preventable innocent suffering.
  • Several constructive claims asserted theism's explanatory advantage without supplying comparative expectations that would discriminate it from naturalistic or other-agent alternatives.

Michael Shermer — Skeptic: 80/100

Published strengths

  • Presented large-scale preventable child suffering as a concrete and central evidential challenge to traditional theism.
  • Required discriminating evidence and distinguished observable artifact makers from a proposed cosmic maker.
  • Supplied Darwinian selection as a specific mechanism for apparent biological design.
  • Grounded cognitive reliability in successful measurement, prediction, intervention, and scientific correction.
  • Offered secular moral considerations involving suffering, reciprocity, cooperation, and flourishing.

Published weaknesses

  • The cross-cultural reproducibility reply did not distinguish present scientific validation from the historical causal question about theology's contribution to scientific institutions.
  • The empirical morality argument moved from objective descriptive discoveries to binding normative conclusions without stating the necessary justificatory bridge.

Scientific Intelligibility, Cognition, and Historical Origins

5:41–74:41

David Wood — section score 70/100

Christian beliefs in an orderly, knowable, and worthwhile creation helped motivate pioneers of modern science, making theology a contributing historical cause rather than an obstacle to inquiry.

Strongest feature: The argument identifies a historically relevant connection between theological expectations, prominent Christian investigators, and confidence that nature is orderly, intelligible, and worth sustained investigation. Principal limitation: Its categorical contrast with other civilizations substantially outruns the selected examples because institutional, economic, philosophical, and cross-cultural causes are neither isolated nor comparatively evaluated. Live burden: The historical thesis therefore needs representative evidence showing that Christian commitments contributed distinctively while acknowledging that contribution need imply neither necessity nor unique sufficiency. Locked score: The mixed band reflects a recoverable, pertinent causal proposal with recognizable historical support, yet also materially overstated counterfactual language, missing qualification, compressed rival explanations, and limited charity toward alternative accounts of scientific emergence.

Michael Shermer — section score 80/100

People across religious cultures reproduce the same physics because nature is publicly knowable, so Christianity is unnecessary for validating contemporary scientific practice.

Strongest feature: The reply uses cross-cultural reproducibility to show persuasively that scientific findings remain publicly accessible and testable regardless of an investigator's religious commitments or geographical setting. Principal limitation: It slides from Christianity's evident dispensability for contemporary validation to the different historical proposition that theological attitudes contributed nothing causally to scientific emergence. Live burden: The skeptical response must distinguish current justification from institutional origins and supply comparative historical evidence concerning whether Christian commitments were causally important, incidental, obstructive, or replaceable. Locked score: The mixed band recognizes a clear, well-supported universality argument that materially challenges religious necessity, while reflecting missing scope qualifications, incomplete contact with the narrower causal thesis, overstatement about pioneers' motivations, and limited attention to the possibility of contingent historical contribution without present epistemic dependence.

Design, Cosmic Origins, and Natural Mechanisms

47:38–127:09

David Wood — section score 74/100

Biological organisms exhibit functionally ordered constituent parts, whereas God is not proposed as a comparable composite organism, so one designer-regress question may not transfer unchanged.

Strongest feature: The reply offers a coherent category distinction between organisms whose constituent parts exhibit functional arrangement and a proposed divine designer not characterized as similarly biologically composite. Principal limitation: That distinction blocks only one formulation of regress and does not establish natural unavailability, intentional design, divine agency, or a principled explanatory stopping point superior to alternatives. Live burden: The argument must stabilize its meaning of design and show why a noncomposite agent is independently admissible, evidentially supported, and explanatorily preferable rather than merely exempted from organism-level analysis. Locked score: The mixed band reflects a recoverable and relevant distinction with some logical value, alongside materially overstated biological premises, limited evidence, incomplete contact with the earlier discrimination challenge, and insufficient charity toward the strongest explanatory-regress objection.

Michael Shermer — section score 77/100

Natural selection supplies a cumulative bottom-up mechanism through which simpler structures can become complex, apparently purposeful organs such as eyes and wings without intelligent direction.

Strongest feature: The reply supplies a specific, established, bottom-up mechanism for apparent biological purpose and carefully keeps its strongest claim within cumulative change among complex biological organs. Principal limitation: Although it decisively challenges the asserted absence of natural mechanisms, it does not answer the narrower claim that a designer need not possess organism-like arranged constituent parts. Live burden: The naturalistic case should connect variation, selection, inheritance, and relevant examples to any particular design inference while acknowledging that evolutionary success alone does not resolve every cosmic or metaphysical design question. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent coherence, matched calibration, substantial warrant, accurate representation, and central relevance, with only bounded responsiveness costs for leaving the distinct regress category and nonbiological forms of design outside its otherwise powerful answer.

Evidence, Falsifiability, and Explanatory Method

31:56–73:36

David Wood — section score 78/100

Theism need not occupy temporary gaps in knowledge because it can seek positive explanation in established facts and the intelligible organization of nature considered comprehensively.

Strongest feature: The reply clearly distinguishes inference from temporary ignorance from a proposed explanation grounded in established knowledge and the intelligible order of nature as an integrated whole. Principal limitation: Denying reliance on gaps does not itself provide positive likelihoods, causal specificity, or any observation that discriminates divine agency from natural processes or other possible agents. Live burden: The theistic case must identify the known features doing evidential work and explain why those features are appreciably more expected under theism than under serious alternatives. Locked score: The strong band reflects direct contact with the objection, stable terminology, and an important conceptual clarification, but also an unresolved discriminating-evidence requirement, only implicit explanatory support, slightly overstated self-vindication, and incomplete representation of the skeptic's methodological challenge.

Michael Shermer — section score 84/100

When favorable and unfavorable outcomes alike are accommodated as divine action, the God hypothesis lacks observations that could discriminate it from competing explanations.

Strongest feature: The move formulates a decisive methodological demand by asking what conceivable outcome would count against an explanation that absorbs favorable and unfavorable events as confirmation. Principal limitation: Its wording moves too readily from one Christian treatment of evil to the God hypothesis generally, overlooking probabilistic confirmation and indirect testing that need not supply deductive falsification. Live burden: The critique should specify which theistic predictions are under examination and demonstrate that their qualifications genuinely accommodate every relevant observation without comparative evidential cost. Locked score: The strong band recognizes coherent reasoning, direct contact with the falsifiability burden, and an important insulation diagnosis, while reflecting partially unstable scope, slightly overstated necessity, and limited engagement with sophisticated accounts of comparative rather than strictly falsifying evidence.

Objective Morality and Human Moral Status

90:54–131:47

David Wood — section score 71/100

Evolutionary hardwiring and social instruction may explain why people hold moral convictions, but neither causal genealogy alone establishes that the condemned conduct is objectively wrong.

Strongest feature: The move articulates the essential distinction between explaining the causal origin of moral convictions and supplying normative reasons that justify judging conduct objectively right or wrong. Principal limitation: It presents hardwiring and social teaching too nearly as exhaustive naturalistic options, overlooking secular accounts based on consistency, reciprocity, interests, agency, impartiality, or flourishing. Live burden: The theistic case must show why those candidate reasons cannot generate obligation and then defend divine grounding without merely relocating the normative question to God's nature, commands, or purposes. Locked score: The mixed band reflects strong internal coherence, vivid illustration, and indispensable relevance to moral grounding, yet also a complete miss of the immediately preceding cognition target, slightly overstated exhaustiveness, limited evidential support, and insufficiently charitable treatment of naturalistic justification.

Michael Shermer — section score 76/100

Sentience, suffering, reciprocity, promise-keeping, cooperation, and flourishing supply secular normative reasons that people can examine and improve through collective moral reasoning.

Strongest feature: The reply moves beyond genealogy by presenting suffering, reciprocity, promise-keeping, cooperation, and sentient flourishing as recognizable considerations for criticism, improvement, and practically reasoned moral conduct. Principal limitation: It treats flourishing as foundational without adequately explaining its objectively binding authority, adjudicating conflicts among interests and candidate goods, or distinguishing widespread endorsement from categorical requirement. Live burden: The secular account must construct an explicit normative bridge from shared vulnerability, agency, impartial reasoning, and reciprocal constraint to obligation, while demonstrating nonquestion-begging priority rules for difficult collisions. Locked score: The strong band reflects central relevance, substantial evidential contact, practical support, and generally charitable engagement, tempered by implicit qualification, somewhat overstated authority, and a consequential unresolved metaethical gap.

Evil, Innocent Suffering, and Theistic Coherence

30:08–124:57

David Wood — section score 72/100

Calling God's permission of suffering wrong invokes a moral standard applicable to God, so an atheist critic may owe an account of that standard's objectivity and accessibility.

Strongest feature: The reply identifies a genuine metaethical question about the status and accessibility of the moral standard invoked when divine permission of suffering is judged objectively wrong. Principal limitation: It treats the objection principally as an external atheist accusation, although an internal critique can provisionally use the theist's own claim that God is perfectly good. Live burden: The response must address the original evidential tension between preventable innocent suffering, goodness, and power while separately showing why secular moral realism cannot support any external formulation. Locked score: The mixed band reflects high relevance to moral grounding and a clear presuppositional challenge, but also materially overstated necessity, incomplete responsiveness, weak handling of internal critique, and reduced charity in suggesting that atheism must be independently established before the suffering objection can operate.

Michael Shermer — section score 85/100

The enormous scale of preventable suffering and death among innocent children creates a direct conflict with jointly affirming an all-powerful and wholly good God.

Strongest feature: The argument presents the problem of evil with exceptional clarity, joins the familiar divine attributes to concrete preventable child mortality, and directly occupies a central comparative burden. Principal limitation: Its categorical incompatibility leaves implicit the consequential premise that no morally sufficient reason could justify divine permission, rather than distinguishing strict contradiction from powerful evidential tension. Live burden: The skeptical case must compare serious theodicies and explain why unknown-reason possibilities do not adequately reduce the force of the observed scale, innocence, and preventability. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise structure, substantial empirical support, high relevance, and fair presentation, while reserving limited space for the unstated sufficient-reason premise and the absence of developed comparison with the strongest theistic alternatives.
