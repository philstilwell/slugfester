Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Resurrection case: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Built a coherent cumulative route from identifiable burial and empty-tomb traditions through appearances and the origin of resurrection belief.
  • Defended First Corinthians 15 with unusually specific external and internal textual considerations.
  • Correctly identified evidential likelihood under no resurrection as a necessary component of comparative explanation.
  • Pressed the important distinction between direct pagan borrowing and broader resemblance by demanding categorical and causal specificity.

Published weaknesses

  • The culminating best-explanation claim compared mainly theft and apparent death, leaving the strongest literary, visionary, sociological, and composite alternatives insufficiently tested.

Robert Price — Historical-analogy critique: 80/100

Published strengths

  • Distinguished openness to miracles from acceptance of particular reports with excellent conceptual precision.
  • Kept source sufficiency separate from metaphysical possibility and repeatedly acknowledged uncertainty about proposed reconstructions.
  • Applied literary, form-critical, analogical, and sociological challenges across every major factual component.
  • Offered cognitive-dissonance reduction and retrojection as intelligible alternatives to a simple event-to-belief pathway.

Published weaknesses

  • Several central objections relied on broad genre or source-quality assertions without the detailed comparisons needed to establish their application to the particular traditions.
  • The UFO and fairy-tale analogies weakened engagement with the strongest probabilistic and evidential formulation of the resurrection case.

Source Proximity, Textual Integrity, and Historical Method

28:01–120:07

William Lane Craig — section score 83/100

Universal manuscript inclusion, early citation, contextual continuity, vocabulary, and pronoun usage strongly support Pauline authorship of First Corinthians 15:3–11 against interpolation.

Strongest feature: The response offers convergent external and internal evidence, including universal manuscript presence, early citation, vocabulary, pronoun continuity, and argumentative disruption resulting from excision. Principal limitation: Its impressive authenticity case does not by itself establish every appearance's historicity and only partially contacts the earlier methodological objections concerning analogy and case-specific miraculous inference. Live burden: Craig must connect authentic Pauline use to the tradition's dating, testimonial reliability, experiential content, and comparative explanatory significance without treating textual authenticity as historical verification. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional precision, evidence, calibration, and engagement with interpolation, while acknowledging reduced responsiveness to the legally exposed methodological targets and the remaining distance between authentic text and established event.

Robert Price — section score 82/100

Historical assessment can remain open to miraculous possibility while requiring case-specific evidence, because divine capability does not authenticate every reported miracle.

Strongest feature: The response cleanly distinguishes metaphysical possibility, divine capability, and historical actuality, preventing either automatic supernatural acceptance or antecedent dismissal from replacing case-specific evidential assessment. Principal limitation: This methodological distinction is exceptionally sound but does not itself determine how Craig's particular cumulative evidence performs, because his stated inference purports to be comparative rather than indiscriminately credulous. Live burden: Price must apply the standard to burial, tomb, appearances, and belief formation, explaining which evidential links fail and why resurrection underperforms identifiable alternatives. Locked score: The very strong band reflects outstanding conceptual precision, calibration, relevance, and charitable reframing, while withholding complete fulfillment because the decisive case-specific evidential comparison remains largely prospective rather than executed.

Burial Tradition and the Empty Tomb

2:08–137:23

William Lane Craig — section score 84/100

Early Pauline and Markan traditions independently support Joseph of Arimathea's honorable burial of Jesus in an identifiable tomb, with no competing burial tradition presented.

Strongest feature: The argument identifies Joseph, an ascertainable tomb, Pauline attestation, and comparatively early Markan material, thereby constructing a concrete historical proposition rather than an indefinite burial possibility. Principal limitation: Early provenance and apparent independence strengthen transmission credentials, yet neither characteristic independently establishes factual accuracy, and the asserted absence of competing tradition receives insufficiently discriminating consideration. Live burden: The case still must demonstrate that Pauline and Markan materials genuinely provide independent, historically reliable access and that alternative burial scenarios inadequately explain the evidentiary record. Locked score: The strong band appropriately reflects excellent central relevance, coherent cumulative organization, and specific source grounding, while retaining meaningful reservations about evidential calibration, implicit uncertainty, and incompletely defended historical confidence.

Robert Price — section score 80/100

Ancient vanished-body, heavenly-ascent, appearance, and skeptical-disciple motifs place the empty-tomb narrative within a recognizable theological hero-story pattern rather than straightforward reportage.

Strongest feature: The move connects tomb disappearance and postmortem appearances to recognizable ancient narrative patterns, directly challenging an uncomplicated transition from theological storytelling to historical reporting. Principal limitation: Structural resemblance alone neither demonstrates literary dependence nor excludes historically transmitted content, and the asserted genre classification receives insufficient element-by-element comparison, chronology, and causal specification. Live burden: Price must identify diagnostically significant parallels, distinguish broad motifs from close correspondence, and explain why genre formation better predicts the Christian material than historically grounded theological narration. Locked score: The strong band reflects direct contact with two foundational claims, coherent analogical pressure, and genuine source-critical relevance, while retaining reservations about evidential detail, implicit qualification, and an overdrawn opposition between theology and history.

Nature and Historical Status of the Postmortem Appearances

12:16–137:46

William Lane Craig — section score 84/100

Paul's early witness list, acquaintance with participants, and multiple Gospel strands support postmortem appearance experiences reported by different individuals and groups.

Strongest feature: The move places an early Pauline witness list, personal acquaintance, recipient diversity, and multiple narrative strands behind a clearly recoverable claim that individuals and groups experienced appearances. Principal limitation: It insufficiently distinguishes reported experience from bodily encounter, and scholarly acknowledgment cannot substitute for demonstrating source independence, experiential character, or the reliability of every listed occurrence. Live burden: The case must clarify precisely what the earliest testimony establishes and show why visionary, communal, literary, or dependent-transmission accounts inadequately explain its distribution. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent relevance, structured evidential presentation, and substantial historical proximity, while recognizing understated uncertainty, partly unexamined alternatives, and a conclusion somewhat stronger than the supplied warrant.

Robert Price — section score 78/100

Form-critical tensions and institutional features permit treating First Corinthians 15:3–11 as a later composite creed, weakening its status as early appearance evidence.

Strongest feature: The reply directly challenges both Pauline authenticity and historical use by identifying creed form, compositional fragments, textual tensions, and potentially later institutional characteristics within the passage. Principal limitation: Traditional or composite formulation can be early and authentically quoted by Paul, while universal manuscript attestation and contextual integration receive less direct rebuttal than the interpolation hypothesis requires. Live burden: Price must date the alleged insertion, explain its universal transmission and contextual fit, and demonstrate why compositeness indicates lateness rather than early communal preservation. Locked score: The strong band recognizes direct responsiveness, explicit qualification, and relevant form-critical observations, while reflecting unstable dating implications, comparatively limited evidential warrant, and insufficient engagement with the strongest compatibility between early tradition and authentic Pauline incorporation.

Origins of the Disciples’ Resurrection Belief

14:29–140:10

William Lane Craig — section score 82/100

The disciples sincerely adopted resurrection belief despite messianic disappointment, crucifixion's stigma, and Jewish expectations locating resurrection generally at history's culmination.

Strongest feature: The argument sharply identifies the explanatory discontinuity between a crucified leader, messianic disappointment, perceived divine curse, general resurrection expectations, and the disciples' specific proclamation. Principal limitation: Its characterization of Jewish expectations and unanimous disciple predispositions is excessively uniform, leaving insufficient room for diversity, reinterpretation, adaptation, and uneven development within disappointed religious communities. Live burden: The pro case must preserve the genuine historical surprise while demonstrating that available Jewish categories and ordinary belief-revision mechanisms could not plausibly generate this particular early conviction. Locked score: The strong band rewards central relevance, explanatory clarity, and a meaningful contextual challenge, while registering compressed intellectual history, implicit qualifications, and incomplete engagement with adaptable or developmental alternatives.

Robert Price — section score 83/100

Expectations of Jesus' future return could have been projected backward into prior appearances and resurrection, presenting a developmental alternative without claiming certainty about its occurrence.

Strongest feature: The argument explicitly offers a developmental mechanism connecting expected future return with backward projection of resurrection and appearances, while carefully presenting that reconstruction as possibility rather than established history. Principal limitation: A conceivable pathway does not demonstrate actual occurrence, and the move supplies insufficient chronological, textual, or sociological evidence for the proposed stages, timing, and distribution. Live burden: Price must show that retrojection arose early enough, explain the specific appearance traditions and bodily-resurrection content, and outperform interpretations grounded in experiences contemporaries understood as encounters. Locked score: The strong band reflects admirable calibration, substantial responsiveness, coherent possibility generation, and appropriate uncertainty, while preserving a major evidential reservation about whether the hypothetical development explains the available particulars.

Resurrection as the Best Explanation

18:04–145:01

William Lane Craig — section score 81/100

Because theft and apparent-death theories fail to explain the asserted burial, empty tomb, appearances, and transformed belief, divine resurrection is offered as their best cumulative explanation.

Strongest feature: The move directly reaches the motion by unifying burial, empty tomb, appearances, and resurrection belief under one proposed explanation and contrasting that account with recognizable older alternatives. Principal limitation: Rejecting theft and apparent death does not exhaust serious literary, visionary, sociological, or composite explanations, while scholarly rejection and asserted absence cannot perform the missing comparative analysis. Live burden: The inference must condition explicitly on the underlying facts, specify divine resurrection's predictive advantages, and compare the strongest ordinary alternatives individually and jointly under consistent explanatory criteria. Locked score: The strong band reflects maximal motion relevance and intelligible cumulative structure, but also substantial evidential overstatement, limited representative charity, missing uncertainty, and an inadequately completed best-explanation comparison.

Robert Price — section score 76/100

The resurrection may be conceivable, yet literary and theologically developed narratives do not supply adequate historical warrant for confidence that the asserted events occurred.

Strongest feature: The response carefully separates the conceivable truth of resurrection from whether surviving texts justify historical confidence, directly addressing the motion without relying on metaphysical impossibility. Principal limitation: Calling narratives literary creations does not automatically eliminate historically usable testimony, and the contrast between literary and historical evidence remains insufficiently defined and source-specifically demonstrated. Live burden: Price must identify concrete redactional or legendary features affecting each foundational claim and explain why critical extraction cannot recover credible traditions beneath theological composition. Locked score: The strong band reflects maximal motion relevance, explicit uncertainty, charitable concession, and a valid evidential distinction, while recognizing underdeveloped criteria, comparatively modest supporting evidence, and an overbroad inference from literary construction to historical insufficiency.
