Historical facts and source reliability

7:30–60:41

Mike Winger — section score 84/100

Jesus' death by Roman crucifixion under Pontius Pilate is presented as a securely supported historical premise for evaluating the resurrection claim.

Strongest feature: The move identifies a necessary historical premise with a clear agent, official setting, named scholarly support, and asserted source multiplicity, making its relevance to any bodily-resurrection inference immediate and exceptionally easy to recover. Principal limitation: The appeal to scholarly agreement and eleven independent sources remains compressed because neither the independence criteria nor the evidential contribution of those sources is demonstrated within the selected material. Live burden: The argument must distinguish consensus as useful orientation from the underlying evidence and explain why dependence, dating, genre, or transmission concerns do not materially weaken the death premise. Locked score: The strong band recognizes coherent presentation, substantial historical plausibility, and indispensable burden contact while retaining a modest deduction for unsupported numerical and independence claims that carry evidential weight.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 79/100

Late, noncontemporary, source-dependent accounts with unresolved inconsistencies do not securely transform the proposed minimal-facts list into established historical facts.

Strongest feature: The reply targets the cumulative case at its foundation by identifying dating, dependence, and inconsistency as evidential variables that can weaken confidence before any best-explanation comparison begins. Principal limitation: The selected presentation ranges broadly across historical premises without documenting the severity and relevance of each alleged problem for each individual fact, whose support and source history may differ substantially. Live burden: The con side must provide itemized source criticism showing which claims depend on which texts, what inconsistencies matter, and how much confidence should be reduced rather than treating all premises uniformly. Locked score: The strong band reflects central relevance, appropriately nonabsolute force, and a credible source-sufficiency challenge, while acknowledging that the reply remains only partial without detailed evidential accounting.

Testimony, sincerity, and corroboration

14:10–72:07

Mike Winger — section score 75/100

Transformation, persecution, and costly commitment support the conclusion that Peter, James, Paul, and other disciples sincerely believed they encountered the risen Jesus.

Strongest feature: The move draws a restrained and logically relevant inference from costly commitment to sincerity, identifies particularly supported individuals, and avoids claiming that willingness to suffer automatically proves the resurrection belief true. Principal limitation: Its language sometimes expands from Peter, James, and Paul to the disciples generally, although the selected evidence provides materially stronger historical support for the named individuals than for the broader group. Live burden: The pro side must document who knowingly faced which costs for an appearance-based belief and preserve the distinction between sincere conviction, accurate memory, and veridical encounter. Locked score: The strong band reflects a coherent sincerity inference and useful corroborative role, while accounting for scope expansion and the limited capacity of transformation evidence to establish the underlying event independently.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 91/100

Sincere testimony may establish that extraordinary experiences were reported without sufficiently establishing that the reports' supernatural interpretation is true.

Strongest feature: The reply sharply distinguishes testimony, sincere experience, reported content, and supernatural truth, directly diagnosing the inferential gap between believing witnesses and an independently warranted resurrection conclusion. Principal limitation: The point establishes a necessary caution about testimonial sufficiency but does not quantify how much cumulative evidential value early, multiple, or costly testimony might nevertheless possess when combined with other historical considerations. Live burden: The skeptical case must assess the strongest testimonial configuration rather than testimony in abstraction and explain why dependence, transmission, and alternative experience mechanisms keep its total support below the required threshold. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise concepts, proportionate force, and decisive contact with a recurring pro inference, while leaving room for a fuller comparative account of testimony's positive but limited contribution.

Resurrection and rival explanations

12:30–50:42

Mike Winger — section score 81/100

Individual and group experiences interpreted as appearances of Jesus are historical data that a simple hallucination account does not adequately explain.

Strongest feature: The move usefully distinguishes post-death experiences from the further resurrection conclusion, names both individual and group reports, and supplies scholarly acknowledgment that appearance experiences belong within the historical data requiring explanation. Principal limitation: The argument moves too quickly from reported group experiences to the inadequacy of hallucination without establishing independent testimony, simultaneity, phenomenological similarity, or the precise psychological alternative being rejected. Live burden: It must demonstrate that the sources reliably preserve genuinely shared perceptual events and compare resurrection with socially reinforced, visionary, memorial, or transmission-based explanations at equivalent levels of specificity. Locked score: The strong band recognizes focused evidence, explanatory relevance, and qualified historical framing, while preserving a material deduction because occurrence of experiences does not by itself determine their external object or cause.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 87/100

Known generators of false reports, including deception, legend, error, and exaggeration, remain better grounded than an unsupported resurrection mechanism.

Strongest feature: The reply introduces relevant background knowledge by contrasting a poorly specified resurrection mechanism with familiar processes that generate inaccurate or exaggerated reports, thereby challenging the preferred explanation's initial comparative footing. Principal limitation: It does not examine how any particular natural process fits every proposed datum, and its use of dismissive mechanism language risks collapsing divine agency into caricature rather than evaluating the strongest formulation. Live burden: The skeptical comparison must connect identified natural mechanisms to source dependence, appearance traditions, empty-tomb reporting, and movement development with enough detail to demonstrate cumulative adequacy rather than mere availability. Locked score: The strong band recognizes legitimate prior-weight reasoning and direct contact with explanatory viability, while retaining deductions for rhetorical compression and the absence of a datum-by-datum natural account.

Event evidence and supernatural causation

17:28–82:52

Mike Winger — section score 73/100

If Jesus rose, his religious, miraculous, and prophetic context is offered as evidence that the event should be understood as divine action.

Strongest feature: The move recognizes that establishing an unusual return to life and identifying God as its cause are distinct burdens, then attempts the indispensable causal bridge through the event's surrounding religious and prophetic context. Principal limitation: Religious meaning, miracle traditions, and prophecy are grouped together without demonstrating their independent reliability or showing why contextual fit strongly discriminates divine action from human interpretation, coincidence, or later narrative formation. Live burden: The argument must specify which contextual facts are independently established, how strongly they predict divine intervention, and why the proposed agent is better supported than an unspecified cause. Locked score: The strong band rewards direct contact with a necessary causal question and an explicit conditional qualification, while reflecting substantial underdevelopment in the inference from meaningful context to divine agency.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 88/100

Resurrection cannot become the best explanation merely by contextual labeling; supernatural interaction and causal eligibility require independent evidential support.

Strongest feature: The reply directly challenges the eligibility of resurrection as an explanatory candidate and correctly refuses to let religious context substitute automatically for evidence concerning possibility, interaction, and causal attribution. Principal limitation: Requiring prior evidence that the supernatural is real can sound categorically stronger than necessary, because a hypothesis might receive some support through the very observations it is introduced to explain. Live burden: The con side must formulate a noncircular threshold that permits hypothesis evaluation while demanding enough independent or discriminating evidence to distinguish supernatural causation from an unsupported label. Locked score: The strong band rewards direct responsiveness and an important causal safeguard, while reflecting compression between modal possibility and actual interaction and some overstatement in treating preliminary proof as a prerequisite.

Extraordinary claims and evidential burden

58:01–89:56

Mike Winger — section score 82/100

Reasonably established nonmiraculous facts may be assessed before resurrection is compared as their best explanation, without presupposing that conclusion.

Strongest feature: The reply clearly separates historical-premise assessment from explanatory comparison and correctly observes that supporting evidence need not prove a conclusion before it can be considered, thereby resisting a potentially circular threshold. Principal limitation: Defending the admissibility and sequence of evidence does not establish that the selected premises are sufficiently reliable or that resurrection ultimately satisfies the opponent's proportionate evidential standard. Live burden: The pro side must carry the valid two-part framework through a symmetrical comparison that incorporates background plausibility, source uncertainty, combined alternatives, and the distinct inference to divine agency. Locked score: The very strong band reflects an exceptionally clear and justified reframing that preserves the opponent's legitimate demand for evidence, while leaving the framework's substantive historical and causal execution unresolved.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 88/100

Bodily resurrection is extraordinary, and the absence of physical, contemporary, and possibility-establishing evidence leaves its available support insufficient.

Strongest feature: The reply addresses evidential sufficiency directly and identifies physical, contemporary, and possibility-related deficits rather than simply declaring extraordinary claims inadmissible or supernatural events impossible. Principal limitation: It does not fully defend why evidence must take those particular forms, and repeated absence language can understate the testimonial, traditional, and contextual evidence actually offered by the pro side. Live burden: The skeptical case must explain how evidential quantity, quality, independence, and background conflict jointly determine insufficiency without converting useful evidence categories into categorical prerequisites. Locked score: The strong band recognizes clear burden contact and concrete diagnostic content, while reflecting incomplete threshold justification and an occasionally overstated description of the positive evidence available.

Reasonable belief and suspension of judgment

5:25–117:01

Mike Winger — section score 85/100

Reasonable resurrection belief can arise from first establishing likely historical facts and then cumulatively comparing the explanations offered for those facts.

Strongest feature: The two-stage framework is lucid, carefully qualified, and directly connected to the motion, giving the subsequent historical claims and explanatory comparisons an intelligible argumentative destination rather than presenting them as disconnected observations. Principal limitation: Announcing a sound comparative method does not itself establish that the selected facts are reliable, mutually independent, or collectively more probable under resurrection than under natural or unresolved alternatives. Live burden: The case must therefore defend each material datum, specify comparative explanatory criteria, and show that their cumulative interaction supplies substantially more warrant than the individual claims considered separately. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent relevance, recoverable structure, and proportionate possibility language, while preserving a meaningful evidential gap between an appropriate method and its successful execution.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 92/100

Finding resurrection insufficiently supported permits nonacceptance without implying impossibility, nonoccurrence, or a presupposition against every supernatural event.

Strongest feature: The reply precisely distinguishes possibility, occurrence, burden satisfaction, and belief, showing that withholding acceptance after an unmet burden neither assumes naturalism nor establishes that resurrection did not happen. Principal limitation: The conceptual distinction is exceptionally strong, but applying it still depends on the preceding empirical assessment that the offered evidence actually remains insufficient under an appropriate standard. Live burden: The con side must continue grounding suspension in specific source, explanatory, and causal deficits so that nonacceptance follows from the record rather than from a merely formal allocation of burden. Locked score: The exceptional band reflects outstanding clarity, calibration, charity, and direct resolution of the circularity accusation, while recognizing that even a correct burden principle requires case-specific evidential support.
