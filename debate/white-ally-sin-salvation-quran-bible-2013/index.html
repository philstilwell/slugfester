Overall assessment

James White — Salvation through Christ's atonement: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Grounded Christ’s voluntary redemption and purification in a specific Titus passage while preserving a substantive role for transformed conduct.
  • Distinguished salvation’s ground from its consequences with unusual clarity in answering the command to work out salvation.
  • Pressed a central philosophical burden by asking how direct pardon preserves the connection between divine holiness and moral law.
  • Presented Day of Atonement typology and messianic diversity as concrete resources for biblical continuity rather than relying solely on abstract doctrine.

Published weaknesses

  • The Adamic-representation reply asserted inherited representative consequences without supplying the specific textual bridge demanded by the inherited-guilt challenge.
  • The scriptural-authority response relied on asserted inspiration and providential recognition without giving a sufficiently independent criterion of canonical authority.

Shabir Ally — Direct forgiveness and accountable deeds: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Presented a coherent Quranic framework joining repentance, repaired harm, mercy, good conduct, and final moral accounting.
  • Carefully limited the inherited-guilt challenge to what the cited Psalms and Genesis materials establish rather than claiming universal disproof.
  • Raised a central moral challenge concerning innocent suffering and the just transfer of punishment or righteousness in substitutionary atonement.
  • Used Gospel chronology, Pauline interpretation, and New Testament deeds language to create a cumulative challenge to uniform atonement continuity.

Published weaknesses

  • The Pauline-development case called Paul’s interpretation a misunderstanding and inferred doctrinal construction more confidently than its limited textual comparison supported.
  • The Gospel-development and Isaiah-shaping arguments moved too quickly from literary sequence or correspondence to modification and deliberate historical construction.
  • The deeds argument weakened its textual case by saying Paul forgot his doctrine and by asserting a direct James-Paul literary reply without sufficient historical support.

Human Sin, Moral Capacity, and Inherited Condition

18:14–57:37

James White — section score 74/100

Adam knowingly rebelled as humanity’s representative, bringing spiritual rebellion, death, and separation from God upon himself and his descendants.

Strongest feature: The move clearly describes Adam’s informed rebellion and connects it to consequential death, alienation, and the condition of his progeny, supplying an intelligible representative framework for the pro account. Principal limitation: It asserts rather than demonstrates Adam’s representative status and does not answer the narrower textual challenge concerning whether Genesis or Psalms establishes inherited personal guilt as distinct from inherited consequences. Live burden: The account must define representation, distinguish transmitted culpability from mortality and corruption, and supply the missing scriptural bridge showing why descendants bear more than consequences. Locked score: The mixed band reflects genuine relevance and a recoverable theological proposition, limited by its classification as a relevant nonanswer, categorical universal scope, and insufficient engagement with the opponent’s carefully restricted evidentiary claim.

Shabir Ally — section score 85/100

Poetic statements in Psalms and Adam’s Genesis narrative do not, without additional argument, establish that later generations inherit Adam’s personal guilt.

Strongest feature: The argument carefully limits its conclusion to what particular Psalms and Genesis materials establish, distinguishing poetic expression from legislative teaching and avoiding the stronger claim that every original-sin doctrine is disproved. Principal limitation: Its compressed treatment announces a turn toward Genesis more readily than it supplies the complete textual examination needed to exclude representative guilt, inherited corruption, or cumulative canonical reasoning. Live burden: The case must separate inherited personal culpability from inherited mortality, alienation, and inclination, then demonstrate which categories the relevant passages affirm, deny, or leave unresolved. Locked score: The very strong band recognizes disciplined scope, direct contact with a central human-condition premise, and proportionate calibration, while retaining room for fuller exegesis of Genesis and the cumulative alternative.

Divine Holiness, Justice, and Direct Forgiveness

11:32–65:44

James White — section score 79/100

Because divine law expresses God’s holy nature, forgiveness requires a principled ground preserving both holiness and law rather than simply suspending judgment.

Strongest feature: The reply identifies the deepest conceptual burden for direct forgiveness by connecting divine law with divine holiness and insisting that pardon must remain principled rather than merely leave violated law without explanation. Principal limitation: The asserted necessity depends on an implicit entailment from God’s nature to a particular satisfaction requirement, while repentance, restitution, condemnation of wrongdoing, and moral transformation receive little separate analysis. Live burden: The argument must show why direct pardon cannot uphold law through stable merciful governance and why its proposed ground uniquely preserves holiness without introducing comparable transfer difficulties. Locked score: The strong band reflects central relevance, clear recoverability, and substantial pressure on the opposing mechanism, moderated by incomplete responsiveness to its stated conditions and an underdeveloped argument from divine-law continuity to required satisfaction.

Shabir Ally — section score 83/100

The Quran joins sincere repentance, repaired harm, predominantly good deeds, and God’s direct forgiveness within a final judgment that accounts for human conduct.

Strongest feature: This constructive account coherently integrates repentance, restitution, predominantly good conduct, divine mercy, and final accountability, while its explicit conditions make the proposition unusually recoverable and directly responsive to the motion. Principal limitation: The cited passage establishes each component’s presence but incompletely explains their precise relationship, leaving uncertain whether deeds constitute evidence, conditions, comparative weight, or independent grounds for favorable judgment. Live burden: The account must articulate a principled decision rule connecting repentance, repaired injury, moral balance, and forgiveness without reducing mercy to discretionary lenience or works to mechanical entitlement. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional relevance, stable qualification, and substantial textual support, moderated only by the underdeveloped connective warrant governing how the identified conditions jointly determine salvation.

Christ's Identity, Sacrifice, and the Atonement Mechanism

24:31–75:36

James White — section score 84/100

Titus identifies Jesus as God and Savior who voluntarily gives himself to redeem, purify, and form a people zealous for good deeds.

Strongest feature: The constructive move grounds Christ’s identity, voluntary self-giving, redemption, purification, and transformed conduct in a specific New Testament passage, integrating mechanism and moral consequence with exceptional economy and directness. Principal limitation: The additional claim that believers receive Christ’s righteousness by faith as their adequate standing extends beyond the quoted material, which more explicitly establishes redemption from lawlessness and purification for good deeds. Live burden: The case must connect voluntary redemption to the justice of representation and independently substantiate imputed righteousness rather than allowing adjacent doctrinal commitments to carry the mechanism. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent scriptural specificity, stable central claims, coherent integration, and motion-level importance, with limited restraint for the compressed righteousness component and unresolved transfer explanation.

Shabir Ally — section score 77/100

Substitutionary atonement appears morally and conceptually problematic when an innocent son bears punishment or when payment and defeat mechanisms remain unclear.

Strongest feature: The objection isolates a central moral difficulty for substitutionary atonement by asking how punishment, liability, or righteousness can be transferred without treating an innocent party unjustly, and it directly pressures the proposed mechanism. Principal limitation: The presentation groups several distinguishable atonement models together and relies heavily on inflammatory third-party characterization, insufficiently separating imposed punishment, voluntary self-giving, representative solidarity, ransom, and divine action. Live burden: The objection must address the strongest divine-self-giving account while still explaining why voluntariness and shared identity fail to justify representation or transfer. Locked score: The strong band recognizes high relevance and a genuine conceptual challenge, but reflects unstable mechanism boundaries, somewhat excessive characterization, and incomplete engagement with the opponent’s most charitable formulation of Christ’s agency.

Grace, Good Deeds, and the Ground of Salvation

29:39–61:55

James White — section score 89/100

Good deeds neither earn nor complete salvation but arise as the Spirit purifies redeemed people and conforms them to Christ after sufficient saving work.

Strongest feature: The argument repeatedly and precisely distinguishes salvation’s sufficient ground from Spirit-produced conduct, preserving a substantial role for obedience while denying that deeds earn, complete, or supplement Christ’s saving work. Principal limitation: The causal sequence is internally coherent but does not by itself eliminate the rival interpretation that continued obedience may function as a condition of final salvation rather than exclusively as evidence. Live burden: The account must explain warning passages, imperatives, and final judgment language while maintaining that transformed conduct is consequential without becoming dispensable, meritorious, or independently salvific. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional clarity, stable terminology, direct relevance, and a well-supported ground-versus-result distinction, with only the continuing burden of integrating apparently conditional biblical language into that framework.

Shabir Ally — section score 70/100

Paul sometimes links judgment with deeds, while James connects justification to works, suggesting substantive moral action remains integral within New Testament salvation teaching.

Strongest feature: The move correctly foregrounds New Testament passages connecting faith, works, justification, and judgment, creating a relevant internal challenge to any presentation that makes transformed conduct practically dispensable after Christ’s work. Principal limitation: Describing Paul as forgetting his doctrine and James as an obvious direct reply introduces historical and rhetorical claims not established merely by thematic overlap, chronology, or differing formulations. Live burden: The argument must demonstrate literary dependence or genuine contradiction while addressing the alternative that Paul and James confront different errors, employ justification differently, and still share a faith-producing-obedience framework. Locked score: The mixed band reflects important textual tension and useful burden contact, constrained by overstated opposition, unstable characterization, limited historical warrant, and failure to exclude a coherent distinction between salvation’s ground and its evidential consequences.

Scriptural Continuity, Development, and Authority

21:11–106:42

James White — section score 73/100

Jewish diversity and messianic readings of Isaiah 53 and Psalm 22 challenge the claim that sacrificial-messianic atonement was merely Paul’s contextual distortion.

Strongest feature: The reply correctly challenges any assumption of a single uniform Judaism and invokes Isaiah 53, Psalm 22, and messianic diversity as potential antecedents to Christian sacrificial interpretation. Principal limitation: Broad references to speculation are not delimited by date, community, or interpretive content, and the move does not analyze the opponent’s specific claim that later Gospel accounts increasingly modified earlier portrayals. Live burden: The case must document sufficiently early suffering or atoning messianic readings and connect them to Christian claims without treating interpretive diversity itself as positive proof of continuity. Locked score: The mixed band reflects meaningful contact with Pauline-invention and retrospective-shaping allegations, constrained by imprecise historical support, partial responsiveness, and failure to answer the detailed developmental evidence directed at the Gospels.

Shabir Ally — section score 75/100

Paul applies Deuteronomy’s curse language to Jesus and develops justification through Christ’s death apart from observance of the law.

Strongest feature: The argument identifies a specific interpretive bridge between Deuteronomy’s curse language and Galatians, making its scriptural-development challenge concrete, relevant, and substantially more testable than a generalized allegation of doctrinal invention. Principal limitation: Calling Paul’s reading a misunderstanding and attributing an entire justification doctrine causally to that move exceeds the limited comparison presented, especially without examining typology, antecedent traditions, or Paul’s broader reasoning. Live burden: The challenge must show why contextual legal meaning excludes a warranted typological application and why the disputed interpretation generated, rather than expressed or systematized, earlier Christian convictions. Locked score: The strong band reflects clear textual contact and central relevance, tempered by categorical causal language, incomplete historical support, and insufficient engagement with the strongest continuity-based alternative explanation.
