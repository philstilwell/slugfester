Overall assessment

Ray Comfort — Evangelical atonement: 68/100

Published strengths

  • Presented a clear gospel sequence from personal guilt through judgment and substitution to repentance, faith, and eternal life.
  • Made the works-grace distinction explicit and directly answered the rich ruler objection with a coherent diagnostic reading of the commandments.
  • Used memorable courtroom and debt imagery to make the proposed legal function of atonement readily intelligible.
  • Repeatedly contacted central burdens concerning judgment, mercy, salvation conditions, suffering, and experiential warrant.

Published weaknesses

  • The case repeatedly assumed divine authority or scriptural reliability when those premises were themselves under direct epistemic challenge.
  • The courtroom payment analogy never adequately explained why personal punishment transfers justly or why direct divine forgiveness is unavailable.
  • Categorical motive attributions portrayed unbelievers as accountability-avoiding criminals without a method for excluding sincere disagreement.
  • The experiential test lacked independent success and failure conditions, leaving rival religious experiences and unsuccessful sincere seekers unresolved.

Matt Dillahunty — Skeptical moral critique: 86/100

Published strengths

  • Pressed a sustained responsibility challenge based on divine foreknowledge, feasible alternatives, inherited guilt, and deliberate world selection.
  • Separated debt cancellation from character reform and directly challenged why a sovereign God would require sacrificial suffering before forgiveness.
  • Demanded truth-sensitive grounds for God, scripture, resurrection, and salvation instead of treating confidence or faith as self-validating.
  • Identified how post hoc blame of unsuccessful seekers could insulate the promised salvation procedure from meaningful failure.
  • Used brain dependence and personality alteration as concrete empirical pressure against an unsupported postmortem self.

Published weaknesses

  • The world-selection arguments sometimes moved too quickly from creating avoidable conditions to exclusive or comprehensive responsibility for creaturely wrongdoing.
  • The inherited-guilt criticism did not fully establish that the disputed doctrine meant liability solely for ancestors' acts rather than inherited condition plus personal wrongdoing.
  • The rich ruler argument left unresolved whether Jesus's commands were meritorious salvation conditions, evidence of repentance, or a diagnostic exposure of misplaced confidence.

Moral law, sin, and personal accountability

13:52–30:48

Ray Comfort — section score 61/100

Atheists reject God to avoid moral exposure and accountability, resembling criminals who avoid and resent law enforcement.

Strongest feature: The analogy gives the affirmative accountability thesis a vivid motivational explanation and directly connects rejection of God with avoidance of perceived moral exposure. Principal limitation: It generalizes across atheists from scriptural assertion and criminal comparison, offering no evidential procedure for separating culpable evasion from sincere epistemic or moral disagreement. Live burden: The reply must substantiate the attributed motive independently, confront nonbelievers' stated reasons, and answer the distinct objections concerning inherited guilt and a creator's responsibility for selected conditions. Locked score: The weak band reflects central thematic relevance and readily recoverable meaning, outweighed by nonresponse to decisive objections, radically overstated psychological certainty, weak warrant, unstable scope, and materially uncharitable representation.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 86/100

If God knowingly could create otherwise, deliberately selecting inevitable sin makes divine world selection relevant to responsibility for original sin.

Strongest feature: The conditional challenge precisely connects omniscience, feasible alternatives, deliberate world selection, and responsibility, thereby attacking the culpability premise underlying both deserved judgment and substitutionary payment. Principal limitation: The inference moves too quickly from creator-level selection to shifting responsibility away from Adam and Eve, without resolving whether divine foreknowledge and creaturely agency can sustain concurrent responsibility. Live burden: The critique must defend the feasibility of a relevant sinless alternative and explain why selection diminishes, reallocates, or defeats human responsibility rather than merely adding divine responsibility. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects forceful burden contact, explicit conditional structure, and substantial conceptual warrant, moderated by the unproved feasible-world premise and compressed treatment of compatibilist agency.

Judgment, suffering, and divine goodness

7:41–97:12

Ray Comfort — section score 67/100

Death is sin's divinely imposed wage, demonstrating wrongdoing's seriousness and preceding the judgment faced by every person.

Strongest feature: The move clearly supplies the judgment component required by the affirmative route, linking wrongdoing, deserved mortality, divine seriousness, and postmortem adjudication without obscuring its intended theological conclusion. Principal limitation: It asserts universal desert and final judgment categorically while providing neither an independently established divine authority premise nor an explanation showing proportionality between finite wrongdoing and the threatened consequences. Live burden: The argument must demonstrate why death constitutes deserved punishment rather than ordinary biological mortality and why the asserted judgment remains compatible with proportionate goodness. Locked score: The mixed band recognizes direct engagement with a central bridge and unusually clear proposition recovery, while preserving substantial deductions for unsupported proportionality, asserted scriptural authority, and unexamined alternative accounts.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 79/100

A creator who knowingly selected this world despite available alternatives bears responsibility for foreseeable atrocities occurring within it.

Strongest feature: The reply leverages the conceded availability of alternative creation to keep creator-level responsibility central, directly challenging the claim that human wrongdoing or a fallen world exhausts causal accountability. Principal limitation: It moves from knowingly creating permissive conditions to responsibility for a particular atrocity without distinguishing causal contribution, permission, moral blame, and the agency of the immediate wrongdoer. Live burden: The skeptic must defend a feasible alternative preserving relevant goods while preventing the atrocity and explain the degree of responsibility world selection establishes. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects substantial central relevance, a meaningful avoidability inference, and effective response contact, moderated by compressed causal attribution, undeveloped feasibility comparison, and limited engagement with freedom or greater-good defenses.

Necessity and moral logic of substitutionary atonement

6:03–89:27

Ray Comfort — section score 72/100

Human beings violated God's law, but Jesus paid their fine, enabling the divine judge to release them justly.

Strongest feature: The courtroom formulation concisely identifies the indispensable substitutionary mechanism, connecting acknowledged human culpability, Christ's payment, judicial release, and divine justice in an immediately intelligible argumentative sequence. Principal limitation: Monetary fines are transferable liabilities, whereas personal punishment and moral blame ordinarily require additional justification before another person's suffering can legitimately discharge the offender's responsibility. Live burden: The account must articulate a principled correspondence between civil debt payment and penal substitution, including why voluntary innocence-bearing satisfies justice instead of merely relocating punishment. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects excellent central relevance and recoverable structure, moderated by underdeveloped transfer conditions, limited independent warrant, and an analogy whose disanalogies remain substantively consequential.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 88/100

A sovereign God who creates and enforces the rules could forgive directly, making Jesus's blood sacrifice apparently unnecessary.

Strongest feature: The challenge targets atonement's indispensable necessity premise, asking why the sovereign creator, rule-maker, enforcer, and judge cannot forgive directly instead of requiring a sinless sacrifice. Principal limitation: Direct forgiveness is proposed without a developed account of justice, restoration, victims' interests, or reasons divine nature might constrain forgiveness independently of arbitrarily selected rules. Live burden: The objection must show that direct mercy can address wrongdoing adequately, while the affirmative must identify a noncircular principle explaining why justice specifically requires substitutionary suffering. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise central engagement, disciplined interrogative calibration, and strong exposure of an unexplained necessity claim, with modest restraint because a complete restorative alternative remains underdeveloped.

Conditions and efficacy of salvation

9:39–96:37

Ray Comfort — section score 79/100

Acknowledging guilt, repenting, and trusting the crucified and risen Christ bring eternal life as an unearned divine gift.

Strongest feature: The move clearly completes the affirmative salvation sequence by distinguishing gifted eternal life from earned reward and identifying acknowledgment, repentance, and trust in the risen Christ as operative conditions. Principal limitation: The promised efficacy is stated with certainty, although the resurrection, the mechanism connecting faith to forgiveness, and the availability of eternal life receive no independent verification within the offered support. Live burden: The affirmative must specify how these conditions relate, explain what successful trust produces, and provide truth-sensitive grounds for believing that the promised outcome follows. Locked score: The strong/competent band reflects excellent relevance, stable formulation, and substantial internal coherence, tempered by incomplete mechanism specification, limited evidential warrant, and categorical confidence exceeding the demonstrated premises.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 88/100

Jesus told the rich ruler to keep commandments, relinquish possessions, and follow him, complicating salvation by repentance and faith alone.

Strongest feature: The reply introduces a specific scriptural counterexample that directly tests the affirmative's stated salvation conditions by highlighting commandments, relinquishment of possessions, and following Jesus in the rich ruler narrative. Principal limitation: The narrative's diagnostic purpose remains contestable because those demands might expose misplaced confidence or function as evidence of repentance rather than constitute meritorious conditions for salvation. Live burden: The skeptic must establish the passage's operative soteriological meaning, while the affirmative must reconcile its commands with grace through faith without merely asserting a preferred doctrinal interpretation. Locked score: The very strong band reflects strong textual specificity, central relevance, and effective contradiction pressure, moderated by interpretive ambiguity concerning whether obedience earns salvation, evidences transformation, or reveals inability.

Evidence, revelation, and warranted trust

24:19–95:56

Ray Comfort — section score 58/100

Creation and scripture disclose God, while denial reflects suppression of recognized truth to avoid the moral responsibility God demands.

Strongest feature: The move offers a recognizable epistemic route through creation and scripture and connects that route to the affirmative's account of moral knowledge and accountability. Principal limitation: It uses scripture to validate scripture-grounded claims and universalizes a psychological diagnosis of suppression without independently distinguishing sincere evidential disagreement from motivated denial. Live burden: The reply must provide public criteria showing that creation discloses the specific God asserted and that unbelievers knowingly suppress this recognition rather than reasonably interpreting ambiguous evidence differently. Locked score: The weak band reflects genuine subsidiary relevance and a recoverable thesis, but substantial deficiencies in circular support, scope stability, responsiveness to the preceding moral objections, evidential warrant, and charitable representation of nonbelief.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 89/100

Presupposing God as judge without evidence cannot independently establish the gospel's truth or demonstrate its internal moral coherence.

Strongest feature: The reply identifies the unestablished divine-judge premise with exceptional precision and explains why scripture, culpable-suppression claims, and courtroom analogies cannot independently validate conclusions that already assume it. Principal limitation: Internal coherence can sometimes be evaluated conditionally without first establishing a worldview's truth, so the objection should separate external warrant from contradictions or implications assessed within stipulated premises. Live burden: The skeptic must clarify which gospel conclusions require independent evidence and which fail even conditionally, while the affirmative must supply noncircular grounds for divine authority. Locked score: The very strong band reflects decisive responsiveness, stable scope, high relevance, and powerful epistemic diagnosis, with limited reservation because truth-establishment and conditional coherence analysis are not identical tasks.
