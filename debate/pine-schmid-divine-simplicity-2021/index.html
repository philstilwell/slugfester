Overall assessment

Gregory Pine — Divine simplicity follows from God as the uncaused, purely actual source of being: 75/100

Published strengths

  • Pine clearly distinguishes essentially dependent causation from historical succession, giving the argument for a sustaining source an accessible and relevant explanatory foundation.
  • His distinction between rational and real differences offers a substantive defense against treating every distinct divine description as an additional intrinsic component.
  • Pine candidly separates confidence in the Thomistic system from demonstrated understanding of creative willing, accurately acknowledging unsurveyed alternatives and unresolved explanatory demands.

Published weaknesses

  • The initial causal argument moves from an unmoved terminus to unrestricted pure actuality without excluding unused capacities that Schmid subsequently identifies.
  • Repeated appeals to causal, nonreceptive knowledge leave Schmid's expressly permitted causal direction untouched and fail to discriminate contingent creation from possible noncreation.
  • Precontainment of temporal changes is offered against explanatory differences between possible creations, leaving the specific demand for a discriminating total explanation substantially unanswered.

Joseph Schmid — Divine simplicity faces unresolved objections and is not established over alternative models of God: 87/100

Published strengths

  • Schmid grants the motion argument's dependence premises while isolating the unsupported transition to universal immovability, maintaining unusually precise scope and burden control.
  • The aloneness argument and factivity rejoinder distinguish necessary possibilities from contingent actuality without assuming that divine knowledge passively receives information from creatures.
  • He supplies explicit tests for intrinsic multiplicity and clarifies modal terminology, addressing the classical framework without treating every conceptual distinction as a part.

Published weaknesses

  • The necessary-propositions objection relies on distinct existent truth bearers without adequately excluding Pine's rational-distinction account of divine ideas and intelligible multiplicity.
  • The changing-truth argument does not adequately exclude timelessly indexed knowledge, leaving its transition from changing water to successive divine cognition insufficiently secured.
  • The explanatory difference defense overstates the consequences of denial and leaves the principled stopping point for differentially influential basic reasons underdeveloped.

From dependent motion to pure actuality

Gregory Pine — section score 78/100

Principal limitation: establishing a source independent of this particular dependent series does not establish the impossibility of every unrealized capacity; the illustration supports termination more directly than unrestricted divine actuality. Live burden: Schmid's subsequent distinction between being actually unmoved and being incapable of change requires an additional exclusion argument; a causally irrelevant potency would otherwise survive the proposed explanation of derivative motion.
