Overall assessment

Mike Licona — Resurrection hypothesis: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Established an early Pauline and apostolic evidential foundation that directly addressed the historical-appearance burden.
  • Used explicit explanatory criteria rather than relying solely on the failure of a single naturalistic alternative.
  • Correctly argued that disciplinary investigation need not require laboratory repeatability and that event classification can be separated from precise agent identification.
  • Acknowledged qualifications, including uncertainty about the particular cause of a purportedly law-transcending event.

Published weaknesses

  • The affirmative repeatedly moved from unresolved phenomena or comparative weaknesses to supernaturalism, resurrection, or probable theism without supplying sufficiently discriminating causal bridges.

Matt Dillahunty — Skeptical evidentialism: 88/100

Published strengths

  • Consistently separated evidence that an event occurred from evidence identifying its cause.
  • Directly challenged the accessibility and independence of the Pauline, apostolic, and five-hundred appearance reports.
  • Explained why eliminating fraud or naturalistic rivals does not automatically provide resurrection with positive explanatory content.
  • Maintained a calibrated withholding position that generally avoided claiming the reported events were disproved.

Published weaknesses

  • Several formulations made the skeptical threshold too absolute, especially treating prior belief in a divine agent or positive supernatural ontology as necessary before evidential updating could occur.

Historical foundation for the appearance claims

18:03–116:27

Mike Licona — section score 81/100

Paul's early testimony, claimed experience, apostolic contacts, and shared proclamation support historical claims of individual, hostile, and group resurrection appearances.

Strongest feature: The move identifies an early source, connects Paul with Jesus's disciples, and constructs a historically significant evidential foundation encompassing individual, hostile, and group appearance traditions central to the affirmative case. Principal limitation: Its virtually-undisputed framing compresses important distinctions among reported belief, subjective experience, transmitted proclamation, and veridical appearance, while the breadth of scholarly agreement and independence of corroboration remain insufficiently qualified. Live burden: The affirmative must establish precisely what the earliest sources warrant, how independently the traditions support one another, and why their evidential accessibility and reliability justify treating the asserted experiences as historical data requiring explanation. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects excellent burden relevance, clear source-based organization, and meaningful historical warrant, moderated by scope instability and somewhat overstated evidential conclusions.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 89/100

Paul offers limited direct access to Jesus's life, death, burial, and tomb, while surviving reports do not independently demonstrate a physical return from death.

Strongest feature: The reply grants Paul's importance while identifying his lack of direct access to Jesus's ministry, execution, burial, and empty-tomb history, challenging the affirmative's historical foundation. Principal limitation: Direct eyewitness access to every event is unnecessary for historical knowledge, and the criticism does not assess Paul's claimed experience, apostolic contacts, early tradition, or corroboration from other sources. Live burden: The skeptical side must examine the transmission chain, independence, dating, and content of the appearance traditions, and show why their combined warrant remains insufficient despite Paul's proximity to witnesses. Locked score: The very strong band reflects focused source criticism, careful calibration, relevance, and responsiveness, while recognizing the need for fuller engagement with indirect historical corroboration and testimonial convergence.

Resurrection versus naturalistic explanations

27:47–119:15

Mike Licona — section score 80/100

Using explanatory scope, power, plausibility, and limited ad hocness, resurrection allegedly explains Paul and reported group appearances better than hallucination.

Strongest feature: The move uses recognizable inference-to-best-explanation criteria, grants hallucination meaningful scope and economy, and directly compares its expected consequences with Paul and the asserted group appearances. Principal limitation: The treatment narrows hallucination to insufficiently developed variants, assumes contested historical data, and omits fuller consideration of priors, alternative psychological models, mixed explanations, transmission error, and other naturalistic possibilities. Live burden: The affirmative must compare strengthened rival hypotheses under the same criteria, defend the reliability of every fact being explained, and show that resurrection contributes discriminating explanatory content rather than benefiting chiefly from weaknesses assigned to competitors. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects central relevance and genuine comparative reasoning, tempered by underdeveloped rival modeling, limited representational charity, and slightly overstated confidence.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 90/100

Eliminating hoax or lacking a satisfactory natural account does not establish resurrection when divine action supplies no discriminating mechanism or explanatory content.

Strongest feature: The move clearly separates defeating a rival explanation from positively supporting resurrection and directly challenges whether divine action contributes testable, discriminating, or causally informative explanatory content. Principal limitation: Equating every divine explanation with magic overstates the criticism because intentional-agent explanations can sometimes explain without specifying a physical mechanism, provided independent evidence identifies the agent, capability, and relevant intention. Live burden: The skeptical case must specify what positive content a minimally adequate resurrection hypothesis would require and why the affirmative's historical, theological, and comparative considerations fail to supply that content. Locked score: The very strong band reflects direct contact with the central comparative bridge, excellent diagnostic responsiveness, and substantial evidential warrant, moderated by a somewhat categorical dismissal that understates possible agent-level explanations.

From anomaly to supernatural causation

50:28–129:06

Mike Licona — section score 79/100

Automatically preferring any natural explanation can prejudge anomalous evidence and prevent recognition of events that known natural causes cannot plausibly explain.

Strongest feature: The private-information hypothetical demonstrates how preferring any natural explanation could prejudge evidence and clarifies the affirmative concern about methodological exclusion rather than ordinary evidential caution. Principal limitation: Showing dogmatic exclusion defective does not establish a supernatural cause, distinguish one from unknown natural alternatives, or demonstrate that resurrection evidence possesses the hypothetical immediacy, specificity, and independently checkable informational strength. Live burden: The affirmative must provide a noncircular threshold for concluding known natural explanations inadequate and explain what positive features license supernatural classification after crossing it. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects a relevant challenge to prior exclusion and vivid counterexample, moderated by incomplete causal discrimination and limited transfer from the hypothetical to the historical case.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 89/100

Science and history cannot confirm supernatural causation without a positive definition and a method distinguishing that cause from error, fraud, hallucination, or unknown nature.

Strongest feature: The move sharply distinguishes investigating events attributed to supernatural causes from confirming a supernatural causal connection and identifies causal discrimination as the decisive methodological requirement. Principal limitation: Saying inquiry is barred until supernatural ontology is positively understood may demand more than causal investigation ordinarily requires, since hypotheses can sometimes be tested through predicted contrasts before their underlying ontology is comprehensively explained. Live burden: The skeptical case must articulate workable minimum criteria for supernatural attribution and demonstrate that resurrection evidence fails those criteria without treating every nonnatural hypothesis as methodologically inadmissible by definition. Locked score: The very strong band reflects central burden contact, precise event-cause separation, outstanding diagnostic responsiveness, and strong methodological coherence, tempered only by a somewhat excessive prerequisite concerning complete positive understanding of supernatural causation.

Historical method and standards for extraordinary claims

32:42–117:36

Mike Licona — section score 79/100

Historical and scientific disciplines employ different methods, so absence of controlled repeatability alone does not disqualify alleged supernatural events from responsible investigation.

Strongest feature: The reply correctly observes that disciplines employ different investigative methods and usefully rejects controlled laboratory repeatability as a universal prerequisite for examining singular historical or allegedly paranormal events. Principal limitation: Methodological diversity establishes admissibility for investigation, not a successful causal inference, and the announced examples remain insufficiently connected to standards capable of distinguishing supernatural action from testimony error, fraud, coincidence, psychology, or unknown natural processes. Live burden: The affirmative must specify discipline-appropriate verification and causal-discrimination procedures, then show that the cited phenomena and resurrection traditions satisfy them rather than merely falling outside repeatable experimentation. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects clear methodological precision, apt qualification, and direct engagement with exclusion concerns, reduced because the response only partially answers the stronger demand for warranted supernatural attribution.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 85/100

Resurrection proponents must overcome evidential withholding by showing their hypothesis superior, rather than expecting skepticism or the practical default to be disproved.

Strongest feature: The move correctly places the affirmative evidential burden on resurrection proponents and establishes a motion-level reason for withholding belief until comparative superiority receives adequate support. Principal limitation: Its null-hypothesis terminology shifts among practical default, assumed truth, unconfirmed proposition, and criminal-law analogy, risking the stronger claim that skepticism is true rather than simply evidentially appropriate. Live burden: The skeptical case must formulate withholding without reifying a null position and must examine whether the particular Pauline, apostolic, and appearance evidence actually satisfies or fails the proposed evidential threshold. Locked score: The strong and competent band reflects exceptional motion relevance, coherent burden allocation, and useful calibration of assent, reduced by conceptual instability and only partial engagement with the affirmative's concrete historical premises and explanatory criteria.

Background supernaturalism and identification of divine agency

6:39–132:38

Mike Licona — section score 71/100

Several independently reported paranormal phenomena collectively suggest that reality may include a supernatural dimension, supplying background plausibility for resurrection.

Strongest feature: The move offers a coherent cumulative-evidence strategy, explicitly presents supernaturalism as an empirically suggestive background proposition, and connects several announced experience categories to the debate's subsidiary plausibility burden. Principal limitation: The selected evidence only announces those categories, leaving supernatural dimension partly undefined and providing neither case verification nor disciplined comparison with psychological, testimonial, fraudulent, or presently unknown natural explanations. Live burden: The affirmative still must demonstrate that independently reliable cases survive ordinary causal screening and that their combined evidential force supports a supernatural ontology rather than merely a heterogeneous collection of unresolved anomalies. Locked score: The mixed band reflects substantial relevance and intelligible cumulative structure alongside materially overstated calibration, compressed inferential bridging, and comparatively underdeveloped evidential substantiation.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 80/100

Unexplained experiences cannot establish a supernatural realm while that category remains negatively defined, unconfirmed, and unsupported by discriminating causal investigation.

Strongest feature: The reply precisely distinguishes reports attributed to supernatural causes from confirmation of a supernatural realm, directly diagnosing the central inferential gap in the affirmative's cumulative background case. Principal limitation: It remains chiefly methodological and does not inspect representative paranormal cases, their safeguards, or the possibility that independently robust anomaly classes could acquire cumulative evidential force without individually establishing supernaturalism. Live burden: The skeptical side must show how ordinary causal screening applies to the strongest announced examples while preserving its appropriately limited conclusion that unexplained does not mean false, fraudulent, or necessarily natural. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, stable terminology, matched calibration, and coherent causal caution, with some evidential limitation because the displayed reply challenges the inference more thoroughly than the underlying cases.
