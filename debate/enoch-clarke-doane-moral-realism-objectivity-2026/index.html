Overall assessment

David Enoch — Robust moral realism vindicates objectivity: 84/100

Published strengths

  • Established a carefully qualified account of ordinary stance independence without pretending that discourse evidence alone completed the realist ontology.
  • Kept the exchange comparative by demanding that rival views demonstrate superior overall handling rather than merely identifying a difficulty potentially shared across theories.
  • Directly connected ought-judgment with first-person deliberative closure while clearly distinguishing completed deliberation from weakness of will or failed execution.
  • Conceded exceptional reflective anomalies and developed a coherent centrality-sensitive method for comparing their explanatory cost with failures concerning ordinary practice.
  • Repeatedly calibrated claims through explicit qualifications, including retreats from overstrong formulations and acknowledgment of phenomenological variation.

Published weaknesses

  • The rival-package replies repeatedly characterized iteration as a pseudo-problem without supplying the stable criterion needed to distinguish illegitimate repetition from a genuine higher-order practical question.
  • The indispensability argument compressed the bridge from usefulness in deliberation to ontological respectability, leaving the claimed parity with explanatory posits insufficiently defended.
  • The objection to noncognitivism invoked unspecified additional commitments, preventing a concrete comparison between those costs and realism's own theoretical commitments.

Justin Clarke-Doane — Realism does not uniquely settle action: 88/100

Published strengths

  • Distinguished mind-independent truth from action-settling objectivity and consistently connected that distinction to the motion's demanding deliberative requirement.
  • Developed a two-horn privilege dilemma that directly answered both the realist ontology and the attempt to dismiss iteration as a pseudo-problem.
  • Supplied an identifiable attitude-centered alternative, thereby meeting the comparative burden rather than relying exclusively on internal criticism of realism.
  • Used an informed rival-logics case to separate causal compliance from rational settlement and to challenge universalized first-person closure.
  • Defended the possible theoretical importance of rare foundational anomalies without overstating them as guaranteed refutations of the realist framework.

Published weaknesses

  • The deflationary alternative remained substantially programmatic and did not explain how objectivity, disagreement, compositional practice, and action guidance would be jointly preserved.
  • The informed-deliberator proposal lacked a developed competence criterion, sampling method, or safeguard against treating specialist artifacts as evidence about deliberation generally.
  • Several foundational and logical analogies established intelligible possibilities more securely than they established direct applicability to ordinary moral deliberation.

Ordinary objectivity and the realist foundation

1:29–9:19

David Enoch — section score 83/100

Ordinary judgments that humiliation is wrong present that wrongness as independent of anyone's preferences, social conventions, beliefs, or practices.

Strongest feature: The everyday humiliation example sharply separates moral wrongness from personal liking, communal convention, counterfactual belief, and prevailing practice, while the explicit qualification prevents this discourse premise from pretending already to demonstrate a complete realist metaphysics. Principal limitation: Illustrative linguistic evidence cannot establish how consistently ordinary speakers understand moral claims, and independence from familiar stances does not alone identify robust normative facts as the uniquely adequate explanation. Live burden: The argument must connect this carefully bounded characterization of ordinary objectivity to an affirmative ontology while answering the possibility that another theory could preserve the same independence. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent conceptual precision, direct contact with a central bridge, and disciplined calibration, tempered by limited empirical breadth and an intentionally incomplete realist inference.

Non-cognitivism as a comparative alternative

24:07–44:40

David Enoch — section score 86/100

Strongest feature: The reply establishes a disciplined comparative standard, correctly observing that a challenge shared across remotely plausible metanormative positions cannot by itself select against realism, while the immediate retreat from an initially stronger formulation demonstrates unusually careful calibration. Principal limitation: The move does not independently establish that every plausible alternative inherits the same difficulty, and common failure would remove discriminatory force without showing that action-settling objectivity is an illegitimate requirement. Live burden: The argument must evaluate the proposed attitude-centered alternative and demonstrate either equivalent residual difficulties or offsetting costs in its account of ordinary objectivity. Locked score: The very strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, logical structure, representational fairness, and burden relevance, with the remaining limitation confined to evidential support for the universal comparative premise rather than the comparison's validity.

Justin Clarke-Doane — section score 83/100

Strongest feature: The proposal answers the comparative-alternative demand directly by locating practical closure in deciding, prescribing, or adopting an action-directed attitude, thereby explaining structurally how one selected action excludes incompatible alternatives without relying on another truth-apt property's metaphysical privilege. Principal limitation: Building exclusion into the relevant attitude does not yet demonstrate that the attitude preserves ordinary objectivity, outperforms realist first-person closure, or avoids merely stipulating the desired connection between judgment and action. Live burden: The alternative must distinguish deliberative settlement from causal execution, accommodate akrasia, and specify how warranted criticism remains possible across conflicting prescriptions. Locked score: The very strong band recognizes central comparative relevance, coherent architecture, and broad responsiveness, while the proposal's acknowledged complications and underdeveloped objectivity-preserving resources constrain its evidential warrant.
