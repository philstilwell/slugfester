Overall assessment

Stephen Boyce — Historicity defense: 78/100

Published strengths

  • Built a cumulative Pauline case from Jewish identity, legal status, birth language, death, burial, and resurrection rather than relying on a single expression.
  • Developed a concrete biological-kinship inference from Paul's singular designation of the named James and distinguished it from generalized communal brother language.
  • Directly answered the allegorical reading of Galatians by separating the Hagar-and-Sarah framework from the fullness-of-time declaration.
  • Introduced pre-Christian Qumran interpretation as relevant context for an earthly Davidic messianic expectation.
  • Consistently distinguished the modest historical question from the much larger burden of proving theological claims.

Published weaknesses

  • The Hegesippus argument treated a later remembered family succession as categorical proof without adequately defending transmission or independence.
  • The Tacitus reply inferred possible archival consultation from specificity, although the same details could derive from informed report or Christian tradition.
  • The five-hundred-witness argument overstated practical investigability despite absent names, setting, testimony, and perceptual description.
  • The historical-versus-theological reply did not answer the documentary-loss calibration actually posed.

Richard Carrier — Mythicist challenge: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Separated physical embodiment from earthly location and directly showed why Pauline flesh, death, and resurrection language does not alone settle where events occurred.
  • Preserved disciplined ambiguity in the James exchange instead of claiming that a nonbiological interpretation had been proved.
  • Pressed concrete source-critical limitations involving Gospel anonymity, lateness, unnamed sources, literary dependence, and uncertain external corroboration.
  • Undercut the five-hundred report by identifying missing names, circumstances, perceptual content, and independent testimony.
  • Offered a positive ecstatic-event mechanism that directly answered both bodily-resurrection and group-appearance inferences.

Published weaknesses

  • The comparative-savior argument moved from broad motif resemblance to an exception burden without defining a defensible comparison class or base rate.
  • The Gospel critique used universal language about every chapter and every historical claim beyond the documentation presented.
  • The divine-manufacture reading of Davidic seed remained insufficiently defined and textually underargued.
  • The ecstatic explanation was presented as what actually happened despite limited comparative evidence for that specific mechanism.

Resurrection appearances, conversion, and early movement origins

101:58–154:30

Stephen Boyce — section score 67/100

Principal limitation: The inference moves from transformation and conviction to a bodily event without specifying the Damascus experience or excluding revelatory, visionary, and nonbodily explanations that could produce sustained conviction. Live burden: Boyce must connect Paul's changed belief to discriminating evidence about an executed body, while the alternative account must explain the conversion's intensity and resurrection-centered content without assuming sincerity settles causation. Locked score: The evaluation recognizes relevance while constrained by overstated warrant, unstable terminology, missing qualification, and inadequate comparison with alternatives.
