Overall assessment

David Wood — Theistic moral realism: 79/100

Published strengths

  • Landed a precise and forceful distinction between conditional guidance toward accepted goals and obligations that purport to bind a welfare dissenter.
  • Connected objective moral facts, authority, human worth, and moral cognition within a unified theistic route directly relevant to the motion.
  • Qualified moral intuition as fallible and distinguished ancient covenantal authorization from permissions claimed by present Christians.
  • Used the dissenter and harmful-end cases to expose a genuine normative bridge that the secular account needed to articulate more fully.

Published weaknesses

  • The proposed divine ground was repeatedly identified more clearly than it was demonstrated, leaving goodness, authority, and the exclusion of non-theistic realism underdeveloped.
  • The contrast with atheism sometimes became too sweeping, particularly when secular accounts of human nature, value, and cognitive reliability received insufficient consideration.

Matt Dillahunty — Secular humanism: 84/100

Published strengths

  • Landed the debate's strongest diagnostic reply by separating creative power from independently established goodness, commands, and moral authority.
  • Presented a fallible, evidence-sensitive, and revisable method for moral inquiry without reducing ethical judgment to majority opinion.
  • Explained mortal human significance through relationships, future agency, contributions, and effects with clear and proportionate claims.
  • Supplied practical secular resources including reciprocity, cooperation, due process, collective risk assessment, and proportionate intervention.

Published weaknesses

  • The shared-goal analogy established objective means-end comparisons more successfully than it established the categorical authority of well-being itself.
  • Reciprocity and unavoidable social participation were invoked as obligation-generating considerations without fully justifying the binding normative principle connecting them.

Objective Morality and Divine Grounding

12:35–127:16

David Wood — section score 75/100

Theism can ground nonphysical moral properties in God and universal obligations in the authoritative relationship between creator and persons.

Strongest feature: The constructive directly addresses the motion by connecting objective goodness, universal obligation, moral responsibility, and authoritative personal direction within one comparatively comprehensive theistic framework. Principal limitation: It identifies God as a possible metaphysical ground without sufficiently explaining the grounding relationship, independently establishing divine goodness, or deriving authority from creation rather than merely asserting it. Live burden: The pro side must clarify how divine nature constitutes goodness, why that nature generates obligations for persons, and why competing non-theistic realist foundations remain comparatively inadequate. Locked score: The strong band recognizes excellent motion relevance, coherent conceptual integration, and a readily recoverable thesis, while preserving substantial reservations about evidential warrant, metaphysical specification, unsupported explanatory confidence, and the uncompleted transition from divine existence to binding authority.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 88/100

Creative power alone establishes neither goodness nor moral authority, so theism must independently justify God's character, commands, and jurisdiction.

Strongest feature: The reply sharply distinguishes creating persons from possessing moral goodness or legitimate authority over them, exposing separate inferential bridges that the constructive had compressed together. Principal limitation: Contrasting this gap with secular goals and disagreement-resolution methods does not itself establish the categorical authority of those goals or show that practical methodology supplies moral ontology. Live burden: The con side must preserve its successful demand for independent justification while positively explaining why secular commitments generate obligations rather than only workable procedures and socially enforced expectations. Locked score: The very strong band reflects exceptional diagnostic precision, decisive responsiveness, stable terminology, proportionate force, and direct contact with the theistic route's central weakness, with only a residual comparative burden concerning the independent normativity of secular flourishing.

Flourishing, Consequences, and Binding Obligation

18:31–64:25

David Wood — section score 87/100

Welfare-based ethics remains conditional if rejecting its objective removes obligation, and harmful goals can make horrific conduct instrumentally effective.

Strongest feature: The objection precisely isolates the difference between effective means toward an accepted goal and categorical obligation, then stress-tests consequential reasoning through a dissenter committed to destructive purposes. Principal limitation: It overstates the secular position by suggesting that rejection leaves morality entirely speechless, overlooking possible arguments from vulnerability, reciprocity, social participation, and reasons applicable independently of personal motivation. Live burden: The pro side must show why those secular considerations remain merely prudential or institutional, while preserving the distinction between lacking motivation and lacking morally authoritative reasons. Locked score: The very strong band reflects decisive contact, unusually clear scope, forceful diagnostic structure, and direct pressure on the central secular bridge, tempered by an insufficiently charitable characterization of available secular resources and slightly excessive inferential force.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 79/100

Given shared human aims, physical consequences permit objective comparisons between choices and identify better or worse means for advancing flourishing.

Strongest feature: The chess analogy intelligibly shows how objective physical consequences can distinguish more effective from less effective conduct once participants share a determinate practical objective. Principal limitation: The reasoning moves too quickly from objectively assessable means toward objectively authoritative moral ends, leaving the categorical status of the governing commitment to well-being insufficiently justified. Live burden: The con side must explain why flourishing supplies reasons that bind dissenters, not merely conditional recommendations, while separately answering the evolutionary challenge concerning the reliability of naturalistic moral cognition. Locked score: The strong band reflects coherent instrumental reasoning, direct contact with the secular-foundation burden, and commendable real-time qualification, balanced against partial responsiveness, limited normative warrant, unstable foundational scope, and incomplete engagement with the distinct epistemological objection.

Human Value, Rights, and Cooperation

25:30–140:23

David Wood — section score 75/100

Creation and sustenance by God make morality reality-responsive, while bearing God's image provides persons with rights and morally significant worth.

Strongest feature: The reply supplies a unified reality-based account of obligation and human status, directly contrasting merely conditional goal pursuit with rights grounded in the divine image. Principal limitation: The asserted movement from image-bearing to determinate rights remains compressed, because neither the relevant characteristics nor the mechanism producing equal entitlements receives sufficient specification. Live burden: The pro side must identify which rights follow from divine image, explain why every person possesses them equally, and establish their content without depending on disputed interpretive assumptions. Locked score: The strong band recognizes direct burden contact, substantial responsiveness, stable overall scope, and an intelligible theological alternative, while registering limited evidential support, partly unstable terminology, implicit qualifications, and an underdeveloped inference from created resemblance or status to concrete universal protections.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 87/100

Mortality leaves powerful reasons to protect life because future agency, relationships, contributions, and effects on others remain deeply significant.

Strongest feature: The reply directly explains why mortality does not erase value, grounding serious reasons against premature death in future agency, relationships, contributions, and enduring effects upon others. Principal limitation: Relational significance establishes substantial concern but does not automatically secure equal intrinsic worth or universal rights for isolated, disliked, incapacitated, or minimally contributing persons. Live burden: The con side must show how its relational account protects every person comparably when social attachment, anticipated contribution, preference satisfaction, and reciprocal usefulness differ dramatically. Locked score: The very strong band recognizes a clear, proportionate, fully responsive, and intuitively powerful answer to the mortality challenge, while reserving the unresolved foundational question of whether variable relationships can support equal protection without importing a more intrinsic conception of status.

Moral Knowledge, Disagreement, and Correction

9:59–129:12

David Wood — section score 76/100

Evolutionary and social explanations may account for moral convictions without showing that those convictions accurately represent objective moral reality.

Strongest feature: The argument clearly distinguishes a causal genealogy of moral conviction from truth, directly engaging the indispensable epistemological question of whether naturalistic cognition reliably corresponds to independent moral facts. Principal limitation: Evolutionary influence alone does not establish unreliability, because selection, reflection, interpersonal correction, and evidence-sensitive deliberation could produce substantially dependable faculties despite their survival-related origins. Live burden: The pro side must demonstrate that naturalistic formation is systematically insensitive to moral truth, while addressing the strongest account on which evolved capacities become increasingly reliable through criticism and cooperative inquiry. Locked score: The mixed band reflects exceptional relevance and a recoverable challenge, moderated by materially overstated force, incomplete qualification, compressed warranting, and insufficient engagement with the principal reliability alternative.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 84/100

Evidence and internal consistency can reveal when ostensibly beneficial conduct actually conflicts with a professed commitment to human well-being.

Strongest feature: The reply candidly accepts human fallibility while explaining how empirical consequences and internal consistency can expose genocidal means that contradict a professed commitment to well-being. Principal limitation: Evidence most directly corrects factual mistakes and inconsistencies, but cannot by itself resolve every disagreement between coherent yet morally antagonistic conceptions of value or flourishing. Live burden: The con side must specify the normative standards governing evidence, explain their authority, and address agents whose harmful commitments remain internally consistent after all relevant consequences are understood. Locked score: The strong band recognizes a clear, proportionate, and substantially complete answer to the error-correction challenge, supported by methodological humility and direct responsiveness, while retaining reservations about limited empirical jurisdiction and an assumed resolution of disputes that may concern values rather than facts.

Motivation, Atrocity, and Practical Application

35:48–136:34

David Wood — section score 79/100

Theistic conversion can transform conduct by replacing human insignificance with recognition of objective duties toward persons who bear God's image.

Strongest feature: The autobiographical example gives concrete support to the carefully limited proposition that adopting theism can transform conduct by reinterpreting other persons as bearers of objective significance. Principal limitation: Practical transformation supplies evidence about motivational efficacy, not the truth of divine-image metaphysics, and one experience cannot establish general motivational superiority over secular commitments. Live burden: The pro side must connect transformation to the comparative foundation without treating beneficial consequences as independent confirmation of the theological beliefs producing them. Locked score: The strong band reflects a recoverable, appropriately qualified possibility claim, meaningful contact with motivational symmetry, and evidence adequate for the personal example, balanced against limited generalizability and the continuing separation between changed behavior and the ontological accuracy of the worldview credited for that change.

Matt Dillahunty — section score 81/100

Social life has unavoidable consequences, while invoking God neither guarantees motivation in indifferent agents nor prevents religiously rationalized atrocities.

Strongest feature: The reply sharply distinguishes society's judgment of obligation from the motivational reach of moral argument, then argues that invoking God encounters the same wholly indifferent agent, providing contact with challenged claims and a symmetry grounded in the supplied exchange. Principal limitation: Treating complete indifference as a shared motivational endpoint does not resolve whether secular and theistic accounts differ in the existence, authority, or categorical character of obligations, and the absolute impossibility language exceeds what this excerpt demonstrates. Live burden: Establish why motivational symmetry answers the authority question, while preserving the important distinction between ignored obligations and nonexistent obligations. Locked score: This remains a strong, responsive reply whose reach is narrower than its universal phrasing suggests.
