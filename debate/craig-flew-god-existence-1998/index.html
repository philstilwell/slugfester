Overall assessment

William Lane Craig — Theism: 74/100

Published strengths

  • Craig presented several independent routes to theism, allowing the weighted case to survive weaknesses in any single cosmological, moral, historical, or experiential inference.
  • His numbered causal syllogism connected the universe’s beginning to a cause with exceptional responsiveness, relevance, clarity, and a strong locked move score.
  • His libertarian and Molinist replies precisely distinguished divine cooperation from determination, producing the strongest pro moves in the morality and freedom section.
  • Craig kept resurrection evidence comparative by emphasizing the empty tomb, appearances, transformed belief, and Flew’s failure to supply a rival naturalistic explanation.

Published weaknesses

  • Craig treated actual infinity as self-contradictory too quickly, leaving mathematical subtraction examples insufficiently connected to the metaphysical impossibility of an infinite past.
  • The design trilemma asserted that law and chance fail without fully establishing cosmic probabilities or addressing Flew’s demand for an applicable reference class.
  • The moral grounding case moved from objective wrongness to God while underdefending why secular objective standards cannot supply authority independently of divine command.

Antony Flew — Atheistic skepticism: 77/100

Published strengths

  • Flew framed his burden narrowly as insufficient reasons rather than disproof, earning the strongest epistemic scores through clarity, charity, relevance, and disciplined calibration.
  • His domain objection sharply separated causal experience within the universe from claims about the universe’s origin, directly pressuring Craig’s central cosmological premise.
  • His closing reference-class challenge exposed the unresolved basis for cosmic probability assignments and secured con’s weighted advantage within the contested fine-tuning section.
  • Flew challenged resurrection evidence through document chronology and differences between Pauline visions and later bodily narratives, thereby narrowly winning the weighted historical section.

Published weaknesses

  • Flew’s eternal-person objection was compressed and insufficiently precise about why timeless agency is inconsistent, making it his weakest locked cosmological response by far.
  • His claim that omnipotence could guarantee freely chosen obedience initially blurred constitution with determination, allowing Craig’s libertarian impossibility reply to land strongly.
  • Flew often defended evidential insufficiency without offering competing explanations for Craig’s historical data, leaving the cumulative resurrection inference only partially and indirectly answered.

Cosmological causation and the universe’s beginning

4:04–1:29:13

William Lane Craig — section score 75/100

Craig argues that contradictory operations involving actual infinities make an infinite past impossible, intending to establish that the temporal universe must have begun.

Strongest feature: Craig articulates an inference from contradictory instantiated infinity, using infinity-minus-infinity indeterminacy and Hilbert’s authority, through finitely many past events to the universe’s beginning. Principal limitation: Mathematical nonuniqueness neither demonstrates metaphysical inconsistency in concrete infinities nor licenses transferring abstract cardinal behavior to temporal reality; moreover, a beginning leaves divine causation unestablished. Live burden: Craig must justify that abstraction-to-instantiation bridge across temporal theories, while Flew must explain specifically why the purported contradictions cannot constrain an actually infinite history. Locked score: The exact 69 reflects logical coherence 60 and evidence warrant 59 for the underdefended bridge, responsiveness 84 and relevance and burden 80 for engaging the cosmological premise, precision and clarity 77 for clear progression, and calibration and charity 58 for overstating the illustration and authority.

Antony Flew — section score 75/100

Flew argues that explanation must terminate somewhere and may reasonably stop at the Big Bang, resisting Craig’s inference from cosmic beginning to divine cause.

Strongest feature: Flew contests Craig’s transcendent-cause inference by reasoning that explanatory chains terminate in brute facts and the Big Bang could be the epistemic terminus, blocking progression from cosmic beginning to divinity. Principal limitation: Necessary termination does not establish the universe as an appropriate brute fact, and shifting between knowledge’s endpoint and metaphysical explanation’s endpoint obscures the unsupported stopping criterion. Live burden: Flew must justify stopping cosmically without special pleading, while Craig must demonstrate that a timeless cause improves explanation instead of relocating its demand. Fine-tuning and design inference

William Lane Craig — section score 72/100

Craig emphasizes the delicate balance of life-permitting initial conditions against vastly more numerous prohibiting possibilities, intending their extreme improbability to require explanation.

Strongest feature: Craig isolates that life-permitting initial conditions occupy a delicate range against life-prohibiting alternatives, making their occurrence surprising and supporting design. Principal limitation: Calling the odds incalculable without specifying admissible possibilities or a defensible probability measure leaves the inference vulnerable to reference-class, selection-effect, and unknown-law objections and cannot identify agency. Live burden: Craig must define the comparison space and distribution, address observational selection, and establish design over chance or necessity, while Flew must explain why specified life-permission remains unsurprising if those foundations are supplied. Morality, freedom, hell, and divine goodness

14:15–1:21:37

William Lane Craig — section score 78/100

Craig distinguishes evolved taboo from genuine wrongness, arguing that atheistic human origins cannot provide objective authority and intending morality to require God.

Strongest feature: Craig distinguishes moral knowledge and conduct from ontological grounding, arguing from humanity’s evolutionary contingency and rape’s adaptive taboo toward atheism’s inability to confer authority. Principal limitation: This genealogy-to-truth inference remains unsupported because biological origins neither disprove secular moral realism nor exclude agency-based reasons and nondivine foundations, which Craig never evaluates. Live burden: Craig must establish divine grounding’s superiority without conflating causal provenance with justification, while Flew must explain how shared standards become categorically binding. Locked score: The exact 69 reflects logical coherence 60 and evidence warrant 52 for the unproven exclusivity inference, responsiveness 88 and relevance and burden 82 for advancing morality’s Godward route, precision and clarity 81 for separating ontology from behavior, and calibration and charity 51 for neglecting formidable secular alternatives.

Antony Flew — section score 75/100

Flew compares finite human offenses with endless punishment, arguing that infinite consequences violate proportional justice and burden claims about a perfectly good God.

Live burden: Flew must connect infinite punishment to Craig’s God-concept and answer desert theories, while Craig must reconcile endless consequences with finite agency, proportional justice, and goodness. Live burden: Craig must substantiate the event and demonstrate why God’s miracle outperforms alternatives, while Flew must show why the facts fail proportionate historical standards or favor another explanation. Religious experience, personhood, and epistemic burden

William Lane Craig — section score 66/100

Strongest feature: Craig separates public demonstration from immediate experiential warrant, using encounters and Hick’s comparison with inescapable realities to explain how awareness could prima facie justify theistic belief. Principal limitation: These illustrations establish psychological immediacy, not veridical identification, because inference from experience to God remains vulnerable to perceptual error, incompatible religious encounters, and nonconfirmation. Live burden: Craig must provide reliability tests and explain how private warrant advances the motion; Flew must address justification without miscasting it as proof.
