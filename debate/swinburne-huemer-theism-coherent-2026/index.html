Overall assessment

Richard Swinburne — Coherent theism: 75/100

Published strengths

  • Swinburne supplied a coherent modal boundary for omnipotence by excluding contradictory descriptions from the range of genuine actions.
  • His unified-ability account directly challenged the assumption that every unrestricted capacity must be decomposed into infinitely many scalar powers.
  • He clearly separated a rejected timeless reconciliation from his adopted everlasting temporal conception.
  • The two-natures distinction gave a precise and highly responsive answer to the incarnation challenge.

Published weaknesses

  • The foreknowledge argument asserted incompatibility with freedom without answering the strongest dependence-based or compatibilist alternatives.
  • The supertask reply wrongly inferred a last positive-duration period from the premise that every event occupies some period.
  • Several tradition-based replies narrowed the target without sufficiently documenting the relevant historical conception or answering residual quantitative infinity.

Michael Huemer — Incoherence critique: 83/100

Published strengths

  • Huemer's no-greatest-finite-power reply exposed the missing upper bound in the proposed maximal-power account.
  • His residual-knowledge argument showed that restricting future free choices leaves mathematical, past, and present truths untouched.
  • The incarnation objection tested abstract bodilessness against a central Christian doctrine rather than an incidental example.
  • His lamp response granted positive event duration and then accurately distinguished that premise from the existence of a last interval.

Published weaknesses

  • The opening infinity argument did not fully establish that unrestricted attributes constitute impossible non-extensive magnitudes rather than extensive ranges.
  • The timelessness objection overlooked Swinburne's explicit rejection of timelessness and therefore criticized a position he did not adopt.
  • The spatial-infinity defense relied on an insufficiently defended actualist ontology of points and segments.

Maximal power and quantitative infinity

5:40–35:24

Richard Swinburne — section score 77/100

Omnipotence is the greatest logically possible power over genuine actions, excludes contradictory descriptions, and remains compatible with goodness when evaluative knowledge faces no conflicting desires.

Strongest feature: This central reply gives omnipotence an intelligible modal boundary, distinguishes genuine actions from contradictory descriptions, and integrates maximal power with a recognizable account of perfectly informed goodness. Principal limitation: Excluding logical impossibilities does not establish that a greatest realizable power exists, and the language of an amount remains uncomfortable beside the proposed range-of-actions interpretation. Live burden: Swinburne must explain the ordering relation that makes one power genuinely greatest without becoming an impossible scalar infinity, while separately addressing Huemer's parallel objections concerning knowledge and intelligence. Locked score: The strong band reflects direct burden contact, explicit qualification, and substantial conceptual coherence, tempered by an underdefended maximality bridge and incomplete coverage of the broader quantitative-infinity case.

Michael Huemer — section score 84/100

Traditional maximal theism appears incoherent because unlimited divine power, knowledge, and intelligence would constitute impossible quantitative, non-extensive infinities.

Strongest feature: This motion-level constructive clearly identifies the alleged contradiction, connects several maximal divine attributes to quantitative infinity, and supplies a unified target for the subsequent exchange. Principal limitation: Its crucial transition from unrestricted ranges of abilities or truths to a single impossible non-extensive magnitude remains insufficiently demonstrated, while traditional theism's conceptual diversity receives inadequate qualification. Live burden: Huemer must establish that omnipotence, omniscience, and intelligence necessarily instantiate measurable magnitudes rather than indefinitely extensive domains, dispositions, or systematically unified representational capacities. Locked score: The strong but not decisive band reflects exceptional relevance and an intelligible organizing argument, moderated by unstable terminology, compressed historical scope, and an underdeveloped metaphysical bridge requiring substantial additional defense.

Divine knowledge, freedom, and the relevant theistic conception

12:25–33:27

Richard Swinburne — section score 72/100

The impossibility of quantitative infinity does not establish Christian theism's incoherence unless the historically relevant conception actually attributes infinity in those disputed senses.

Strongest feature: This reply appropriately narrows the dispute by questioning whether Huemer's quantitative conception belongs to historically relevant Christian theism, and its cautious formulation avoids claiming comprehensive doctrinal unanimity. Principal limitation: Doctrinal non-unanimity does not independently answer the conceptual impossibility argument or establish that prominent maximal formulations are irrelevant to the motion, especially without representative historical substantiation. Live burden: Swinburne must specify which theological conception governs the debate, document its historical significance, and explain why its retained attributes escape Huemer's underlying quantitative analysis. Locked score: The mixed band recognizes coherent scope control and meaningful contact with an applicability premise, while reflecting incomplete responsiveness, limited evidential development, and failure to resolve the central metaphysical objection itself.

Michael Huemer — section score 86/100

Removing future free choices from divine foreknowledge leaves infinitely many mathematical, past, and present truths requiring knowledge and integrated cognitive use.

Strongest feature: This highly responsive reply grants Swinburne's proposed foreknowledge restriction and then identifies concrete residual truth classes sufficient to preserve an infinite cognitive domain despite that restriction. Principal limitation: It moves too quickly from infinitely many known propositions to an impossible quantitative magnitude of knowledge or intelligence, leaving the extensive-versus-non-extensive distinction inadequately defended. Live burden: Huemer must explain why cardinal multiplicity generates an objectionable cognitive quantity rather than an unrestricted representational range, and why systematic compression or unified grasp cannot block that inference. Locked score: The very strong band reflects precise diagnostic contact, exceptional relevance, concrete examples, and comprehensive engagement with multiple preceding replies, moderated by an unresolved bridge between infinite informational extension and impossible cognition.

Actual infinity, divisibility, and supertasks

37:00–48:20

Richard Swinburne — section score 69/100

One-to-one correlation between divisions of an action and an infinite numerical sequence cannot alone establish that the action contains a genuine actual infinity.

Strongest feature: This diagnostic reply correctly observes that representational correlation alone cannot establish the ontology of what is represented and reasonably demands an additional criterion for genuine actual infinity. Principal limitation: Its categorical statement about every series is insufficiently bounded, and it attacks correlation as sufficient while leaving Huemer's stronger explicit premise that spatial points and segments actually exist unanswered. Live burden: Swinburne must engage that realist premise directly, specify a defensible criterion separating potential from actual infinity, and restrict the correlation argument to mathematically appropriate domains. Locked score: The mixed band recognizes high responsiveness to one inferential route and a valuable insufficiency objection, while reflecting overstatement, reduced charity, weak positive warrant, and incomplete contact with the opponent's best formulation.

Michael Huemer — section score 88/100

Finite spatial traversal involves actually existing infinitely many points and segments, although traversing that extensive infinity requires only finite power.

Strongest feature: This careful reply distinguishes actual extensive infinity from infinite quantitative power, directly challenges the claim that mathematical subdivision is merely artificial, and concedes finite traversal requirements. Principal limitation: Its conclusion depends on an actualist ontology under which all spatial points or segments genuinely exist, rather than space being merely potentially divisible or differently constituted. Live burden: Huemer must defend that ontology and explain how the spatial example illuminates the targeted divine magnitudes without confusing harmless cardinal extension with allegedly impossible non-extensive quantity. Locked score: The strong band reflects excellent responsiveness, explicit qualification, stable terminology, and disciplined concession, moderated by limited support for the decisive ontological premise and the example's subsidiary rather than motion-completing significance.

Bodilessness, incarnation, and divine action

39:51–45:23

Richard Swinburne — section score 84/100

Walking, marrying, and comparable bodily activities are not missing divine powers because their descriptions conflict logically with God's necessarily bodiless nature.

Strongest feature: This constructive supplies a clear category distinction between genuine limitations and predicates logically incompatible with necessary bodilessness, giving the apparent-inabilities bridge a disciplined modal structure. Principal limitation: Necessary bodilessness is treated largely as definitional, although Christian incarnation creates an immediate doctrinal complication requiring more than reiteration of the stipulated divine nature. Live burden: Swinburne must justify the necessity claim independently, distinguish person-level from nature-level predication, and show why excluding bodily activities preserves rather than revisionarily narrows recognizable omnipotence. Locked score: The strong band reflects stable scope, explicit qualification, considerable logical coherence, and direct central relevance, tempered by incomplete warrant for the governing definition and an initially unresolved compatibility problem involving incarnation.

Michael Huemer — section score 84/100

Incarnation challenges necessary divine bodilessness because Christians identify Jesus as God, and Jesus was embodied, walked, and apparently could have married.

Strongest feature: This concise challenge identifies the strongest doctrinal counterexample to necessary divine bodilessness and directly tests Swinburne's abstract predicate exclusion against the incarnation central to Christianity. Principal limitation: It leaves implicit the standard distinction between the one incarnate person and his divine and human natures, so the transfer from bodily personal predication to embodied divine nature remains unsettled. Live burden: Huemer must show why true statements that God walked through the incarnate Christ entail bodily capacities in the divine nature, rather than activities mediated through humanity. Locked score: The strong band reflects exceptional responsiveness, immediate doctrinal relevance, precise challenge format, and substantial prima facie force, tempered by an unresolved nature-level distinction and correspondingly limited completeness.

Timelessness, change, and foreknowledge

26:06–43:42

Richard Swinburne — section score 79/100

Christian tradition includes a temporally responsive God who changes intentions or judgments, so exhaustive foreknowledge is not mandatory for every relevant theistic conception.

Strongest feature: This historically oriented reply limits its conclusion to doctrinal non-unanimity and supplies a concrete route by which a temporal God could remain responsive without exhaustive foreknowledge. Principal limitation: Changing judgments, revising intentions, and lacking advance knowledge are insufficiently distinguished, while the historical assertion receives little representative evidence and bypasses the immediately pressing power objection. Live burden: Swinburne must document the tradition invoked, clarify the metaphysical relationship among divine temporality, responsiveness, and ignorance, and show that the resulting conception remains recognizably motion-relevant. Locked score: The mixed band rewards cautious calibration, intelligible doctrinal scope, and meaningful subsidiary support, while registering limited evidential warrant, only partial responsiveness, and no resolution of Huemer's central residual-infinity reasoning.

Michael Huemer — section score 68/100

A timeless God cannot change intentions because existence outside temporal succession conflicts with possessing successive incompatible mental states.

Strongest feature: This reply states a genuine conditional incompatibility clearly: timeless existence cannot straightforwardly accommodate succession between incompatible intentions or judgments without further metaphysical explanation. Principal limitation: It applies that conditional criticism to Swinburne despite his immediately explicit rejection of timelessness, thereby missing the decisive qualification that his adopted conception is everlasting and temporal. Live burden: Huemer must redirect the objection toward a position actually affirmed, or show that Swinburne implicitly retains timeless commitments incompatible with his stated temporal account. Locked score: The mixed band reflects direct relevance to a possible repair conflict and an intelligible conditional insight, but substantially reduced representational charity, defective scope control, materially overstated application, and incomplete responsiveness to the opponent's actual position.
