# v4.1.6 Score-Failure Analysis

The failed gate is narrow, real, and not attributable to a mechanical defect. Exact compiler replay, all three source-schema validations, and the one-pass score derivation remain intact. The six final signed score differences from the retired comparator are +2, +5, +3, +6, +5, and +5. Their mean is +4.33 and median is +5; every new total is at or above its retired counterpart.

The relative judgments are much more stable than the absolute scale. All three winners are preserved, and the new winning margins differ from the retired margins by at most three points. Adjudication moved five of six side totals strictly closer to the comparator and left the sixth unchanged. Thus the third pass improved legacy agreement; it did not cause the miss.

The strongest remaining explanation is a systematic scale or representation difference. The lean ledger scores 34 selected moves where the retired comparator scored 81, retaining about 42% of the old move population. That compression may emphasize the most score-relevant moves and omit weaker but still burden-relevant material. It also operates under tightened v4 rubric anchors. The data do not isolate those two effects, so the audit does not claim a unique cause.

The comparator is useful but not a gold standard: its own frozen reliability artifact reported a 0.885714 six-side rank correlation against a 0.90 requirement and marked its reliability gate failed. Even so, the v4.1.6 acceptance rule made the five-point absolute threshold binding, and the present result therefore remains a failure.

No post-hoc four-point offset, threshold relaxation, formula change, or automatic re-adjudication is justified on these same three cases. My recommendation is to treat Debates 55, 103, and 161 as development diagnostics, prospectively freeze the next policy, and validate it on a fresh disjoint sample of two-speaker debates. The key editorial choice is whether the new 195-debate series should prioritize internal consistency under the tightened rubric or numerical continuity with the retired score scale.
