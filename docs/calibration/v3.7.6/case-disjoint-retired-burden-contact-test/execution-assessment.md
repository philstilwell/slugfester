## The adjudicated dispute

`burden-contact-disjoint-152-08` split between an attack on the pro side's moral-lawgiver bridge and no route contact. The excerpt says groups use political or religious reasons to justify conduct they already know is evil. Pass A treated prior moral recognition plus dogmatic rationalization as an attack on the claim that objective obligations require a transcendent lawgiver. Passes B and C applied a stricter proposition test: misuse of religion is compatible with either account of moral grounding and therefore does not itself supply a reason against the lawgiver claim.

The two-vote no-contact resolution is the narrower reading. The dispute is useful rather than merely procedural: it identifies the remaining boundary between a criticism of religious moral practice and an argument about the metaphysical ground of objective obligation. The current exact-contact rule resolved it without adding a new label or changing the threshold after observation.

The single final difference from the provisional retired reference is this same case. Reference agreement was not a gate condition, and the stable two-vote result must not be used to rewrite a benchmark or presented as human ground truth.

## Quality judgment

Engineering, isolation, provenance, deterministic semantic decoding, adjudication scope, and cost control are at an **A level**. Composite burden-contact classification is **A-minus within this retired dyadic test**: it met a demanding 11-of-12 initial threshold and resolved the sole dispute cleanly, but it passed with no margin above the threshold. This warrants a held-out integration preregistration, not production rollout.

The overall reassessment workflow is still not ready for all 195 debates. This test isolates one field and supplies atomic excerpts rather than asking the scoring passes to reconstruct a full debate. All selected speaker attributions were high confidence, so the mandatory audio-verification branch was enforced but not exercised. Numerical scoring, Overall Commentary, and AI Extension were prohibited and remain untested under the corrected burden representation.

One documentation issue should be corrected before the held-out gate: the canonical v3.7.6 workflow file still describes the exposed eight-case smoke and its perfect-agreement threshold, while the disjoint test's actual 12-case threshold lives in the separately frozen manual and manifests. This did not alter any individual semantic choice or the deterministic pass formula, but future model-facing workflow text should contain only invariant decision rules; sample-specific thresholds and claims should live exclusively in the test manifest.
