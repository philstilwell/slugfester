For EACH candidate, judge these SEPARATE questions as yes, no, or unclear:

FORM: Is this a compact, repeatable affirmation or catchphrase presenting a general
conclusion as settled? An ordinary argument with contested premises is not a slogan
merely because you find it weak. Brevity, memorable phrasing, metaphor, humor, and
emotion alone are not reasoning defects. Set form=no when a protected claim is not
slogan-like. Do not force all protected arguments into the slogan category.

SUBSTITUTION: In its actual use, does the affirmation substitute for a reason not
supplied? Repeating a conclusion, invoking group loyalty, comfort, fear, or the very
authority under dispute may do this. But a concise summary of supporting reasons
genuinely given elsewhere in this exchange is not a substitute for missing reasons.
Read the relevant earlier argument and later response. A disputed premise or an
incomplete argument alone does not establish slogan substitution. Identify the
specific inferential work the affirmation is being made to do.

PROTECTION: Is there POSITIVE TEXTUAL EVIDENCE that objections or contrary results
are neutralized regardless of merits? Examples: treating every possible outcome as
confirmation; treating disagreement itself as proof of the critic's moral/intellectual
defect; making a promised test impossible to fail; expressly excluding relevant
contrary evidence; using unrestricted unseen compensations or intentions so that
no severity or pattern of counterevidence can matter. Explain the protective move,
not just the absence of a stated test. Merely not naming a falsifier, expressing
faith, making a moral/metaphysical claim, or providing weak evidence is NOT enough.
Philosophy, morality, and history can be answerable to reasons without one decisive
experiment. Set unclear when you cannot establish how the claim is protected.

EMOTION: Separately record observable comfort, fear, shame, identity, or ridicule,
or none/unclear. This does not establish manipulative intent or audience response.
Emotional force can coexist with a sound argument.

Count a distinct claim-use episode once within one uninterrupted stretch of its
argument. Immediate repetitions are one episode. Renewed use after an intervening
reply can be a new episode. Do not count merely quoting the opponent, a rhetorical
question that is answered, an expressly hypothetical scenario, or a bare statement
of personal belief as an unsupported public proof unless the actual context does so.
