## When doubt itself becomes confirmation

In his debate with Matt Dillahunty, Sye Ten Bruggencate is asked whether any
experience could change his position. His answer is no. He says that “doubt
presupposes the existence of God reason presupposes God,” then says changing
his position is impossible. [Source passage, about 1:29:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OL8LREmbDi0&t=5389s).

The key point is not that he speaks confidently. He makes the act of questioning
the claim into confirmation of the claim. A critic cannot even raise a doubt
without supposedly relying on what is being doubted. That move would need a
reason showing why reasoning requires this particular religious foundation and
why alternatives cannot work. Repeating the dependence does not establish it.
In this answer, the protection from criticism is explicit.

This passage is a clear illustration, not proof that every theist uses the same
move. Several repeated uses by one speaker also remain several uses by one
speaker—not several independent examples of what all believers do.

## The same test applies to a non-theist

In his debate with John Lennox, Christopher Hitchens provisionally grants that
a creator could exist. He then asks how a person could know that creator's
wishes. That is a legitimate further question: a claim about a cause does not
automatically establish the cause's moral instructions.

He goes further, however, saying that people who claim such knowledge are
already discredited and should leave the argument to others. His closing line
in this passage is that those claiming certainty must be distrusted “a priori”—
in advance. [Source passage, about 18:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5OXPlUCGScY&t=1096s).

The missing step is the reason that every claimed route to this knowledge must
fail. Asking Lennox to establish one is fair. Treating someone as disqualified
for claiming to have one risks closing the inquiry before that person's reasons
are assessed. The study therefore applies its criticism-resistant slogan rule
to this non-theist passage too. The surrounding concession matters: Hitchens
is discussing knowledge of God's will, not rejecting all certainty on every
possible subject.

Lennox later challenges that advance exclusion. He argues that a claim to know
God need not be arrogant if revelation has occurred, then says the issue must
stand or fall on the evidence for that claim. That does not establish that his
evidence succeeds. It does show why the evidence should be examined rather
than dismissed merely because someone claims knowledge.

## Lennox: a missing step is not always an escape from criticism

In the same debate, Lennox quotes Christianity as the sole foundation of Western
liberties and adds, “to this day we have no other option.” He follows this with
claims about Christianity's contribution to universities, liberties, and law.
[Source passage, about 35:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5OXPlUCGScY&t=2122s).

Those are reasons about historical influence. Even if they were all accepted,
they would not by themselves show that no other present basis for liberty is
possible. Something can help a practice begin without remaining its only
possible support. The categorical line carries that extra claim without the
needed reason. The direct review counts it as an unsupported slogan.

It does **not** count it as a confirmed criticism-resistant slogan. An overstated
claim may still be open to correction. The study requires an additional,
observable move that blocks criticism before assigning the stricter label.
This reading also does not independently verify Lennox's historical claims or
the origin of the quotation he uses; the subject here is the step in his argument.

Another Lennox passage illustrates the importance of caution. Asked about the
universe's eventual destruction, he answers that God's plans extend beyond the
present universe. That may seem to rescue a design claim from any disappointing
outcome. Yet the surrounding response invokes a specific religious expectation,
and he soon makes revelation depend on evidence. [Source passage, about 55:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5OXPlUCGScY&t=3320s).

The review leaves this possible protection **uncertain**, rather than treating
it as a confirmed unfalsifiable slogan. Readers may dispute the proposed
evidence or ask what would count against the promised future. Neither
disagreement, by itself, licenses changing an uncertain case into a confirmed one.
