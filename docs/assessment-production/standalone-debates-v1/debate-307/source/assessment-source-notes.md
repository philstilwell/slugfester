## Burdens and qualifications

1. Preserve the stated question byte-for-byte. Both speakers accept textual variation. Ehrman argues that surviving copies sometimes change important wording and that recovering an early common ancestor does not guarantee every original word. White defends reliable preservation/recoverability through the collective manuscript tradition and continuity of the apostolic message. Do not turn White into an advocate of perfect individual copies or Ehrman into an advocate that nothing historical can be known. Do not silently substitute “Is Christianity false?” or “Is every part of the New Testament unreliable?”

2. Distinguish three claims: a local textual difference, a significant interpretive difference, and loss of the overall message. Agreement on the first does not settle the other two. White calls variants important and worthy of study while disputing their effect on the message. Ehrman expressly concedes that the vast majority of variants are insignificant or untranslatable spelling differences, around 18:59–20:13. Do not use the raw variant count as though he argued that all variants substantially alter meaning.

3. Ehrman’s opening presents lost originals, delayed surviving witnesses and copying errors; early circulation examples are illustrative reconstructions. White counters that a larger manuscript population produces a larger variant count and that early geographically dispersed witnesses help reconstruction. Treat dates, quantities and manuscript relationships as claims supported or challenged in this debate, not facts independently established by this assessment.

4. White explicitly compares representative PRINTED editions, not individual manuscripts, at 43:19 and in rebuttal. His roughly 95% claim uses all words; percentages assigning manuscripts to families use variant units. Ehrman separately challenges extrapolation from printed editions to actual manuscript word agreement. Preserve both denominator and evidence-type distinctions. White’s admission he has not collated a Byzantine manuscript is not itself a refutation of a reproducible printed-edition calculation. Ehrman’s “200 within 300 years” is a misstatement immediately corrected to White’s 120 and acknowledged; do not score the mistaken 200 as White’s claim.

5. The captions corrupt “four-tenths” at about 40:30 as “410” and contain numerous personal-name, Greek-word, manuscript-date and reference errors. The supporting audio check clarifies four-tenths; no rating or quotation may depend on the recognition error. White’s dozen manuscripts within a century is challenged on dating; he cites Wallace, Comfort and Skeat and gives examples but does not supply a full list in that exchange. Do not invent the list or independently resolve the dating from outside scholarship.

6. White’s tenacity claim is that the original reading survives among the alternatives in the collective tradition, not that any single manuscript or modern edition is infallible. Multiple streams and intersecting transmission are offered against a simple linear telephone-game model. His P75/Vaticanus argument distinguishes independent copies from a direct parent–child relationship. Not being direct copies does not by itself establish separate access to the autograph. Ehrman’s later hypothetical bottleneck accepts subsequent multiple lines while questioning what happened before the branching.

7. Both appeal to scholarly authorities. Their lists of living scholars, consensus claims and reported book passages are claims presented here. Do not resolve the debate by credentials or import external consensus. Ehrman acknowledges he does not know Barbara Aland’s view when asked. White cites scholarship and his own study, and characterizes some contrary trends as postmodern; assess any relevant inference rather than the label alone.

8. At approximately 1:49:52 onward, White tests whether P52 variation changes the entire meaning. Ehrman distinguishes a significant difference from a complete meaning reversal and discusses reconstruction in a lacuna. Greek phonetics and missing-word reconstructions are recognition-sensitive: do not make an exact Greek reading load-bearing without verified text. Ehrman does NOT say surviving originals are logically impossible. He distinguishes possibility from probability and offers the conjectural Enoch reading of 1 Peter 3:19 as an example, not a demonstrated lost original.

9. Ehrman’s response to percentages uses the example of one negation changing a hundred-word passage. This concerns semantic impact, not a claim that every small variation has such an effect. In the Hebrews “apart from God/by the grace of God” exchange, distinguish whether a specific passage explicitly teaches abandonment from whether that theme is consistent with the rest of Hebrews. The two standards must not be collapsed.

10. Ehrman accepts the same uncertainty about original wording for classical literature, while still using surviving witnesses for historical work. He does not accept that uncertainty about some words destroys all historical knowledge. White’s classical comparison concerns relative attestation and consistency of standards. His broader skeptical-consequence inference must be evaluated against Ehrman’s actual limited uncertainty, not against an invented total skepticism.

11. At about 1:59:23, Ehrman agrees his hypothetical edited Greek text would differ less from a standard critical text than the Textus Receptus does. He later declines an unexamined translation comparison and maintains that significance is not measured by word percentage. Do not treat the limited Greek-edition concession as surrender of all his claims about interpretive differences.

12. For Mark 1:41, White challenges the single free-writing witness and suggests contextual harmonization as an explanation for anger. Ehrman grounds his preference in internal reasoning about a harder reading being softened, not in a general endorsement of Bezae’s reliability. Around 2:03:16 he explicitly describes the effect as a fuller picture or different nuance, not an entirely different book. This qualification governs broader summaries of the example.

At about 2:04:43–2:05:47, Ehrman’s Philippians third-copy scenario is a hypothetical countermodel: later agreement can identify a common ancestor without proving that ancestor exactly reproduces the autograph. It is not documented knowledge that all Philippians manuscripts actually descend from an erroneous third copy. His closing explicitly accepts multiple subsequent lines. White appeals to early dispersed transmission and asks what positive evidence establishes primitive corruption. Distinguish a counterexample to a guarantee from proof of actual corruption.

White argues that inspiration need not entail perfect individual copies and that apostolic use of variant translations rebuts that requirement. Neither speaker’s religious identity substitutes for this argument.
