## Adopted burdens and qualifications

Barker calls himself an atheistic agnostic and defines atheism as absence of theism. He challenges warrant for belief rather than adopting a duty to disprove every conceivable god. His positive assertions about science, history, religion, human nature and afterlife nevertheless require support. He sometimes asserts no evidence, no God or a myth/lie categorically; preserve actual local strength and later qualifications rather than smoothing all claims into agnosticism. His later suffering reply distinguishes a benevolent Christian God from an indifferent or cruel deity. Do not score that as a disproof of every possible god.

D’Souza distinguishes belief from direct knowledge, then argues for a creator from the universe’s beginning and fine-tuning, and specifically for Christianity from resurrection testimony. He claims these are reasonable evidence-based explanations, while conceding limited knowledge of the designer. A generic designer does not by itself establish Christian attributes. Preserve both the positive explanatory burden and his narrower concession that the designer’s own origin may be a separate investigation.

On cosmology, Barker quotes Hawking’s changed position and conditional singularity result, distinguishes mathematical imaginary time from imagination, and presents multiverse ideas as hypotheses while also making categorical claims about a pre-existing quantum state and eternal cosmos. His later qualifications are part of the record, not permission to erase earlier overclaims. D’Souza challenges empirical support and Barker’s changing certainty.
